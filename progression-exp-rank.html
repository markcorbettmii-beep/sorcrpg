Progression, Experience and Rank Systems

Note to Reader: At the end of this article, you'll find various linked docs to help support this text, including Combat, Dice, Movement, and more.

Each player character is primarily defined by three key components:

  • Class Level (Level - reflects experience and growth)
  • Rank (measured in Rank Points - RP, determined by Traits, Armaments, and Fellowships)

Class Level

A character's Class Level, referred to as "level (lvl)", reflects their overall experience (XP) and growth. Class Level provides access to additional combat abilities, talents, skills and traits (tst) and works in many other ways, including proficiencies and/or prerequisites.

XP Acquisition & Distribution

Characters earn XP from combat, exploration, roleplaying, crafting, feats, and achievements. XP is typically shared among party members, but individual tasks grant XP directly to the responsible character.

Milestone vs. Incremental XP

Campaigns may use milestone leveling, where major story events trigger immediate level increases, independent of XP totals.

Rank and Rank Points (RP)

RP measure a character's achievements and heroic exploits separate from XP.

  • RP are earned through exploration, overcoming challenges, and performing exceptional actions, such as achievements.
  • Armaments and Companions also determine a character's rank.
  • RP accumulates over time and determines the character's Rank.
*Fellowships are creatures that accompany characters in several ways. Each fellowship has its own SirC card, just as Characters and every other creature in the game. Refer to the section on "Companions" for more.

Determining Rank

As RP increases, the character advances to higher Ranks. Conversely, if gear is removed or diminished resulting in RP loss, the character's Rank will also decrease.

Creatures and NPCs also have rank. Group challenges (hordes, parties, swarms, etc.) have a total combined rank, which is each individual's score divided by the number of participants in the group.

Whether comparing individual or group ranks, you'll want the numbers close to one another. Ranks too high prove too challenging; ranks too low grant very little or no experience - both waste the campaign's time. The latter often happens when players farm for materials or quest items, but players must be careful not to poach or cross lines protected by nature wardens and/or higher powers of nature.

Rewarding Experience

When rewarding experience points for combat, it's important to consider the challenge posed by the encounter. Characters earn standard XP for defeating monsters of similar rank, but encounters that are either extremely easy or impossible grant no experience ("null").

If the challenge is barely possible - such as a Pauper facing a Heroic, Elite, or Legendary monster - a significant XP boost is awarded to reflect the difficulty and rarity of such a victory. Conversely, high-ranked characters like Legends defeating trivial foes such as Common monsters receive no experience because the encounter is too easy.

  • Standard XP: Challenging but possible encounters (rank difference of one or two).
  • 5x Base XP: Barely possible encounters (rank difference of three, but still achievable).
  • Half Base XP: Easy encounters (rank difference of minus one or minus two).
  • Null (0 XP): Impossible or trivial scenarios (rank difference of four or more, or minus three and below).

Experience System

This experience system is designed for long-term progression, allowing characters to advance through challenging, rewarding levels and - after reaching the pinnacle of standard advancement - continue to grow through Exemplary (EXY) tiers. It emphasizes meaningful growth in prestige and attribute development.

Rank can be advanced via EXY and other stats such as prestige (retire as a Legend), lifeblood, attribute development, etc. - but no talents, skills, or traits beyond Level 30. Abilities may still be learned up to the class tree allotted amount (59), but Abilities may not be strengthened once EXY levels begin (e.g., Smite (5) may not be changed to Smite (6)).

Standard Levels (1–30)

  • Progression: Characters begin at Level 1 and advance through Level 30.
  • XP Requirements: Each level requires progressively more XP, increasing at a rate higher than classic D&D but still achievable.
  • Purpose: Levels offer opportunities to gain new combat abilities and purchase talents, skills, and traits.
  • Capstone: Level 30 is the highest standard level, requiring over 76 million XP. Reaching this represents exceptional achievement.

Sample Level Progression

  • Level 1: 0 XP (to next: 300 XP)
  • Level 10: 22,075 XP (to next: 11,250 XP)
  • Level 20: 1,285,825 XP (to next: 640,000 XP)
  • Level 30: 76,075,825 XP (to next: EXY 0.01)

EXY System (Exemplary Levels)

After Level 30, progression continues with the EXY system:

  • EXY Levels: Begin at EXY 0.00, incrementing by hundredths (e.g., EXY 0.01, EXY 0.02, etc.).
  • XP Requirements: Each hundredth EXY level requires 80,000,000 XP.

EXY System Features

FeatureDetails
Combat AbilitiesNo new combat abilities are granted during EXY advancement.
Talents, Skills, TraitsCharacters may learn additional talents, skills, and traits representing personal refinement, mastery, or unique capabilities.
Vitality ImprovementEach EXY level increases vitality, making characters more resilient, healthy, or enduring.
Prestige AdvancementPrestige improves, making it easier for characters to gain recognition, influence, and potentially retire as a Legend.
Legendary RetirementHigh Prestige allows characters to resign their active role and be honored as a Legend.

Optional Experience Doubling Rule

Characters may choose to double all experience rewards for faster progression, but the entire party must agree. This comes with the following penalties:

PenaltyEffect
Rank PenaltyRank advancement is halved, or ranks are gained at half the usual rate.
Prestige PenaltyPrestige gained per level is reduced by 50%, making Legendary status harder to achieve.
Legendary Retirement DelayTo retire as a Legend, characters must reach one extra EXY level (e.g., EXY 0.10 instead of EXY 0.00).

RP is not granted for routine combat or trivial tasks. It is reserved for exceptional actions or milestones.

3. Rank Advancement Frequency

Rank RangePace
Early Ranks (Pauper → Adventurer)1–2 ranks per 3–5 sessions (quick, to keep early play rewarding)
Mid Ranks (Hero → Elite)1 rank every 8–12 sessions (slows as achievements become rarer)
High Ranks (Master → Legend)1 rank every 15–20+ sessions (very rare, reserved for campaign milestones)

General Guideline: A character should not advance more than one Rank per major story arc (or every 10 sessions on average), with higher Ranks requiring more RP and more impressive feats.

4. Maximum Rank Gain Per Session

RuleDetail
Max RP per session10–20 (early game); 5–10 (late game)
Max Rank per session1 - never allow more than one Rank advancement in a single session
Cooldown RuleAfter advancing a Rank, a character must wait at least 2–3 sessions before becoming eligible for the next Rank, regardless of RP earned

5. Rank Loss

  • If a character loses gear, companions, or fails major challenges, RP may decrease and potentially drop their Rank.
  • Limit: Only one Rank can be lost per session due to RP loss.

Summary

This system starts with challenging but achievable level progression (Levels 1–30), then offers ongoing advancement through the EXY system. While EXY levels do not enhance combat prowess, they allow continued growth in non-combat areas - talents, skills, vitality, and prestige - supporting both longevity and legendary status in the game world.

Refer to GM Guide and Monster Compendium for more on Host Ranks.
Additional titles and thresholds can be added or adjusted based on campaign needs.
