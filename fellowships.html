What Are Fellowships?

Fellowships are creatures — and sometimes humanoids — within the game world that your Character can buy, tame, or befriend (through loyalty) to have them by your side until separated by unfortunate events including theft and death.

Fellowship Types

  • Pets — Wildlife companions. Small pets may accompany your Hero; others are kept on your property where they guard and work.
  • Draft Animals — Workers, mounts, and carriers.
  • Ruminants — Resource animals.
  • Angelics — Angelic companions (note: some may not be mounted).
  • Guardians — Protective companions. Bonuses for Barbarian Beast Masters, Rangers, and those trained in Scout and Hunter talent trees.
  • Temps — Summons and Hirelings. Warlocks and similar classes summon these; duration is default but can be extended with class abilities and lucky rolls at the GM's discretion.
  • Humanoid Hirelings — Squires, bodyguards, cartographers, henchmen, etc. Require special schools or Vocational Tree talents to acquire.

Fellowship Slots

Characters have three slots for active Fellowships:

  • Behemoth — For large fellowships such as dragons. Only Goliath characters may mount behemoths.
  • Standard — Behemoths and standard-sized fellowships.
  • Small — Small character races can mount all three sizes.

By default you may choose a combination of 3 Fellowships for your journeys, with only one of each category at a time — unless an ability or talent allows more. Additional Fellowships may be kept at your home or stables.

Race & Class Bonuses

  • Ling races — Receive a Ling NPC companion and bonus stats.
  • Gnomes — Bonus stats for burrowing animal pets.
  • Blood Draco — Bonuses for behemoth dragons.
  • Paladins & Rangers — Bonuses for squire hirelings.
  • Mountaineers & Hunters — Can calm, capture, and tame wild beasts.
  • Barbarian Beast Masters — Hireling, pet, and pack mule; Scout/Hunter talents may allow an additional simple pet.
  • Warlocks — Summon Fellowships.

Every class and race has Fellowships tied to it. Every class or race has Fellowships tied to it.

Loyalty & Care

Once established, you (or your family, trainers, etc.) begin earning loyalty with your Fellowships, and they earn experience in their primary uses. Fellowships must be fed well and treated right to sustain loyalty — neglected companions may leave. Fellowships may not be leveled or have their base stats altered, but your Character's stats will increase, particularly through PAIRED fellowships that grant bonus stats to specific paired classes and/or races.

SORC Fellowship Cards

SORC Cards are the official, fixed base cards used to represent Fellowships. Cards are developed as modules are released — each module comes with a standard set of base punch-out Fellowship Cards, and if you're lucky, very rare cards respective to the module's level.

Fellowship Cards must be acquired in-game. Box sets include basic punch-out Fellowship Cards with a chance at rare Prize Cards per box set purchased. Prize Cards require the completion of a Prize Quest before those Fellowships can be obtained, and prerequisites still apply. If your character cannot use a card immediately, they may sell, trade, or gift it to another party member — though keeping it is encouraged, as it offers opportunities to learn abilities from different classes.

Fellowships are stubborn. They won't respond to just anyone, and they won't perform if you don't keep them happy — so feed them well and give them plenty of love.

Fellowship Example: Stormstrider

Standing ten feet tall at the shoulder and up to twenty-five feet from beak to tail, the Stormstrider is larger than any typical ostrich. Its feathers shimmer with iridescent hues that shift colors depending on the angle of light. Long, powerful legs allow bursts of up to fifty miles per hour. Its long neck is adorned with a crest of vibrant feathers that fans out when threatened or excited.

The Stormstrider has keen eyesight, able to spot movement from great distances — making it a reliable mount for scouts and travelers. Its large, sharp beak can be used for defense, and it has a surprisingly gentle disposition suitable for riders of all ages. Uniquely, it can sense incoming storms, subtly changing behavior before a storm approaches so riders can prepare. Riders train Stormstriders to respond to unique whistles and signals, and they form strong bonds with their mounts. In some cultures, the Stormstrider is revered as a symbol of freedom and speed.

Fellowship Example: Furnoid

The Furnoid is a tiny, agile creature resembling a cross between a small cat and a fox — sleek and lithe, covered in soft silvery-gray or orange-red fur with russet hints around its ears and tail. Its fox-like snout features bright amber eyes that shimmer with wit. Pointed, highly expressive ears flick at the slightest sound while its bushy white-tipped tail aids balance and communication. Known for playful and inquisitive nature, Furnoids are clever and quick — excellent companions and elusive explorers in woodland habitats.

More Fellowships — including Dragons, Beasts, and others — are detailed in the Book of Behemoths and Beasts & Bugs compendiums.
