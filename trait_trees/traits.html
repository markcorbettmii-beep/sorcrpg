Traits

Introduction to Traits

In the world of Essentia, traits (SurvT) are essential attributes that Characters utilize to navigate challenges, resist damage, and assert dominance in combat. There are twelve traits, each representing a specific aspect of a character’s abilities. These traits can be used both defensively to resist ongoing damage or effects, and offensively to enhance combat effectiveness.

Alignment with Talents and Combat Abilities

Traits are closely aligned with talents and combat abilities that are associated with the same attribute. For example, traits linked to Physique will influence talents related to physical strength and agility, while traits associated with Intelligence will impact mental skills and resistance. This alignment ensures that a character’s proficiency in talents and combat maneuvers is influenced by their traits and the corresponding attributes, creating a cohesive system where core qualities directly impact specialized skills.

Definition

Each trait embodies a particular aspect of a character’s capabilities—both in resisting harm and in gaining offensive advantages. These traits reflect core qualities that influence both defensive resilience and offensive potential, often working in tandem with talents and abilities aligned with the same attribute.

Mechanics

When a character is subjected to damage, effects, or combat actions, they may choose to use a relevant trait. They roll a die (such as a d20), add relevant bonuses from their attributes and development points, and compare the total to a set DC or threshold.

Outcome:
Success: The character resists the effect or enhances their attack.
Failure: The effect or damage proceeds normally, or the offensive benefit is not gained.

Trait Trees

  1. Agility Tree
    Description: Represents quickness, balance, and speed. It helps evade ranged attacks and speed-based effects, and enhances offensive precision with ranged attacks.
    Application: Resistance to martial ranged attacks, balance disruptions, and speed-based effects. Bonus to martial ranged attack rolls, damage, and critical hits.
    ATT: Physique
  2. Strength
    Description: Embodies brute force, physical power, and intimidation. It allows characters to grapple, break through defenses, and intimidate opponents.
    Application: Bonus to melee attack rolls with two-handed and main-hand weapons, damage, and grapple checks.
    Offensive: Increased damage output and control in melee combat; can be used to intimidate or push opponents.
    ATT: Physique
  3. Courage
    Description: Represents mental bravery and resolve, helping resist fear, intimidation, and mental control.
    Application: Bonus to resist fear effects, intimidation attempts, and mental domination.
    Defensive: Reduce effects of fear and intimidation.
    ATT: Fortitude
  4. Constitution
    Description: Indicates physical resilience, providing resistance to poison, disease, venom, bleeding or other ongoing effects after initial damage.
    Application: Bonus to rolls against poisons, diseases, and ongoing sicknesses.
    Defensive: Resist ongoing sickness or poisoning effects; can also aid in recovery.
    ATT: Fortitude
  5. Vigilance
    Description: Reflects awareness, perception, and alertness, allowing characters to detect surprises and environmental hazards.
    Application: Bonus to perception checks, awareness, and avoiding environmental dangers.
    Defensive: Better detection of hazards, traps, and ambushes.
    ATT: Perception
  6. Intuition
    Description: Represents a character’s instinctive sense for danger, hidden truths, or upcoming events. It allows characters to make quick judgments, anticipate enemy moves, and sense deception or traps before they are fully revealed.
    Application: Bonus to perception checks for sensing danger, detecting lies, or noticing subtle clues that indicate hidden threats or deception, helping avoid ambushes and traps.
    ATT: Perception
  7. Dexterity
    Description: Represents quick reactions, agility, and deception. It helps in avoiding traps, dodging attacks, and performing stealth.
    Application: Bonus to dodge, craftsmanship, trap avoidance, and deception checks. Quick strikes, bonuses to offhand martial attacks and close counter evasive maneuvers.
    ATT: Artistry
  8. Deceit
    Description: Focuses on deception, mimicking death, and trickery to mislead opponents.
    Application: Bonus to deception checks, feints, and mimicking death.
    Offensive: Use deception to create openings or manipulate enemies.
    ATT: Artistry
  9. Resistance
    Description: Represents magical resilience, reducing spell damage and magical effects.
    Application: Bonus to resist spell damage, magical effects, and mitigation checks.
    Defensive: Reduce damage and effects from spells and enchantments.
    ATT: Resilience
  10. Toughness
    Description: Physical durability and toughness, enabling characters to withstand penetrating and physical attacks.
    Application: Bonus to absorbing initial damage, before ongoing effects, resist penetrating attacks, and withstand physical assaults.
    Defensive: Increased durability against physical harm.
    ATT: Resilience
  11. Willpower
    Description: Mental strength to resist mind control, temptation, and confusion.
    Application: Bonus to resist mental influence, temptation, and confusion.
    Defensive: Maintain mental clarity and resistance to mind effects.
    ATT: Intelligence
  12. Wit
    Description: Mental resilience, sharp perception, and the ability to avoid deception or complex traps.
    Application: Bonus to perception checks, trap detection, and mental defenses.
    Offensive: Use wit to deceive or manipulate opponents.
    ATT: Intelligence
  13. Luck
    Description: Represents chance and fortune, allowing for narrative or probabilistic mitigation and advantages.
    Application: Bonus to luck-based rolls, narrative advantages, or random effects.
    Both offensive and defensive: Use luck to turn the tide of encounters in unpredictable ways.
    ATT: none

Notes to the GM

Using Attribute Scores

GMs are provided with detailed guidelines within campaign modules but may also use Attribute Scores at their discretion—for example, when adventures go off-script, or when applying homebrew rules or unique story elements.

More on Luck

Luck (LCK) is advanced just like other Attributes. At character creation (Level 0), players assign an initial Luck score. During each level-up, players can invest additional points until reaching the cap of 60. Once an Attribute reaches 60, no further points can be allocated, although you may still track exemplary sublevels (e.g., 60[0.001], 60[0.002]) for record-keeping, but these do not affect the Attribute score itself.

Luck and Outcome Probability

Luck increases the chance of specific outcomes proportionally to the Luck score. For example, a Luck score of 3 adds +3% to the base probability of an event, such as loot drops or crafting success.

Example:
If the base drop chance of a rare item is 0.05%, a Luck score of 3 (LCK + 3) increases this to 0.05% + 3% = 3.05%.
Alternatively, multiply the base chance by (1 + Luck score). For a 0.05% drop rate:
0.0005 × (1 + 3) = 0.002 = 0.2%
(which is a 4x increase).

Note: The maximum Luck score is limited to the character’s level (excluding exemplary levels). For example, at level 60, Luck can be up to 60, making the maximum increase quite significant but still within logical bounds.

Luck Cap & Application

  • The final probability of any outcome, after applying all modifiers (including Luck), cannot exceed 100%.
  • When multiple effects (spells, items, events) modify probabilities, combine all multipliers first, then cap at 100%.
  • Luck does not influence outcomes with a base probability of 0%, unless the GM explicitly decides otherwise.

Exemplary Levels

Beyond level 60 (e.g., 60[0.001]), the game tracks incremental levels in thousandths, but these do not increase the Attribute score directly.

GM Discretion

Some outcomes or story events may be designated as unaffected by Luck, at the GM’s discretion. For more details, see the SorC Probability Loot, Crafting, and Discovery System.

Additional Considerations

Limitations

Usage of traits can be limited per encounter, per day, or under specific conditions to balance gameplay.

Synergy & Development

Traits can be combined or specialized over time, allowing characters to customize their abilities and tactical options.

Inheritance & Acquisition

Traits may be inherited through race or acquired via class, profession, or experience to foster growth and specialization.

Examples in Play

Below are narrative examples demonstrating how each trait can be used both defensively and offensively within a typical game scenario. These examples illustrate how traits influence combat and skill actions, highlighting their dual roles in resisting harm and gaining tactical advantages. They are designed to help players understand how to incorporate traits into their gameplay, showing how both defensive resilience and offensive prowess can be achieved through the same trait in different situations.

Agility

Defensive (Agility Resistance): While sneaking through a forest, the party is ambushed by archers on a nearby ridge. The rogue swiftly ducks behind a tree, dodging a hail of arrows. She uses her Agility trait to resist the ranged attack damage and avoid being hit, rolling her agility die plus her Physique attribute bonus.

Offensive (Agility Bonus): Later, she spots an opening and takes a quick shot with her bow. Her agility allows her to line up a precise shot, gaining a bonus to her attack roll and landing a critical hit on an enemy archer.

Strength

Defensive (Strength Resistance): The party encounters a massive stone door sealed tight with a heavy lock. The fighter uses his raw strength to brace himself against a collapsing beam, resisting its weight and preventing injury to nearby allies. He rolls his Strength trait to resist the crushing force.

Offensive (Strength Bonus): He then wields his warhammer to smash the lock, using his Strength trait to increase his melee attack damage and break open the door, forcing entry.

Courage

Defensive (Courage Resistance): The party faces a terrifying beast that roars and charges. The warrior steels himself, resisting the beast’s terrifying roar and the fear effect it causes, using his Courage trait to avoid panic and maintain his composure.

Offensive (Courage Bonus): Seizing his resolve, he shouts a battle cry, intimidating the beast and gaining an attack bonus, causing the creature to hesitate or cower.

Constitution

Defensive (Constitution Resistance): A character drinks from a tainted water source, risking poisoning. She uses her Constitution trait to resist the poison, rolling her Constitution die plus her Fortitude attribute bonus to negate ongoing sickness and avoid damage.

Offensive (Constitution Bonus): Later, she drinks a healing potion, using her Constitution to more quickly recover stamina and health, gaining a bonus to her healing check.

Vigilance

Defensive (Vigilance Resistance): While traveling through a jungle, she hears rustling in the bushes. Her vigilance allows her to detect a hidden trap before it triggers, avoiding injury or ambush by rolling perception checks plus her Perception attribute bonus.

Offensive (Vigilance Bonus): She spots a weak point in the enemy’s formation, gaining an advantage on her next attack or surprise round.

Dexterity

Defensive (Dexterity Resistance): The party attempts to sneak past a guarded camp at night. The rogue uses her Dexterity to avoid detection, dodging patrols and traps, rolling her Dexterity die plus her Artistry bonus.

Offensive (Dexterity Bonus): She then swiftly strikes a guard with a dagger, using her Dexterity to perform a quick, precise attack that catches the enemy off guard.

Deceit

Defensive (Deceit Resistance): The party needs to infiltrate an enemy camp. She uses her Deceit trait to convincingly pretend to be a lost traveler, avoiding suspicion and detection, rolling deception checks plus her Artistry bonus.

Offensive (Deceit Bonus): Later, she tricks a guard into revealing information by feigning innocence, gaining an advantage in the interrogation or extraction.

Resistance

Defensive (Resistance): An enemy mage casts a damaging spell on the party. A member uses her Resistance trait to roll against the magical attack, reducing the damage or negating effects of the spell.

Offensive (Resistance Bonus): She then counters with a spell of her own, using her Resistance trait to boost her chances of disrupting the enemy’s magic.

Toughness

Defensive (Toughness Resistance): The party is ambushed by bandits wielding piercing weapons. The heavily armored fighter resists the penetrating attacks, soaking damage and resisting the physical harm.

Offensive (Toughness Bonus): He then pushes into melee, using his toughness to break through enemy lines and deal extra damage or create openings.

Willpower

Defensive (Willpower Resistance): A character faces a corrupting influence or mind control. She uses her Willpower trait to resist the mental influence, rolling her die plus her Intelligence attribute bonus to avoid being controlled or confused.

Offensive (Willpower Bonus): Later, she focuses her mind to strike back, breaking free of the influence with a mental attack that gains an attack bonus.

Wit

Defensive (Wit Resistance): The party encounters a complex puzzle or a deceptive trap. He uses his Wit to see through the deception, avoiding the trap or misinformation, rolling perception checks plus his Intelligence bonus.

Offensive (Wit Bonus): He then uses his wit to deceive an enemy, bluffing or manipulating them into a disadvantageous position.

Luck

Defensive (Luck Resistance): The party faces a series of unlikely hazards in a dungeon. Through a stroke of luck, she avoids a collapsing debris, rolling her luck die plus her Pending bonus to negate the damage.

Offensive (Luck Bonus): Later, her luck turns the tide—she lands a critical hit or causes an enemy’s weapon to malfunction, turning a dire situation into an advantage.

  1. Physique (how strong and agile your character is)
  2. Fortitude (how tough and resilient your character is to ongoing damage from poison, bleeding, or spells that deal channeling or damage over time)
  3. Artistry (how skilled and dexterous your character is)
  4. Intelligence (how smart and quick to learn your character is)
  5. Wisdom (your character’s experience and memory capacities)
  6. Perception (how aware and observant your character is)
  7. Resilience (mental and physical initial damage)

Note: Fortitude reduces the duration and impact of ongoing negative effects—such as bleeding, poisons, curses, hexes, and other persistent afflictions—after the initial damage has occurred. In contrast, resilience helps mitigate or prevent initial damage; if it fails to do so completely, it can still prevent that damage from being fatal.

Each of the seven assigned numbers now represents the attributes’ scores AND the number of points that can be spent on talents, skills or traits aligned to the attribute, or KEPT as the attribute's score.

TST costs vary, and are cheap in the early branches of its tree (talents, skills or traits), but the cost inflates as you advance in said trees. Some TST cost 1 point, others 2, and others 10. It's up to the player to decide where they want their characters to excel. Do you want trait buffs? Do you want to craft enchanting gear to level your way to the top? Do you want to expand your chances of dice rolls and wear special equipment through your adventures, or somewhere in between all of it?

Your score is the remainder of the number you've invested into TST.

So at level 1, if you had 6 points in the attribute physique, you could spend all six, in any order of TST, but you’d now have 0 points in that attribute. You'd need to restock points there to spend them at the next level of progression, level 2. However, it's suggested to keep points in your attributes of interest because between development their score works as a prerequisite, and also works as an adjustment to rolls.

Attribute Summary

Attributes represent your character’s innate strengths and weaknesses. There are seven attributes, each starting at a value set by your character’s race (either zero or a negative number). During character creation, you roll dice to determine your starting scores for each attribute.

Your attribute score serves two purposes:

  • It represents your character’s current ability in that area.
  • It is also the pool of points you can spend on related Talents, Skills, or Traits (TST).

Spending attribute points: As you buy TSTs during character creation or level-up, you spend points from your attribute score. Your attribute score decreases as you spend points. Keep in mind, some abilities, equipment, or skills require a minimum score in their related attribute, so balance your spending accordingly.

Rolling for attributes: You roll for attributes at character creation and again at every class level-up. At each level-up, you re-roll your attributes in the same way as character creation, assigning your new results to your seven attributes and replacing your previous scores (and spendable points).

Unlocking skills and Traits (TST)

To unlock or learn the vocational tree, a TST (a skill or trait), spend 2 points from your points pool. But, you can only unlock it if your Attribute that it’s based on has at least 2 points. Once unlocked, you can spend more points to improve the skill, but it can’t be higher than your Attribute score.
