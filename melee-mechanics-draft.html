How to read this page.

Melee is close work. Everything here resolves inside the Melee Range Chart's three bands, Touch, Reach and Throw. There is no velocity curve in melee: a blade at the end of its reach cuts as hard as one at the start, and only Throw carries a curve of its own.

Three families. Earthborn melee runs on Strength, except Light Blades, which run on Dexterity. TAW melee runs on Agility. Energy melee is its own category and its governing Trait is still being decided. Unarmed and Improvised Tools sit alongside them, both on Strength, neither carrying a weapon Rank.

Armaments and Armor. The second half of this page is what a melee strike is read against: the Cards, a weapon's Grade, Quality and Condition, the damage a Weapon Type does against a given Armor Mould or creature, and the Armor Grades that answer it.

Rule Sets. Sections marked Complex are not in play at a Bare Bones table. Blue is in play at every Rule Set, gold is Complex only, and a purple border marks anything a Modular table may keep or drop.

Melee Attacks

Earthborn Melee

Earthborn melee weapons, blades, blunt weapons, polearms, and other forged or crafted arms, follow standard melee rules using Strength SCR for both hit and damage. Strength is the governing Trait for all Earthborn melee with one exception, the Light Blade.

Light Blade. A dagger, knife, dirk, stiletto, or any blade smaller than a short sword is a Light Blade. Light Blades use Dexterity SCR in place of Strength SCR for both hit and damage. A short sword and anything larger stays on Strength. An Improvised Tool is not a weapon and is never a Light Blade; it stays on Strength.

  • Hit: d100 + weapon rank + Strength SCR vs PROTS
  • Damage: weapon rank dice + Strength SCR + modifiers
  • Reach: 1 square (5 ft.) default unless the card specifies otherwise; Reach weapons extend to 2 squares (10 ft.)

Graze, Critical Hit, Botch, and all other standard melee rules apply as normal.

Technologically Advanced Weapons Melee

TAW melee weapons, shock batons, pulse blades, nano-edge swords, follow standard melee rules with one exception: Agility SCR replaces Strength SCR for both hit and damage, reflecting the precision and speed required over raw physical power.

Energy Melee

An Energy melee weapon is a real blade or haft with elemental substance held around its edge, an energy blade or a plasma edge, drawn from the same six sources as Energy Ranged. It is not a powered device and nothing about it is manufactured; the weapon is ordinary and the energy around it is what strikes. Energy melee is its own category alongside Earthborn and TAW. Like Energy Ranged, they bypass certain armor types that resist ballistic damage and are disrupted by EMP interference and magical effects. They carry no planetary Penalty and Slope Mod does not touch them (see Energy Weapons, pg. 4, and Slope Conditions).

Note: The governing Trait for Energy Melee hit and damage is still being decided and will be confirmed in a later pass. Until then, Graze, Critical Hit, Botch, reach, and all other standard melee rules apply as normal.

Unarmed Combat

Unarmed attacks use no weapon rank. Strength replaces the rank hit bonus.

Hit: d100 + Strength vs PROTS

Damage (main): 1d4 + Strength

Damage (off): 1d2 + Strength

Graze and Critical Hit rules apply as normal. Grapple rules apply as normal.

Anything using hands is Main Hand and Off Hand still. There are no bonuses for hands unless the Class, Path or Branch itself or the Ability, such as those from Monk or Brawler, define it.

Elbows, knees and the like have no Main or Off Hand, and they are treated as Improvised Tools unless the Class, Path or Branch itself or the Ability, such as those from Monk or Brawler, define it.

Certain class paths, such as Fighter, Brawler, prefer their bare hands, grappling, and improvising with objects from their environment as weapons. Characters with innate unarmed abilities may have class-specific bonuses that override or stack with these base values.

Improvised Tools

Any attack using an item in a way it was not designed for, or using an ability the Character has not learned from a Main Class Tier, Path or Branch, is an Improvised Tool. The full rule, its subcategories and its damage sit with Unarmed, see Improvised Tools.

Grapple and Grab

On a successful grapple or grab attack, the target is held until they break free or the attacker releases. To escape, the target rolls d100 + Strength vs the attacker's PROTS (Encounter DIFS). One escape attempt is allowed per turn. Drop damage applies if released or escaped at height. Specific grab rules, carry height limits, and drop damage values are module specific.

Complex Rule Set only.

Limb-Specific Targeting (LST)

LST allows a character to aim at a specific body part, head, groin, limbs, or torso, for increased damage at the risk of full exposure.

Complex Rule Set only.

Hit Penalty: Determined by the attacker's Weapon Rank and the area targeted (see LST Chart).

On Hit: Deal bonus damage equal to twice the hit penalty value.

On Miss: The attacker loses all defenses through their next turn and rolls 1d10 for stumble distance (feet). The stumble leaves them open to a counter attack from their target and attacks of opportunity from every enemy adjacent to any square they stumble into, including the square they occupied when the LST was attempted.

Example: A 1d10 result of 1 while surrounded on all 4 sides, the attacker stumbles 1 foot but must defend all 4 attacks of opportunity from the surrounding enemies.

There is no Graze or Critical Hit on an LST attack. Refer to the GM Codex for more on LST.

Common rank weapon attack values are calibrated so that a baseline attacker (no character modifiers) hits each armor grade at the rates listed in the Base Hit Rate table in the Combat section. All weapon types share the same base hit rates, advantages and disadvantages between weapon types come from armor Vulnerabilities and Resistances, not from the base rate. A blunt weapon benefits from chain's vulnerability to blunt; a piercing weapon benefits from leather's vulnerability to pierce. These interactions are listed in the Armor Grades table.

Type and Ability Gating: A weapon's Type gates which Class Abilities may be readied with it, and that gate is the main work the four Types do. A Martial Ability requires a Tactical weapon, and a Spell Ability requires a Magic weapon. TAW and Energy sit outside this rule entirely: neither answers the Martial or the Spell requirement, and neither takes a Blessing. A TAW piece may be Modified but never Enchanted, there being no magic in it to enchant.

Alignment Requirement: TAW and Energy Armaments and Equipment require an Ambivalent alignment to carry and use. Neither family answers the Martial or the Spell gate on Class Abilities, neither takes a Blessing, and a wielder of either draws no Higher Power Benefit toward Class development (see Higher Power, pg. 2). The refusal belongs to the Higher Power rather than to the one carrying the weapon: a Power sanctions nothing drawn from outside its own universal elements, and both of these are drawn from outside them. Ambivalence is what lets a Character carry them without standing in that contradiction.

Cultural Bonuses: TAW and Energy are Cultural Armaments like any other, and the rule is the same as for every Culture: a Character native to one gets its bonuses when using its Armaments, the six Tawdry Dwarf-lit worlds with TAW and Ignis with Energy. Both are rare outside their culture's reach and common within it (see Cultural Armaments, pg. 2).

Devotion: Each keeps its own practice in place of a Higher Power. The races native to Ignis have only Adoria to pray to, the inner sun being the one power near enough to answer. The worlds out near Tawdry Dwarf do not pray to the Goddess at all; they meditate, and give respect to everything that still provides sustainable life in their part of the universe, which with Tawdry dying is growing thin.

Weapons use the same eleven Grades and the same four Types (Tactical, Magic, TAW, Energy) as armor, and draw from the same Materials lists above, Steel and Iron for Medium, Osmium and Tungsten for Dense, Mithril for Half Light, and so on. This covers two separate things, and they are not the same:

Recipe Manufacturing: A weapon's full crafting recipe lists every component that goes into building it, head, haft, grip, guard, fletching, whatever the weapon has. Each component can be crafted from its own material, at its own Grade, and the recipe's full material list is what determines crafting cost, weight, and what a Blacksmith or other crafter actually needs on hand to build it.

The Damage-Dealing Part: Separate from the recipe, a weapon's Grade for damage, DMG Mods, and Weapon Prerequisite purposes is set ONLY by whatever material makes up the part that actually strikes the target, a hammer's head, a sword's blade, a spear's tip, an arrow's point. Every other component in the recipe (Grip, Haft, Hilt, fletching) can be a different, usually lesser Grade without changing the weapon's Grade, DMG Mods row, or Weapon Prerequisite standing at all, those parts exist in the recipe, but they don't count toward what the weapon IS.

Example: A war hammer's recipe calls for a Dense Grade (Osmium) head and a Half Medium Grade (reinforced leather-wrapped wood) haft. The recipe needs both materials to build it and prices/weighs accordingly, but for damage, DMG Mods, and Weapon/Hand Proficiency purposes, this is a Dense Grade weapon, full stop, the haft never enters that math.

Tactical and Magic Weapons

Tactical is the oldest of the four and the only one every people on every world can make. It is ordinary craft, forged, sewn, carved or knapped, and it asks nothing of a sun. Primal peoples hold the longest unbroken line of it, bone, stone and hide worked by hand, changed less in an age than any other tradition has changed in a generation. Humans built the modular pattern that most of Essentia now copies, panels and mounts reconfigured for the job instead of committed at the forge, which is why a Human kit turns up on worlds Humans never settled. Orcus smiths work grave-iron and salvage into tunnel-fighting arms, hooded against a sun their skin cannot take and left open at the ear so Stonesense is never blinded. Dwarves hold the mountain-hall standard for Steel and Iron, Ogres the two-handed weight nobody else can carry well, and Elves the bow. Tactical is full strength on Adoria-lit and Tawdry Dwarf-lit worlds alike and falls off only out past the thirteen, where ordinary steel finally meets conditions ordinary craft was never asked about.

Magic answers to Adoria. Her light carries sorcery as well as warmth, so an Enchanted weapon and a caster's implement both do their best work on the eight inner worlds, Ignis through Zailister, and both weaken the further out they are carried. Elves keep the deepest tradition of it, nature magic and the Enchanting of ordinary arms, and Halflings the luckiest, small charms worked into everyday things. Zailister sits at the balance point, the one world equally at home with a Stave and a rifle. On the six Tawdry Dwarf-lit worlds magic still works and is simply weaker, never absent, and past the thirteen it is weaker still. A caster who travels outward learns to carry steel.

Weapons Carried

Characters may have up to 3 weapons readied at any time, drawn (Main Hand / Off Hand) or sheathed for quick access. Only readied weapons can activate weapon-specific abilities, so weapon sets should be readied that adhere to a character's Ability choices matching main class, path and later branch. Additional weapons may be packed, stored in a backpack, on certain Companion types, such as draft/pack animals, or in a wagon used for transport, sale, or trade. Packed weapons cannot be drawn or used in combat. All carried weapons (readied + packed) count toward the Character's total carry weight; exceeding 50% of capacity triggers Burdened/Encumbered penalties (see Encumbrance, pg. 5). Swapping the Armament Set: Characters set their full Armament Set at the start of each long rest (8 hours). Mid-adventure, characters may drop or pick up weapons from their packed inventory, but cannot ready them for combat use until the next long rest.

Stave vs. Staff

These are two separate weapon families, not two spellings of the same thing. A Staff is Tactical, part of the same weapon family as the Quarterstaff, Bō, Jō, Long Staff, and Short Staff (see Weapon Cards, above), a plain length of wood or metal, no Type restriction beyond Tactical. A Stave is the dedicated caster implement, Magic by its own nature the same way an orb or wand is (see Weapon Type vs. Weapon Style, Appendix), never Tactical, and the one that can carry a Crown (see Special Weapons, Armaments, pg. 4). Wielding a Staff never satisfies a Stave requirement, and wielding a Stave never satisfies a Staff requirement, even though both are, at a glance, a long stick.

Armor

Protection Score (PROTS)

Condition: Every piece of armor carries a Condition, rolled on d100 when it is found or acquired, and Condition adjusts Armor Score as a percentage of the piece's Grade rather than as flat points, so wear costs the same share of protection at every Grade.

ConditionRoll d100Armor Score EffectMovement
Excellent81-100+5% × GradeNo penalty
Fair (Damaged)11-80-30% × Grade-3 sq.
Broken1-10-50% × Grade (Armor Score halved)Movement halved. No enhancement bonuses.

Armor Score Formula: A piece of armor starts at its Grade's Base Armor Score and is adjusted by its Quality, its Condition and any Enhancement. Those four, Grade, Quality, Condition and Enhanced, are the whole of it:

Armor Scr. = Grade − (Quality % × Grade) − (Condition % × Grade) + Enhanced

Quality and Condition are percentages of the Grade, not flat points, so a worn set loses the same share of its protection at every Grade. Enhancement is added last, in flat points. Quality and Condition are percentages of the Grade, not flat points, so wear and workmanship cost the same share of protection at every Grade. Enhancement is added last, in flat points. Worked example, the Skeleton Soldier: Half Heavy Plate at Grade 40, Poor quality at −5%, Damaged condition at −30%, no Enhancement. 40 − 2 − 12 = Armor Score 26. Quality also adjusts the piece's value by the same percentage (see Armor Quality, Appendix).

PROTS Formula: PROTS = Base Armor Scr. + Defense or Resistance Trait (depending on attack source) + shield Armor Scr. + all other modifiers (buffs, debuffs, environmental effects). Against martial or Nature-sourced attacks, use Defense Trait. Against Spell-sourced attacks, use Resistance Trait. These two never stack on the same attack, exactly one applies based on the attack's source.

Every entity in SORC has a Base Armor Score (Armor Scr.), a number between 1 and 100 representing the raw protection of any armored or unarmored entity. Armor Scr. is the starting point, not the full picture. PROTS (Protection Score) is Armor Scr. plus every permanent Enhancement or Impairment, temporary buff or debuff, and Trait bonus or penalty applied to it. PROTS = Armor Scr. + all modifiers, positive and negative. Armor Scr. never changes unless the armor itself changes. PROTS fluctuates with buffs, debuffs, Enhancements, Impairments, and situational modifiers.

Example: Ice Dragon, on the ground, has a base Armor Scr. of 80 (Armor Scr. 80) and has 12 bonuses to Protection, so its PROTS 92. Rolling 1D100 would need a result of 92 to Hit the dragon and roll for damage. In the air, the Ice Dragon gets a +10 bonus, now giving it +22 (12 + 10) bonus to its base Armor Scr. 80. Flying, the Ice Dragon has a PROTS of 102.

Conversely, a Commoner is wearing cloth and has a base Armor Scr. of 10, Armor Scr. 10, but has part of a wooden gate he's used as a shield giving him +2, however he doesn't have a strong foothold because of the environment, -1 (strong winds). His total PROTS is Armor Scr. 10 + 2 - 1 = 11. The Commoner is easy to Hit. In comparison, a Paladin in Osmium Plate has a base Armor Scr. of 60. If he's holding Bracers, a 2h Weapon or a Shield he can add his Defense score (Defense is a Trait) representing; Parry or Block respectively he'll add his Protection, in this case 4. He also gets the base Armor Scr. of a Shield (Armor Scr. 10) or the lighter option, Bracers (Armor Scr. 5). Let's say he's holding the shield and has a score of 5 on the Trait Defense. Base Armor Scr. is 60, plus 10 for shield, plus 5 because he has Defense (5).

Defense Roll

Defense Trait is passively included in PROTS and does not require a roll. If the attacker's score meets or exceeds the target's total PROTS (which includes the Defense Trait, Base Armor Scr., shield Armor Scr., and all other modifiers), the attack lands. Defense type is determined by equipped gear:

  • Parry, 2H Weapon, Bracers of Defense, or Dual Wield (melee only, excludes orbs, tomes, special items, and environmental objects)
  • Block, Shield
  • Dodge, Light Armor

Characters without the Defense Trait or qualifying gear cannot use Defense. See Defense under Traits (pg. 2).

Defense Example, Omnè Crystal Lock Difficulty Score Check

Setup: Kael (Elf, Thief, Shadowblade, LVL 11, Rank Uncommon) attempts to pick an ancient crystal lock guarding a vault on Omnè's floating platform. The lock has a PROTS of 45 (representing its structural integrity). Kael has the Disarm Trap talent with a score of 8 and Dexterity Trait of 6.

Attempt: Kael rolls to disable: d100 + 8 (talent) + 6 (Dexterity) = [62] + 14 = 76 versus DIFS of 45. Success! He carefully manipulates the crystalline mechanism, feeling the subtle shifts in energy. The lock clicks open, revealing the chamber beyond. No damage taken.

Comparison, If He Failed: If his roll had been [28] + 14 = 42 (below 45), the trap would trigger. Standard trap damage on failure might be 16 Life damage. But if Kael had chosen to destroy the lock instead (attacking its PROTS directly), failure would deal 2x that damage (32 Life) back to him. The Disarm Trap approach was clearly safer.

Defense Example, Zailister Spaceship Security Code Difficulty Score

Setup: Tyx (Goliath, Hacker, DataMind, LVL 13, Rank Rare) tries to bypass a security panel on a Zailister orbital station. The system's defensive DIFS is 58 (representing firewall strength). Tyx has the Hack talent with score 12, Intellect Trait of 10, and a +5 bonus from their specialized hacking equipment.

Attempt: Tyx rolls: d100 + 12 (Hack talent) + 10 (Intellect) + 5 (equipment) = [71] + 27 = 98 versus DIFS of 58. Critical success! The panel sparks briefly as Tyx gains access, bypassing multiple layers of encryption in seconds. The security override code unlocks adjacent doors. Tyx's Hack talent makes this look easy.

Armor Grades

Armor Taxonomy. Grade, Mould, Material and Type are four separate things, and an armor is only fully described once all four are named. The Armor Mould (often just Mould) is the specific build a piece of armor is worked into, Plate, Leather, Chain, Dragon Scale, Osmium Plate. The Material is what it is made from, Hide for Leather and Fur Moulds, Steel and Iron, Osmium and Tungsten, Mithril, Dragon Scale and so on, and the material is what sets the Grade. The same Mould can sit at different Grades depending on its Material, a Steel Plate is Heavy Grade while an Osmium Plate is Dense Grade. Names in the rules read Grade + Material + Mould, so a "Composite Dragon Scale Plate" is Composite Grade, Dragon Scale Material, Plate Mould. Type is a fourth and separate axis entirely, Martial, Magic, TAW, and Energy.

Armor Grades & Materials

Armor is categorized into six main areas: condition, quality, rank (all mentioned above), grades, materials, and Armor Moulds. These factors collectively determine its default weight, armor score, and overall characteristics.

The armor grade defines the overall strength and durability of the armor. It indicates how well-protected the wearer is and influences its weight and mobility. The materials used to craft armor influence its strength, weight, flexibility, and magical properties. Ranging from mythical metals to natural fibers, each material offers unique advantages suited for different warriors, environments, and combat styles.

Material Compatibility: Each armor grade is associated with specific materials suitable for its design, strength, and purpose. Materials are restricted to particular armor grades, and cannot be used interchangeably across all grades. This ensures consistency in armor crafting and maintains the distinctiveness of each grade's characteristics. Mithril is the clearest example: it is Half Light Grade, and Half Light in turn is defined by Mithril, Mould and Grade are one and the same for it, it does not appear at any other Grade.

Armor splits the same two ways weapons do, and they are not the same thing:

Recipe Manufacturing: A full armor set's crafting recipe lists every component that goes into it, the Cuirass, pauldrons, greaves, boots, gloves, plus secondary materials like leather straps, mesh lining, padding, and buckles. Each component can be crafted from its own material, at its own Grade, and the recipe's full material list is what determines crafting cost, weight, and what a Blacksmith or other crafter actually needs on hand to build it.

The Main Piece: Separate from the recipe, the armor's Grade, Armor Score (Armor Scr.), and Armor Grade Threshold standing are set ONLY by the material of its Cuirass, the main structural piece covering the torso. Every other component in the recipe (straps, lining, padding, buckles) can be a different, usually lesser Grade without changing the armor's Grade, Armor Scr., or Threshold at all, those parts exist in the recipe, but they don't count toward what the armor IS.

Example: A cuirass's recipe calls for Heavy Grade (Steel) plate over leather straps and cloth lining. The recipe needs all three to build and price it, but for Armor Scr., Armor Grade Threshold, and Set purposes, this is a Heavy Grade armor, full stop, the straps and lining never enter that math.

The table below covers Tactical Armor Type specifically, ordinary craft rather than magic, technology, or elemental substance. See Magic Armor, TABA, and Energy Armor below for the Materials and Armor Moulds unique to those three Types.

The Armor Mould describes its style and pattern, including design elements like plate, beaded, stitched, scale, or studded configurations. These structural choices affect both the appearance and functional properties of the armor.

Characters' movement is affected by their armor grade and condition. Movement penalties and bonuses are measured in squares (sq.), each square equals 5 ft. Base character speed is measured in feet (ft.); divide by 5 to get squares for tile movement. Each armor grade carries a specific movement penalty in sq. (see Armor Grades table in the Combat section). Additional penalties from armor condition and bonuses from buffs, gear, or abilities stack accordingly.

For example, a Character with a base speed of 30 ft. (6 sq.) wearing full plate armor (-5 sq.) moves 1 sq. (5 ft.) per turn. If the armor is broken, an additional -1 sq. is added for a total of -6 sq. Conversely, movement bonuses (e.g., +2 sq.) are added separately and can offset or negate penalties if specified.

All penalties and bonuses stack unless explicitly stated otherwise. Maximum Armor Score (Armor Scr.) for each armor type is set by its Common base Armor Scr. plus any rank-tier bonuses, see the Common Base Armor Scr. table and rank bonus list above. Enhancements such as magic bonuses (+2 to Armor Scr.) do not affect movement penalties, as they are considered magical effects, not weight or bulk.

While characters can still move with penalties or bonuses, if their armor or gear is broken or heavily encumbered, they may be able to move but cannot fight effectively or at all, depending on severity. Heavy encumbrance that exceeds the Character's capacity results in total incapacitation, meaning they cannot move or fight at all until their load is reduced. Additionally, damage to gear, such as broken weapons or armor, can severely impair combat. For example, a broken armament prevents a character from fighting, regardless of their movement ability.

Cloth armor's default Armor Scr. is 10, but it can be enhanced to improve its Armor Scr. without incurring movement penalties. Cloth is often used as under armor or for concealment, and such items (faulds, gambesons) may also be enhanced within their respective caps.
