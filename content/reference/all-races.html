Bast

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Bast treat most encounters like a game they've already decided to win. Quick tongues and a knack for always landing on top, figuratively and otherwise, have earned them a reputation as charming liars and worse cardsharps. They're easy to underestimate and that's exactly how they prefer it; a Bast caught in a straight fight has usually already lost, but a Bast given an opening rarely needs a second one.

Bast accounts open on a temple-city rather than a homeland, and on the office their forebears held in it, which was to be let in everywhere and accounted for nowhere. That standing was granted, never earned, and the Bast have carried the manner of it long past the city, which is why a Bast enters a room as though already invited and is so rarely questioned on it.

Lore has it that Bast are blessed with nine lives, and there's something to the legend, while most don't dare attempt Rejuvenation before Growth 30, a Bast can try as early as Growth 21, nine LVLs ahead of everyone else.

That same luck runs out eventually, a Bast who's burned through one too many close calls finds the next gamble doesn't go their way nearly as often, and their thin frame rarely holds up to a straight fight once the trickery runs dry.

Brownling

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Charming smallfolk born of Halfling blood and the Brownie Fae race of the wilderness, Brownlings carry slightly pointed ears and an earthy, luminescent complexion that bestows their dual heritage. With deep cultural roots and a gift for artistic expression, they excel in Fae and nature magic, weaving gentle enchantments with ease. Brownlings are also elusive and keen characters making for good rogue and thief roles.

Brownling accounts turn on a bargain rather than a founding. Their forebears are held to have given their work to households freely and unasked, on the single understanding that the work never be repaid, and to have left without argument the moment payment was offered. Every Brownling still carries the shape of that arrangement, generous without limit and unreachable the instant a debt is named.

Of Fae lineage through their Brownie Fae parentage, tracing back to Tenue, through the Veilwood, into Zailister, same as Elves (see Fae, above).

Although adventurous, Brownlings are naturally reclusive and like being home, and with a touch of magic, tending a woodland or upkeeping a household comes naturally to them. Masters of craftsmanship, upkeep and stealth, little escapes their notice or their hands.

That homebound streak runs deep, a Brownling pulled too far or too long from their household or woodland grows visibly anxious, and their craftsmanship suffers the longer they're away from a proper hearth.

Bugbears are tall, broad-shouldered predators covered in coarse, bristled fur, built for both raw strength and the patience of a hunter. Despite their size, they move with surprising quiet through dense brush and rocky terrain, relying on ambush as often as brute force to bring down prey twice their size.

The Bugbear name was not chosen, it was given, first spoken by other peoples as the thing waiting past the edge of a settlement to frighten children into staying inside it. Bugbears know precisely what they were made into and have never petitioned to be called anything else, holding that a name others are afraid to say aloud does most of the work of a border.

For all their size, Bugbears move without a sound through forest and stone, slipping past brush and scree that would announce any other predator's approach long before it ever got close.

All that patience for the hunt leaves little for anything else, a Bugbear forced to sit still without a target in sight grows restless fast, and conversations rarely hold their attention long enough to finish.

Caelen (Corpus Caeleste, Standard Size)

The Omnè Astral invaded Corpus Caeleste. The Caelen fought hard, but their technology was not as advanced, and they had no magic. They were taken by the millions and experimented on. The experimented Caelen became too strong to control, fought back, sought revenge and got a great deal of it, and took their captors' vessels home to reunite with their families, who are just normal Caelen. This card is the experimented line. The Caelen who were never taken remain as they always were.

Caelen accounts begin at the invasion and refuse to begin anywhere earlier. What came before was recorded and is now held to be another people's property, taken along with everything else, and a Caelen asked about it will tell you the question is the theft continuing. What they keep instead is a count of the taken, maintained exactly, and it has never stopped being added to.

The Caelen were remade piece by piece, until almost nothing of the original remained. It was never one decision. A hand, then a lung, then an eye, and somewhere along that line the person stopped being the thing they started as. They do not consider this a loss and have little patience for anyone who does. Corpus Caeleste sits past Tawdry Dwarf where the old star gives too little to live on unaided, and the Caelen answer to that was to stop needing what it could no longer give. See Tawdry Dwarf & Beyond for more.

What is left of them runs precisely. A Caelen does not tire the way flesh tires, does not misjudge a distance, and does not forget a figure they have been given.

What went out with the flesh went out for good. Adoria's light finds nothing in a Caelen to work on, and a mending spell laid on one closes half of what it would close on anyone else.

Centyr

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Centyr, derived from the word "Center," a mythical race combining human-like features with various types of horns and matching animal lower bodies. Centyr beings possess a striking and unique appearance.

At their core, Centyrs have human facial features, retaining the familiar structure of a human face and a human torso, arms and hands. However, it is their horns, ears, lower body, including the rump and hind legs, hooves and tails that truly distinguish them.

These horns come in diverse shapes and sizes, reflecting the wide range of animal influences within the Centyr race. Whether it be the majestic antlers, ears, tail and hooves reminiscent of a moose or elk, the elegant and spiraling horns reminiscent of a unicorn, the curved and twisted horns reminiscent of various goat or sheep, or the tusks, ears, hooves and tail of a forest hog, each Centyr bears a distinctive set of horns that adds to the allure of their appearance.

In terms of their physical structure, the Centyr maintain a human torso, enabling them to engage in social interactions and activities akin to their human counterparts. However, from the waist down, their bodies transform into the matching lower body, tail and hooves of the animal that corresponds to their unique horns. This fusion of human and animal forms not only grants them remarkable physical abilities and characteristics but also showcases their deep connection to the animal kingdom.

Note: Homebrew Centyr may be developed between GMs and Players, for instance a Satyr would fit the description of a Centyr.

Drevane (Omnè, Small Size)

Place Holder awaiting Image.

The original inhabitants of planet Omne Malum, older than any recorded history on the planet and deeply tied to its ecosystem. When Tawdry Dwarf began its long death and the atmosphere of Omnè shifted into something hostile, the Drevane did not build around it. They adapted to it. Over generations their bodies absorbed the pressure, hardened against the toxins, and evolved to thrive in conditions that drove the Omnè settlers into suits. They are watchers and keepers, they remember what Omnè was before Tawdry started dying and they do not share that knowledge freely. They do not oppose the Omnè's construction projects, but they do not help either.

Drevane accounts predate the words now used to keep them, and the Drevane hold that this is why they cannot be given away: the oldest of them cannot be said in any tongue an outsider has, only shown, and showing requires the ground it happened on. A people whose record cannot travel have never seen the point of an ally who cannot come to it.

A Drevane doesn't need to roll to catch a shift in pressure or a whiff of corrupted air, they simply notice, no test required, where anyone else might roll and miss it entirely.

A Drevane's hands were never made for circuitry or machinery, and what they know of Omnè's past, they keep close, walk one into a city of steel and glass and watch them grow visibly uneasy, trusting the feel of open ground over anything built.

Dwarf (Glimmerstone)

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Glimmerstone Dwarves are renowned as Master miners and treasure hunters, with an exceptional talent for uncovering hidden gems and enchanted materials deep within the earth. Their keen senses and innate connection to the mineral veins allow them to detect valuable deposits that others might overlook. They possess an Aptitude called "Mineral Instincts," which enables them to sense the origins and true potential of the materials around them.

Glimmerstone blood runs part dwarf, part Fae, a lineage struck generations back somewhere in the deepest veins they still mine today. That Fae inheritance shows in short, uncanny bursts of speed, a Glimmerstone Dwarf vanishing from one spot for a heartbeat before reappearing already standing in another.

Additionally, they have a skill called "Greed," which involves marking a target so that as it is defeated, it yields increased chances of Rare treasures and enchanted loot. While they excel at hunting for materials, these Dwarves prefer the thrill of discovery over crafting, focusing their skills on unearthing the most valuable resources. This combination of instincts and the active "Greed" effect gives them a significant edge in exploration and resource gathering, helping them uncover the game's most coveted treasures with ease.

That instinct for treasure cuts both ways, a Glimmerstone Dwarf finds it almost physically difficult to walk past unclaimed valuables, even when doing so is clearly the smarter call. Their greed is not subtle, and it does not stop at outsiders, Glimmerstone Dwarves haggle, needle, and undercut one another over a single gem with the same unattractive hunger they'd show a stranger, kinship counts for little once coin or treasure is on the table. What keeps that hunger from costing them everything is the charm to back it up, Glimmerstone Dwarves are natural schmoozers and superb barterers, talking a fair price down to a steal with a smile the other party doesn't realize they should distrust. It helps that they're cute, round faces and bright eyes that put strangers at ease, and that cuteness is deceptive, underneath it Glimmerstone Dwarves are mean, calculating people who've learned to perform charm rather than feel it, all the better to close a deal.

Glimmerstone Dwarves favor light or delicate armor over their kin's heavier plates, trading protection for the speed and reach their treasure-hunting demands. On their feet they wear only cobblers of their own making, wooden shoes carved rather than stitched, shaped with the same sculpting tools they turn to art and ornament. A well-made pair never wears out, and a Glimmerstone Dwarf takes more pride in their cobblers than in almost anything else they own, save perhaps whatever treasure they haven't found yet.

Sturdy and resilient, Common Dwarves are known for their craftsmanship, endurance, and hearty nature. They thrive in mountain halls and underground cities, with a mastery of forging, stonework, and deep-earth navigation. Their tough physiques and unwavering determination make them formidable allies and resilient foes.

Dwarf accounts do not open on a homeland, they open on a list. The craft they are known for is understood among them as the debt owed for that making, stone given shape in return for having been given shape.

Take a Dwarf out of stone and underground and put them in open water or featureless plains, and that same sharp instinct that reads a cave wall like a book goes quiet, they sink fast and read the wide world poorly, and magic study never quite clicks for them either.

The most versatile of all races, humans are natural communicators, traders, and survivors. Their adaptability has allowed them to establish cultures across every corner of Essentia.

Named Human cultures: Nordian, Eldrim, Tanu’la, Xo’kajal, and Humans raised among the Omnè. These are cultures rather than separate Human races; every Human shares the same base Traits and Stats.

Also in our rules - “The closer you are to the cultural root of an armament, recipe, or profession, the more abundant it is.” A Human can be of Omnè Culture, like a Human raised by the Omnè. That Human can use the Omnè stores and local supply as their source of beginning equipment, including equipment that would be rare on Zailister. Other Characters can wear or use that equipment as well, but they need to find it first through trade, travel, discovery, or someone who brought it away from Omnè.

Cultural Reach: A Human culture is not only a way of speaking or a set of customs. The closer a settlement, market, workshop, or household is to a culture's geographic roots, the more common that culture's food recipes, ingredients, cooking tools, armaments, accessories, and trained NPC profession roles become. Near the source, a Character can find them readily or commission them locally. Farther away, the same things become rare, imported, more expensive, or dependent on a specialist who carried the knowledge with them.

Variants, Permissions & Prohibitions: Armaments carry one of five Variants: Telluric, earthborn craft and the only Variant that can become Magic through Enchanting; TAA, Technologically Advanced Armaments including TAW and TABA; Chthonic, elemental force held around the piece; Oxidize, an Attachment joined to the bearer and drawing on the body; or Malign, Telluric craft with a curse worked into it at the forge. Culture, alignment, a Class Path, or a Higher Power may favor, permit, modify, enhance, or forbid a Variant. A Character may physically possess a forbidden item, but cannot equip it for a tradition's restricted Abilities; Basic ATTKs remain unrestricted.

Omnè and Ignis: Omnè's native armament Variant is TAA. They despise magic and are forbidden to equip Magic-type Telluric Armaments. They do not Enchant Telluric pieces; they use technological modifications on permitted TAA, TABA, TAW, and TAG equipment instead. The Primal of Ignis are the contrary example: their native armament Variant is Chthonic, and their culture despises manufactured technology and forbids TAA, including TAW and TABA. Their bone, stone, hide, and elemental work belongs to the Primal Chthonic tradition, not Ignis's native Telluric Variant. These are cultural prohibitions, not changes to a Character's base Traits or Stats.

Beginning stores: Humans and Zailister-origin Races begin from Zailister's Free Market by default. A Human raised into Omnè Culture may begin from Omnè's Circuit Exchange, using Omnè TAA, TAW, TABA, TAG, synthetic equipment, and local professions as their starting supply. Emberscale and Primal may begin from Ignis's Ashfield Camp, while Tredeki, Imperials, Leshy, and Skrath may begin from Tredeki's Fracture Row. Other Characters may later find or acquire those goods, but do not begin with another world's store unless Race or Background grants it.

Imperial Bloodline Cultures

Bloodline culture changes upbringing, customs, armament practice, class tradition, and the way a community understands its Higher Powers. A Character chooses a Path and its Higher Power separately at Level 4; no Bloodline is locked to a patron, alignment, or class.

Crimson: Inspired by ancient Maya city-states, calendar keepers, astronomers, rain and maize stewards, observatory-temples, dynastic memory, and civic obligations. Crimson communities treat timekeeping and public works as sacred responsibilities.

Onyx: Inspired by ancient Mesopotamian omen readers, night observatories, protective incantation specialists, underworld threshold rites, and ancestor tablets. Citrine: Inspired by ancient Eurasian steppe peoples, especially Xiongnu and early Mongolic traditions: horse clans, felt camps, mounted travel, oath alliances, sky and ancestor reverence, seasonal migration, and long-distance trade. Violet: Inspired by ancient Greek and Hellenistic cultures: rhetoric, theater, geometry, civic debate, philosophical schools, mystery traditions, and competitive excellence. Violet communities treat public speech and creative achievement as forms of power.

Azure: Inspired by ancient Egyptian scribal and temple cultures: cosmic order, records, surveying, medicine, river management, astronomy, and the duty to preserve truth across generations. Azure communities value measured judgment and precise memory.

Verdant: Inspired by ancient Persian cultures: garden cities, qanat waterworks, royal roads, healing traditions, sacred groves, stewardship, and the moral duty to make barren ground live. Verdant communities understand power as something maintained through reciprocity and care.

Troll (Bridge)
Cultural Armaments

Bridge Troll armor for choke point, not field: heavy on front and shoulders, almost nothing behind. Built by people who fight where bridge width means nothing can flank them. Salvaged town steel, taken in tolls and fights. Telluric, weighted to front because crossing approached from one side.

Lifespan and Age

Lifespan: ~180 years (Mortal)

Starting Age: 45 | Retirement Age: 85 (see Time, pg. 4)

Bridge Trolls dwell near cities and towns, making homes beneath bridges they use as shelter and choke point. Water below serves as steady food and resource source. They're known for cornering unwary travelers with riddles and tolls as much as threats.

Bridge Trolls keep counting in oldest form: exchange, one asking and one answering until answering fails. Their accounts hold forebears took crossings because a crossing is where question must be answered before passage given. Toll taken in coin rather than answer is debased form of old right.

Years spent in cramped dark beneath bridges and culverts left Bridge Trolls perfectly at home where most can't even stand up straight. There isn't riddle, puzzle, or word-trap they haven't already heard version of.

But that same time in dark under bridge leaves Bridge Troll badly suited to open sunlight and wide horizons. Too much open space leaves them edgy and exposed in way no riddle can talk them out of.

Troll (Mountain)
Cultural Armaments

Mountain Troll work cold-country first and martial second: thick hide layered over already thick hide, weighted for footing on ice rather than duel. What metal there is sits low, where fall does damage. Telluric, hide over hide, metal kept where cold cannot reach skin.

Lifespan and Age

Lifespan: ~250 years (Mortal)

Mountain Trolls are cold-hardened climbers who make homes at elevations where little else survives. Their thick hides shrug off frost and falling rock alike. They guard peaks fiercely, treating intrusion as threat to be driven off or buried in snow.

Mountain Trolls hold that forebears went up rather than be counted with rest. Their line recited at height, in open, where reciting is hardest. What lowland halls settle by asking and answering, Mountain halls hold was always meant settled by who was still standing at top.

Climbers swap stories about Mountain Trolls scaling sheer cliffs and crossing ice and scree without slowing or losing footing—terrain stopping most climbers cold.

All that time alone on exposed peaks left Mountain Trolls poor company and worse diplomats. They don't see point in talking problem out when could just be thrown off cliff.

Sand Trolls adapted to harshest deserts, their leathery hides shedding heat that would scorch other races. Senses tuned to smallest shift in dune or wind. They move through open desert with unsettling ease, vanishing before anyone realizes they were being watched.

Sand Troll accounts hold that forebears followed heat out past last standing stone and lost counting somewhere in crossing. What they keep now is reconstructed, argued over, and shorter every generation. Sand Troll tells you loss was price of getting out, paid willingly.

Caravans crossing dunes swear Sand Troll can walk days on single waterskin and disappear into open ground with nothing to hide behind.

Sand Troll body built to shed heat, not hold it. Cold climates sap stamina fast, and hard freeze can leave one sluggish and slow to react.

Village Trolls set aside solitary, territorial instincts common to their kind in favour of guarding single community, often one depending on them as much as fearing them. They take role seriously, treating every villager as something worth protecting.

Village Trolls broke with counting entirely, holding that line recited between two Trolls is worth less than line recited to village that will still be standing to hear again. Their accounts kept by settlements rather than Trolls, which their solitary kin regard as having given inheritance away.

Nothing slips past Village Troll on watch. They notice trouble approaching home long before anyone else.

Village Troll identity wrapped up in place they guard. Pull one away too long and they grow anxious, distracted, noticeably less sharp.

Snow Dwarf

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Snow Dwarves carved their halls into the same icy peaks as their Arctic cousins, but lean harder into raw endurance than magic or craft, content to outlast a storm rather than out-think it.

Snow Dwarf accounts are the shortest their kin keep, and deliberately so. Where other halls lengthened their lines with the works of each name, the Snow halls record only that each name endured its winters and passed the hall on intact. Nothing is claimed beyond outlasting, and among them nothing further needs claiming.

A Snow Dwarf can march or labor through blizzard conditions that would exhaust or injure other races, shrugging off cold and fatigue that should slow them down.

Snow Dwarves lean so hard on raw endurance that magic and fine craft never really took root for them, spellwork and delicate trades come slower and harder to a Snow Dwarf than to nearly any other race.

Sylvan live deeper in the wild than their Common Elf kin, rarely settling anywhere long enough to be called a town, their dwellings are little more than the trees and hollows they pass through on a given season.

Fae Descent

Wood Elf

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Wood Elves keep to the deep canopy and undergrowth, smaller and quieter than their Common Elf kin, content to let a forest hide them rather than relying on open confrontation.

Wood Elf accounts are never spoken above a certain volume, and the oldest of them are not spoken at all but walked, a route through standing timber that carries the whole of what is remembered. Their forebears went smaller and quieter than their kin on purpose, holding that a people the forest will hide does not need a people's other defences.

Like all Elves, of Fae lineage, descended from Tenue, through the Veilwood, into Zailister (see Fae, above).

A Wood Elf moves through dense brush and forest canopy without snagging a branch or breaking a twig, vanishing into foliage that would slow or expose anyone else.

A Wood Elf's whole skillset assumes cover and canopy, caught in open ground or built-up city streets with nowhere to disappear into, they're noticeably less sure-footed and far easier to spot than they're used to.

Yawnie

Place Holder awaiting Image.

Yawnies move and speak slowly by nature, but mistaking that for weakness comes at a cost, their patience runs deep, and little seems to rattle or hurry them once they've settled into stillness.

Yawnie accounts are the longest any people keeps and are almost never heard to the end. Nothing is compressed and nothing is dropped, on the reasoning that a thing hurried through was not understood, and their forebears are held to have decided very early that they would rather arrive late at a true account than early at a serviceable one.

A Yawnie can hold absolute stillness for hours at a stretch, becoming nearly impossible to spot by sight or motion alike as long as they don't choose to move.

A Yawnie simply doesn't have another gear to shift into, no spell, talent, or emergency ever makes them move faster than a walk, and anything that demands a snap decision under pressure tends to catch them flat-footed.

Native Planet

Zailister

Cultural Armaments

Yawnie work is slow-made and made once, heavy, patient and unhurried in every line, by a people who see no reason to rush a thing that has to last. Little rattles a Yawnie, and their armament is built on the same assumption. Telluric, slow-made, and made once. See Cultural Armaments, and refer to the Tomes of Essentia for the full history.
