Talents

Talents are specialized actions acquired from Vocational Trees, used when characters face interactive challenges in the world. Talents are chosen at creation and improved as you level. Each Talent is tied to a Vocational Tree, and using it requires the right tools in your inventory. Successes grow Talent experience; failures can set you back. To use a Talent, roll the relevant dice, add modifiers, and compare to the challenge’s Difficulty Score.

Key Definitions

Player
Players are the participants of SorC games that create a character(s) by choosing its race, class, backstories, mission statements and other various details. The player also narrates and builds the life of their character(s) throughout their life’s journeys, changing the narrative and dynamics of the game's world(s). Players engage with the game's mechanics, collaborate with other players, and interact with the game world depicted to them by the Game Master (GM).
Character
A character is a fictional persona created and controlled by a player within the TTRPG. Each character typically possesses unique traits, abilities, backgrounds, and motivations that influence their actions and interactions within the game world. Characters can vary widely in nature, ranging from heroic figures to complex antiheroes, each representing the player's narrative choices and role within the game. Sometimes the Character is referred to as the Player Character to distinguish between NPC Characters and Player Characters.
Game Master (GM)
The Game Master (abbreviated as GM) is the facilitator of the game’s experience, responsible for guiding the narrative and overseeing the game's rules and mechanics. The GM narrates and sometimes even builds the game world, develops the story, portrays the game’s Hosts and resolves the outcomes of player and host actions. The GM is a referee and ensures the game remains engaging and balanced, providing exploration challenges and opportunities for players to build their characters on.
Host
A Host is any living being, placed into the game’s world by either the game’s development team or the GM. Some are fictional heroes, villains and monsters, while others are creatures and critters often going unnoticed. From gargantuan to microscopic, hosts are the backbone of the game’s world(s) that bring forth the challenges and recognition of each Character’s journeys.
Race
In SorC, Race refers to a specific ethnic or cultural group within a species that is characterized by shared ancestry, physical traits, cultural practices, and societal roles. While species encompasses the broader biological classification of a being, a race captures the distinct identities, traditions, and abilities that differentiate groups within that species. Races often influence a character's appearance, abilities, and interactions with other beings in the game world, providing unique lore and narrative opportunities based on their background and heritage.
Species
A species in SorC refers to a distinct group of living beings within the game world, categorized based on shared biological, cultural, or magical characteristics. Each species may possess unique traits, abilities, and cultural significance, shaping their interactions and roles within the game environment.
Monster Species
A monster is a hostile or adversarial habitant of the game world that creates challenges or threats to Player Characters. Monsters range from traditional fantasy creatures like dragons and ogres to races particularly made for SorC games, such as Boogers (giant froglike species), Yawnies (slothlike humanoids) and Pearling (an exotic race evolved from clans millions of years ago). Their characteristics, abilities, and behaviors are defined by the rules of the game, and their purpose is usually to serve as obstacles for Player Characters to overcome in pursuit of quest objectives.
Monsters consist of various hostile beings that serve as adversaries to players. They embody the antagonistic forces of the game world and are designed to challenge players with their lives.
Creature Species
A creature is a broad term that encompasses any living entity within the game world, including both friendly and neutral beings. This includes critters (bugs and insects), sentient races, animals, magical beings, and even monsters, usually created. Creatures play many roles, including allies, antagonists, both harmful (like mosquitos) and helpful elements (like edible mushrooms), as well as assist Player Characters in background elements within the narrative and game mechanics.
Creatures encompass a wide variety of living beings, often depicted with unique abilities or characteristics not limited to sentience. They can be allies or adversaries and play multiple roles in the ecosystem of the game world.
Mythical Creature Species
Mythical creature species refer to beings of legend and folklore, often possessing magical traits or remarkable powers. These creatures might include beings like unicorns, griffins, or phoenixes, and their existence often carries significant cultural or historical importance.
Beast Species
Any feral Fauna or Monster with a high amount (x or above to be determined) of strength, Toughness and Willpower. Beasts are typically characterized as feral creatures, often possessing heightened physical attributes. They can range from powerful predators to wild, untamed animals and can interact with the game world as threats or companions.
Flora and Fauna Species
Flora refers to the plant life present in the game world, including trees, shrubs, flowers, and any other vegetative elements that contribute to the Environment. Flora can serve various narrative functions, such as providing resources (like healing herbs), creating obstacles (such as dense thickets), or enhancing the setting's atmosphere. Players may interact with flora in numerous ways, including discovery, harvesting, and even communication and/or combat.
Fauna encompasses the animal life within the game world, including any creatures that are not classified as monsters or NPCs. This can range from common wildlife to fantastical creatures unique to the game setting. Fauna can contribute to the ecosystem of the game world, serve as potential allies or threats, and provide opportunities for players to engage with their Environment. Interactions with fauna provide resources to Player Characters in the game by; hunting, taming, farming and through quests or exploration often unfolding mysteries through the study of various species.
Higher Power Species
A higher power species consists of beings that possess extraordinary abilities and heightened intelligence, often exhibiting traits associated with advanced knowledge, spiritual connection, or divine influence. These beings might serve as guides, guardians, or powerful allies in the game world.
Humanoid Species
Humanoid species are those resembling human characteristics in appearance and behavior but may vary in specific attributes such as size, skin color, and additional features like horns, claws, or tails. This includes races such as elves, dwarves, and halflings.
Mythical Beast Species
Mythical beast species are awe-inspiring and formidable beings derived from myths and legends. These may include legendary dragons, chimeras, or other fantastical beings that embody both the ferocity of beasts and the magical qualities of mythical creatures.
Critter Species
Critter species include smaller, often innocuous creatures that populate the game world. These beings can range from insects to tiny mammals and often provide unique resources or lore. Although Critters are small, many small beings such as spiders, centipedes and other insects carry extremely powerful venoms, poisons and/or diseases, and often they travel in swarms or armies.
Elemental Species
Elemental species embody the fundamental forces of nature, such as fire, water, earth, and air. They often possess abilities related to their elemental nature and can shape the environment and gameplay through their powerful elemental connections.
Extraterrestrial Species
Extraterrestrial species refer to beings from beyond the known realms, often featuring unique biological and cultural traits. These species might introduce new mechanics, abilities, and narratives, expanding the game world's diversity.
Reptilian Species
Reptilian species are characterized by scaly skin, cold-blooded physiology, and a variety of adaptations to diverse environments. These beings may include lizardfolk, dragons, and other reptilian creatures, often possessing unique traits and skills.

Additional Definitions

  • Class: A specific role or archetype that dictates a character's abilities, strengths, and combat style.
  • Talent: Specialized abilities or enhancements reflecting training, innate gifts, or experiences.
  • Skills: Practical abilities related to crafting and other noncombat tasks within the game.
  • Abilities: Unique combat mechanics or techniques used in battle.
  • Traits: Inherent survival characteristics granting advantages in difficult situations.

Equipment and Items

  • Gear: Equipment that enhances a character's stats or abilities.
  • Loot: Items dropped by defeated enemies.
  • Buff: Positive effects applied to characters or allies (self-buff, party buff).
  • Debuff: Negative effects applied to enemies or oneself.
  • Consumable: Items used for temporary effects, such as potions or food.

Role Definitions

  • Healer: Specialized in recovery and support.
  • Tank: Designed to absorb damage and control enemies.
  • DPS: Focused on dealing damage.
  • Support: Aids teammates through buffs or resources.

TTRPG Jargon (Additional)

  • Session Zero: The initial session where players and GM discuss expectations, create characters, and set campaign tone.
  • One-shot: A self-contained adventure played in a single session.
  • Campaign: A series of connected adventures forming an ongoing story.
  • Module: A pre-written adventure or scenario, often published for a TTRPG system.
  • Adventure Path: A series of linked modules forming a larger story arc.
  • RAW (Rules As Written): Playing strictly by the published rules.
  • RAI (Rules As Intended): Playing by what the rules are meant to do, even if unclear in text.
  • GM Fiat: Decisions made by the GM that override or interpret the rules.
  • House Rule: A rule added or modified by the playgroup, not in the published rules.
  • Ruling: An on-the-spot decision by the GM for a unique situation.
  • Retcon: Retroactive continuity; changing previously established facts or outcomes.
  • Meta-gaming: When a player uses out-of-game knowledge for in-game advantage.
  • Min-maxing: Optimizing a character for the most mechanical advantage, sometimes at the expense of story.
  • Railroading: When a GM forces the story in a specific direction, limiting player agency.
  • Sandbox: An open-world style game where players can go anywhere and pursue any story threads.
  • Crunch: Rules-heavy mechanics and gameplay.
  • Fluff: Lore, story, or narrative elements as opposed to mechanics.
  • NPC: Non-Player Character, controlled by the GM.
  • PC: Player Character.
  • Skill Check: A roll to determine success at a task.
  • Saving Throw: A roll to resist or avoid an effect or hazard.
  • Initiative: Determines the order of actions in combat.
  • Turn: A player's part of a round in combat or structured play.
  • Round: One cycle through all participants in combat or initiative order.
