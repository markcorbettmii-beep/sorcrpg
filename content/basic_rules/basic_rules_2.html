Prestige

Prestige defines how the character's reputation, associations, affiliations, how they behave, influencing interactions and decisions throughout SorC, and ultimately the character's legacy. Players can choose which reputation “titles” to “showcase” which will align with their character’s persona, making them more relatable and dynamic. However, the GM may also reveal behaviors of the character that the player is unaware of - such as being seen by a witness committing a “dirty deed” - or positive actions as well, such as saving a furnoid (refer to Fellowships) from a tree.

Prestige guides character decisions and actions, reflecting what they prioritize in their life. It shapes the moral compass of the character, impacting relationships with other characters and the world around them and will affect the player character both positively and negatively in terms of bonuses and penalties respectively to certain attributes. Characters' backgrounds may also influence these attributes in a similar manner.

Traverse 13 planets in an odd world with a life-sustaining and magic-emitting main sun, Adoria, and its sister, the dying star Tawdry Dwarf. Each planet with a gate to its own hell domain, found in throne rooms of extravagant labyrinths.

Standing

Standing reflects a character’s conscience and moral compass, based on alignment, religion, and politics.

Talents

Talents are non-combat abilities characters learn through various vocational trees.

Skills

Skills are craftsmanship practices learned from various professions, or even hobbies.

Attributes

Attributes are the innate predispositions characters are born with that allow them to perform certain cognitive and physical tasks outside of combat. Starting with their chosen race and class, players will determine which attributes their characters are strong and weak in, or if they'll play a more balanced role.

Attributes also serve as prerequisites for various elements in the game. The six attributes; physique, fortitude, artistry, intelligence, wisdom and perception determine how strong a character is in a variety of areas, including abilities, talents, skills and traits.

Players have the freedom to allocate points according to their preferences. These points can enhance athletic or cognitive talents, attack and defense abilities such as evasion, traits like strength and agility, allowing for a customized character build.

Movement

The movement system in SorC revolves around character actions, stamina, and terrain, impacting tactical decisions. Each combat turn is 6 seconds, costing stamina points (SP) per second of activity, with specific costs for different movement types and terrain conditions. Characters have a race-based base speed (Goliath: 40 ft, Standard: 30 ft, Small: 25 ft), which serves as a benchmark for movement calculations.

Movement includes walking, sprinting, climbing, swimming, and special maneuvers, each with specific SP costs influenced by terrain, obstacles, and environmental factors. Attribute scores like Strength (STR) and Agility (AGI) affect movement capabilities, such as climbing and sprinting efficiency. Low SP reduces movement speed and increases action costs, with exhaustion occurring at zero SP.

Traits like Vigilance and Evasion help mitigate stamina costs and improve movement performance in hazardous environments.

Advanced mechanics include environmental effects, momentum, and group tactics, all modulating movement efficiency. Travel beyond combat is managed through the Wayfarer Compass device, which allows teleportation to linked locations such as taverns or hearth rooms. Upgrading the Wayfarer enhances its range, speed, and detection abilities, aiding exploration and navigation.

Overall, SorC's movement mechanics emphasize strategic stamina management, attribute utilization, terrain adaptation, and the use of special talents or gear to navigate the world effectively.

Gear and Equipment

Wayfarer Compass

The Wayfarer Compass is a magical device that allows players to teleport to discovered taverns or locations in the SorC world. As characters level up and upgrade it, they can teleport farther, mark locations, and access new areas. It also enhances their ability to spot items, hazards, or quest objects. The device is essential for quick travel and exploration, growing more powerful as players progress and develop their skills.

Items and Currency Cards

Basic Items: Basic Items, such as torches, ladders, diamond dust, apparel and so forth are simply listed on an index card titled Items and currency. Their details are adjusted as they are used throughout SorC, so if you have 100 gd, and spend 10g, you'd adjust the amount to 90g on your items index card.

Potions and Scrolls: Each potion or scroll, whether alchemical or magical, has its own effects on characters.

Fellowship Cards

Fellowships are companions, pets, mounts, summons, hirelings, and humanoid allies that characters can acquire, tame, or befriend. They have their own stats, can level up in their primary roles, and grow loyal over time. Characters typically start with three fellowship slots but can have more through skills and circumstances. These allies support in combat, travel, and exploration, with unique abilities - such as mounts like the Stormstrider, a fast, weather-sensitive creature - adding depth to gameplay and character interactions. Each fellowship card may or may not be restricted to character class and race.

  • Pets (small): From most rare to most common.
  • Argothians, Rodents, Mammals, Marsupials, Critters, Workers, Pack, Draft, Ruminants, Mounts

Guardians: Guardians can be hired by just about any character, but hirelings are not cheap, and expect pay per day. Summons and Tamed guardian require specific classes, such as warlocks and rangers respectively, but other classes can use rare scrolls or other magical methods if they have the ability to read them; however, any Guardian summoned by a scroll or other form of magic aside from a spell, is temporary for a random period of time. Refer to section .00x for more on Fellowships.

Companions (small races only): Companions are humanoid races that are companions to the character.

Wild: Only certain classes, such as Rangers, Beast Masters and so forth can tame wild.

Hirelings: Hirelings are hired bodyguards, bounty hunters, henchmen, or hosts that are seasoned in a particular vocational tree the party may need for their adventure, like a theologian or scout. Hirelings are typically stronger than the party that has hired them and expect daily pay at an agreed upon rate.

Summons: Class specific or requires a scroll or other forms of magic.

Summary of SorC Cards

The SorC card system is a central tool for managing your character’s abilities, equipment, and allies throughout the game. Each card in SorC represents a specific aspect of your character - whether it’s a weapon, piece of armor, special skill, spell, companion, or even resources like potions and currency. Cards are not just static records: they evolve with your character, reflecting upgrades, modifications, buffs, and debuffs as adventures unfold.

How it works:

  • Base Cards: Every item, ability, or ally starts as a core card with original stats and properties.
  • Index Cards: Each base card is paired with a physical index card, where you record changes - such as enhancements, temporary bonuses, or conditions (like curses or wear). This keeps your stats up-to-date as you play.
  • Enhanced Stats Card: This card consolidates a card’s current total stats after all modifications, so you always have an at-a-glance summary of the real capabilities of your gear, skills, or companions.
  • Card Categories: There are various types of cards you’ll use - Profile Cards (identity and background), Stat Cards (attributes and traits), Ability Cards (combat abilities, spells, talents, skills), Equipment Cards (weapons, gear, armor), Fellowship Cards (companions, pets, mounts, hirelings), and Item/Currency Cards.
  • Upgrades and Swaps: When you upgrade equipment or swap out parts (like adding new boots to an armor set), you update the relevant index card to reflect the change in stats. Some sets grant extra bonuses if kept together.
  • Limits: You cannot use cards above your character’s level, and you must meet any attribute or skill requirements to equip or use certain cards. Players may have access to many cards but must choose which ones to bring on each adventure.
  • Dynamic Tracking: All changes, trades, or losses are tracked on the cards and index cards, ensuring the game world remains consistent and evolving. Items and allies persist in the world unless destroyed, and their stats/history may change at the GM’s discretion.

Why use the card system?
Clarity: Cards make it easy to see your current gear, powers, and companions at a glance.
Customization: As you earn upgrades, find new items, or forge alliances, your cards change to reflect your growing capabilities.
Immersion: Cards help bring the world to life, making your character’s journey, relationships, and achievements tangible and trackable.

In summary, the SorC card system is your toolkit for adventure - organizing everything you can do, wield, or befriend, and adapting as your story unfolds. Master your cards, and you’ll master your destiny in the world of SorC!

Vitality Chips

The Vitality System in Slayers of Rings & Crowns tracks four core components - Life, Mana, Stamina, and Endurance - using stackable colored chips, each representing 10 points. Chips are allocated at character creation based on race and size, with capacity (extent) and regeneration speed (Spirit) influencing maximum resource levels and recovery rates. Actions like combat, spellcasting, and crafting consume these chips, affecting performance and survival.

  • Life measures health and damage capacity; damage removes red chips, and regeneration depends on Spirit.
  • Mana fuels magic; casting spells costs blue chips, with optional spell slot mechanics for traditional magic systems.
  • Stamina governs immediate physical actions; depletion restricts actions to basic movement, with regeneration also influenced by Spirit.
  • Endurance affects long-term energy and sustained actions; recovers slowly and is used for prolonged activities.

Players can attune to ancient relics to gain Essence Points, boosting their extent or Spirit, often through challenging quests and faction standing. The system emphasizes strategic resource management, environmental considerations, and optional mechanics like spell slots and rests for magic recovery. Overall, it encourages balancing aggressive tactics with careful conservation to survive and thrive in the universe of Essentia.
