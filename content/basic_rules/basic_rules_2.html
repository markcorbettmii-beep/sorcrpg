SorC Cards

In SorC, players use a comprehensive card-based system to track their abilities, equipment, and companions, with cards representing everything from weapons and armor to pets and fellowship members. Each card has its original base stats, conditions and rarity, and is paired with a detailed index card for noting modifications, buffs, curses, and special traits, allowing characters to customize and adapt their gear and allies over time. SorC emphasizes active exploration and discovery, as players seek out new cards and forge relationships with guardians, mounts, and other creatures. The GM manages the distribution of card sets, item states, and modifications, creating a dynamic and evolving gameplay environment where equipment and alliances can change based on player choices and in-game events.

The Dice

Our game utilizes the d20 dice rolling system and implements the use of the following sided dice: d4, d6, d10, d12 and d20 where the coefficient is how many times the die is rolled, d represents die and six the amount of sides of the die. For instance, 2d6 is a six sided die rolled twice or two six sided dice.

Prestige

Prestige defines how the character's reputation, associations, affiliations, how they behave, influencing interactions and decisions throughout SorC, and ultimately the character's legacy. Players can choose which reputation “titles” to “showcase” which will align with their character’s persona, making them more relatable and dynamic. However, the GM may also reveal behaviors of the character that the player is unaware of - such as being seen by a witness committing a “dirty deed” - or positive actions as well, such as saving a furnoid (refer to Fellowships) from a tree.

Prestige guides character decisions and actions, reflecting what they prioritize in their life. It shapes the moral compass of the character, impacting relationships with other characters and the world around them and will affect the player character both positively and negatively in terms of bonuses and penalties respectively to certain attributes. Characters' backgrounds may also influence these attributes in a similar manner.

Movement

The movement system in SorC revolves around character actions, stamina, and terrain, impacting tactical decisions. Each combat turn is 6 seconds, costing stamina points (SP) per second of activity, with specific costs for different movement types and terrain conditions. Characters have a race-based base speed (Goliath: 40 ft, Standard: 30 ft, Small: 25 ft), which serves as a benchmark for movement calculations.

Movement includes walking, sprinting, climbing, swimming, and special maneuvers, each with specific SP costs influenced by terrain, obstacles, and environmental factors. Attribute scores like Strength (STR) and Agility (AGI) affect movement capabilities, such as climbing and sprinting efficiency. Low SP reduces movement speed and increases action costs, with exhaustion occurring at zero SP. Traits like Vigilance and Evasion help mitigate stamina costs and improve movement performance in hazardous environments.

Advanced mechanics include environmental effects, momentum, and group tactics, all modulating movement efficiency. Travel beyond combat is managed through the Wayfarer Compass device, which allows teleportation to linked locations such as taverns or hearth rooms. Upgrading the Wayfarer enhances its range, speed, and detection abilities, aiding exploration and navigation.

Overall, SorC's movement mechanics emphasize strategic stamina management, attribute utilization, terrain adaptation, and the use of special talents or gear to navigate the world effectively.

Equipment: Armaments, Gear, Items and Materials

defining the differences...
Equipment consists of various types of Objects referred to as Gear, Armaments, Items and Materials characters acquire for aid during adventuring and combat. Adventuring gear consists of Items such as; backpacks, ropes, javelin, flint and steel and torches, and other Items to aid characters when faced with various environmental challenges, such as furniture, flora, and so forth; while, Armaments relate to weapons and armor that allow characters to partake into combat effectively. Currency, Loot, Crafting n Discovery System

To the GM The SorC Probability Loot, Crafting, and Discovery System gives you precise control over what items, materials, or discoveries players can earn after defeating monsters, completing challenges, or exploring the world. Each possible reward has its own drop chance, and you determine what appears by rolling dice and checking simple tables. This is the system used for all non-action outcomes of our modules, including chances of weather, natural disasters, and not only encounters, but the tank of the encounter, hopefully within the party’s challenge rating. You can keep the exact chances and methods hidden from players, revealing only the excitement of the roll and the results. Or, if you prefer transparency, you can share the loot chances and let players see the dice in action. The system is flexible—use it behind the screen or out in the open, whichever fits your table’s style. Currency System Value is determined using four types of coins in ascending order: Silver, Electrum, Gold, and Platinum. The value of these coins can vary by region, but a standard conversion helps maintain consistency throughout the economy. Typically, Silver is used for everyday purchases, Electrum for moderate transactions, Gold for large purchases, and Platinum for exceptionally high-value items. Currency Breakdown Silver Coin Role: The basic unit of currency, used for small purchases, taxes, and everyday expenses. Example Weight Standard:Approximately 180 silver coins equal 1 pound (if weight is tracked). Electrum Coin Conversion: 1 Electrum Coin = 5 Silver Coins. Role: Used for transactions that require a modest increase in value over silver. Gold Coin Conversion: 1 Gold Coin = 50 Silver Coins. Role: Commonly employed for substantial purchases and major economic transactions. Platinum Coin Conversion: 1 Platinum Coin = 100 Silver Coins. Role: Reserved for exceptionally valuable items such as estates, vehicles, spacecraft, or one-of-a-kind relics. Currency Notes For transactions of extraordinarily high value, certified currency notes may be used. These vouchers represent a fixed monetary value, easing large-scale trade across regions. For instance, in some areas a note might be structured to represent a value equivalent to approximately 70 Silver Coins for every 5 Gold Coins indicated, though local variations can apply. Economy of the Planets Economy of the Planets “*Economy of the Planets* Ignis is the first play following Adoria, the mighty sun of Essentia's universe. Of the 13, Ignis is the most rich in minerals and elements, and since it's the closest planet to Adoria's magic emitting radiation, it also harnesses the most magic within its atmosphere and elements. Its inhabitants do not rely on the biological sustenance required on other planets, so trade is not common ground here, although piracy and smuggling is a common practice of all the planets in the universe. Due to an indifference of resource value on Ignis, races here, other than humanoid species, all but ignore the richness of the planet's minerals and gems. The only interests elementals and giant insects have for Ignis' riches is for practical purposes, such as camouflage and shelter. That being said, any trade between Ignis and any other planet would have to be done deep within underground humanoid societies, and there are ways to and from these clandestine trading posts through teleportation and other forms of magic. The second and third planets are much the same, but there is much more biological life on these planets, particularly the third which is very close to Zail, the richest in resources that support biological life among all thirteen planets. Because of this, trade is a common between civilizations of the two planets following Ignis and Zail - whose natives find a particular interest in the magical properties held of the traded minerals. In contrast, the societies of the second and third planets (names disclosed in the full story) are interested in Zail’s advanced technology because, although the fifth through thirteenth planets are more technologically advanced, Zail is the closest planet to them. *Currency* Zail is the universe's reserve currency holder, so the global economic hub of the universe. Rich in life-dependent natural resources, Zail serves as a central hub for currency exchange. However the first planet, nor the last three planets recognize currencies from any other planet, necessitating currency conversion facilities and fostering unique trading opportunities and diplomacy. Note: there is illegal smuggling between all 13 planets and each planet has different “doors” that allow foreigners in and out, including portals, sophisticated trading posts, and there’s a gate to hell among each of the 13 planets, which also offers entry in. Any planet further out from Zail requires a pass to get past its "air space."
