Playable Classes

Reminder: As explained earlier in our rules, Races feature Favored and Averse standing toward specific classes, with one full main class tree and all of its abilities through its respective paths and branches, as well as included various bonuses and penalties to various main classes. See Favored and Averse Class Bonuses under Playable Races, and each race's own entry, for the classes that favor or oppose it.

A character's alignment is determined by their background, not chosen freely. When a background is selected (whether from a module's curated options or a custom background created with the GM), its alignment is visible to the player, not just the GM, since it's going to be shared with the player anyway; the GM confirms the character's alignment based on that background's moral and ethical elements immediately. The background locks in at that same moment; it cannot be switched afterward. The GM confirms the player's alignment sometime during character creation, but must do so before the player chooses their race and class, since both depend on it. Favored and Averse standing applies only after alignment has been assigned, it has no bearing on which alignments are available to a character. Because alignment can shift over time through a character's choices, mission statements, and actions during play (via Fallen or Risen mechanics), a character who later reaches a previously unmet alignment may begin selecting Abilities in that class like any other.

A character who already holds an alignment-bound class and later drifts away from the alignment it requires is a separate case, see Fallen and Risen Classes below.

For example, a Centyr is Averse toward Doom Knight, an Evil-aligned class. If a Centyr's background is assigned Evil alignment at creation, they can take up Doom Knight but apply the -5/-7/-9 penalty (see Favored and Averse Class Bonuses, Pg. 2) instead of a neutral baseline, since a character must be Evil-aligned to play a Doom Knight, not Neutral or Lawful. If a Centyr's background is assigned Neutral alignment at creation, they cannot take up Doom Knight unless they later become Evil through play, where they can begin choosing Abilities from the Doom Knight class tree. The same logic runs in reverse for Favored classes, the bonus only applies once the character has been assigned an alignment and taken that class. See Alignment and Class Compatibility below.

After choosing their race, a player chooses their main class. At each level of progression, they choose one Ability or Spell from their active main class tree, alignment permitting†. Through the first 3 levels, players choose one Ability or Spell out of 3 available to that main class. At level 4, they may begin choosing Abilities from their Path, or they may begin choosing from another main class tree, again alignment permitting. If they stay on the main class's Path from level 4 through level 20, they'll have the choice of choosing Abilities or Spells from that Path's Branch, all the way through level 30, maxing out that tree, which can only be done once since the Ability and Spell cap is 59.

†Remember, some classes require a specific alignment, such as the Avenger requiring Lawful alignment.

Every Ability and Spell roll made in that class is then modified by the race's Favored or Averse standing toward it (see Favored and Averse Class Bonuses, Pg. 2), along with Traits, buffs from magic or advantages, and other enhancements, such as those from Armaments.

Characters can carry any Armaments they want, so long as they can bear the weight, but only ready a restricted loadout at the start of each game day (see Weapons Carried, Pg. 4). An Ability or Spell can be chosen the moment its prerequisites are met, but it cannot be used until its required Armament is readied, and the readied loadout only changes at the start of the next game day.

For example, a character can wear a 1h holy hammer and shield with chain armor, and use Avenger Abilities and Spells through level 3. At level 4, they could choose an Ability from the Monk path, but would have to wait until the next game day, doffing the chain, hammer and shield the day before, and donning the Armaments the Monk path requires (quarterstaff, robe, leather, etc.), before they could use the new Monk Ability they chose.

Remember, Armaments must be readied daily, and every class, Path, and Branch has its own allowed and restricted Armaments that must be readied before its Abilities or Spells can be used.

Each path offers two starting armament sets. Players choose one set at the start. Both sets are valid for that path, the choice comes down to playstyle. For example, an Avenger choosing the Cleric path may start with a shield and 1h blunt weapon, or a 2h blunt weapon.

Beyond the starting sets, characters may use any weapon or armor they can physically carry, as long as they've chosen an Ability or Spell that requires it, and they meet the Strength and capacity requirements to carry it. Armament options expand with each path and branch as characters progress.

Players manage their Abilities with cards. The Starter Set includes classes up to Level 5 only.

For instance, an Avenger, which later has path choices between; Cleric, Monk and Priest, will choose Abilities that can be used only with their weapon choice(s). They can choose more than one weapon, if they're strong enough to carry it.

An example: Garoth chooses the main class avenger, looks at the Ability Cards (see SORC Cards, Action) for this class, its path and later its path's branch and chooses the basic armaments; a heavy canvas ghee armor set and a quarter staff as his weapon. This weapon gets him through the first three given avenger Abilities, and the proficiency of learning Abilities in the Monk path (see below). As an avenger, he could have also chosen a 1h hammer and shield or 2h mace, or several other combinations of weapon combinations.

He also notices that later in the Monk path, he'll be able to use Abilities that allow him to strike with one hand and chooses a holy symbol related to his higher power of choice that he would be able to hold in the other hand later.

Note: certain monk classes can eventually dual wield, using a quarterstaff in their mainhand and a smaller weapon in their offhand.

The heavy ghee will have less of an Armor Score (AS) than Plate, but he'll have more mobility which will add to his overall Protection Score (PROTS). More on combat below.

Each class below offers Unique bonuses and Penalties, referred to as native bonuses and Penalties, and thereafter has its own combat Abilities, both martial and caster, and variations of armor and weapon choices through 30 levels.

Two examples:

A fighter class that prefers to use their hands, that excels in the brawler path, will learn to use environmental objects well, can develop tough skin, and also learn proficiency in a wide variety of weaponry, but they'll have already passed up a lot of that weapon's proficiency during bare knuckle brawling style combat.

An Avenger, restricted to blunt weapons only, that chooses the priest path can eventually use daggers if they choose the Salii branch.

Note: some path restrictions can be unlocked or overridden through specific branches as characters progress, such as the Avenger main class, whose Priest path's Salii branch unlocks the use of magical daggers. Salii daggers must be enchanted before the Salii can use them, they cannot be coated with poisons or carry other applied effects, but may be used to strike and cast. Other class paths carry similar branch-specific rules regarding restrictions and unlocks.

Players are not restricted to what weapon they can use, but they won't be able to use them unless they have chosen Abilities that utilize them. For instance the level 4 monk can buy a sword, but they'd have to wait until level 5 and choose a new main class tree, such as warrior earning its first native Ability that allows the use of the sword. Bear in mind the monk, now a monk/warrior sacrificed learning the second Ability in the monk path.

Moving along, there are other restrictions that don't allow the use of armaments, such as agility prerequisites for certain ranged Abilities, strength or charisma to wear certain armor, and path restrictions to weapon types; eg. † monks and † clerics can't use edged weapons, and warlocks cannot use holy weapons while paladins can't use unholy weapons; however paladins are not restricted to any weapon type, unless they don't meet other requirements, such as the agility prerequisite to use a bow.

† Line paladins, monks and clerics cannot use unholy weapons, unless they fall and upset the higher power of their choice which will restrict the use of all Abilities and powers of the tree; however †fallen classes have their own main class trees that require 10 levels in their former main class as they sustained restrictions until no longer usable.

† Fallen and Risen classes are detailed below and further in the GM Codex.

Each main class tree runs from 1-3 Native picks through Path picks (4-20) into Branch picks (21-30), 30 total for a single line, growing exponentially in power toward the end, the jump from pick 29 to 30 is the largest in the tree, with the 30th pick substantially more powerful than everything before it. This gives players tough decisions between choosing power or diversity among their picks.

Some classes run a single line like this. Others run two parallel lines instead, forking at every level from Native through Branch, across every path and branch that class has, each line gated behind its own respective armament set. What the two lines represent depends on the class: for the Avenger and the Strategist, the two lines are Abilities and Spells side by side. For classes built around a role split instead of a resource split, both lines might be Abilities (or both Spells), divided by playstyle rather than by casting versus martial. A class's entry notes whether it runs one line or two, and what those two lines are, when it does, see Starter Set Class Trees below for the full structure of every class playable at launch.

The 59 Ability cap ensures no character can fully max two class trees. At most, one tree can reach 30, and the same governs a class's two lines when it has them: a character can invest in both of a split class's lines at once, but can fully max only one of them to 30, leaving at most 29 across everything else they hold, whether that's the class's other line or an entirely separate tree.

With a maximum of 3 weapons carried and the Choose Weapons Per Day rule (see Combat), a character cannot activate Abilities from every class tree in a single session regardless of how many they possess. This makes wide-spread Ability investment genuinely viable across a campaign, unused trees become available when the situation calls for them, even if they sit idle in a given day's session.

Alignment and Class Compatibility

A character's alignment is determined by their backstory during creation and remains their foundational choice until altered by play. Each module provides curated backgrounds with built-in moral and ethical elements, Lawful, Evil, or Neutral, visible to players and GMs alike, since it's going to be shared with the player anyway. When a background is selected, the GM confirms the character's starting alignment based on that background's elements. Players can also create custom backgrounds with the GM, with alignment considered as part of that creation. Alignment determines which classes a character can access. Two classes require Lawful alignment, Avenger and Overseer. Four classes require Evil alignment, Doom Knight, Warlock, Witch, and Tyrant. The remaining ten, Barbarian, Bard, Druid, Fighter, Magic User, Marksman, Shaman, Sorcerer, Strategist, and Warrior, accept all alignments. Once an alignment has been assigned, players select their class or classes from those available to that alignment. A Lawful character can combine Lawful classes with any neutral class, but cannot hold Evil-tree Abilities. An Evil character can combine Evil classes with any neutral class, but cannot hold Lawful-tree Abilities. A Neutral character can freely combine Neutral classes together, but cannot select either Lawful-only or Evil-only classes. Alignment is not fixed after creation; it shifts over time through a character's choices, mission statements, and actions during play. A character's current alignment position is tracked on the Alignment bar of their Character Sheet's Progression Tracker. If a mission statement or actions drift significantly from a character's alignment, it may trigger Fallen or Risen mechanics. Warning: a falling or rising character whose alignment drifts all the way to Neutral can no longer learn new Abilities or Spells from the class tree they are leaving, see The Neutral Threshold under Fallen and Risen Classes below. See Fallen and Risen below, and Titles under Prestige, for how a shifting alignment reshapes a character's class standing.

Ability Progression

Abilities and Spells carry no point cost. At each level of progression, the player selects one Ability from their active class tree. Once chosen, that Ability is available whenever its required armament is equipped (for martial Abilities) or drawn (for casting Abilities and Spells).

The 59 Ability Cap, Three Build Examples

Example 1 (30 + 29 = 59), Theron, Warrior (All) / Barbarian (All)
Both neutral trees, no alignment conflict. Theron has 30 Abilities in Warrior, all of them, through the Blade Master path and the Duelist branch. He then chose 29 in Barbarian through the Mountaineer path. The 30th Barbarian Ability sits one step out of reach. 30 + 29 = 59. He chose one peak over two. Total dominance in one tree, one step short of mastering a second, nothing else.

Example 2 (20 + 20 + 19 = 59), Lyra, Sorcerer (All) / Marksman (All) / Bard (All)
All three neutral trees, no alignment conflict. Lyra has 20 Abilities in Sorcerer (Wizard path), 20 in Marksman (Bowman path), and 19 in Bard (Strumalong path). Branches open at Ability 21, none of her three trees have crossed that threshold. She holds full working paths in two trees and nearly all of a third, with no branch Abilities in any of them. Which role she fills each session depends entirely on what she draws.

Example 3 (14 trees, 3-5 Abilities each = 59), Varek
Because lawful and evil trees cannot be combined, the maximum-diversity build draws from 14 compatible trees. Varek is an evil-neutral character holding 3 native Abilities in each of his 14 trees, Barbarian (All), Bard (All), Doom Knight (Evil), Druid (All), Fighter (All), Magic User (All), Marksman (All), Shaman (All), Sorcerer (All), Strategist (All), Tyrant (Evil), Warlock (Evil), Warrior (All), and Witch (Evil), for 42 total. All 14 trees also carry Ability 4, the first step into that tree's path, adding 14 more for 56. Three trees, Barbarian (Destroyer path), Doom Knight (Death Guard path), and Warlock (Grimlock path), each hold a 5th Ability, bringing him to 59. No branches. No tree exceeds Ability 5. Varek cannot outlast or outpower a specialist in any role. But there is almost no situation a campaign can generate that leaves him without an answer. His ceiling is always someone else's. His floor never is.

Slots and Prerequisites, every single pick point in a class tree (Native 1-3, Path 4-20, Branch 21-30) is called a Slot. Each Slot offers three named choices; a character learns only ONE of the three per Slot, and that pick still counts as a single point against the 59-point Ability cap described above, offering three choices never adds extra picks.

Starting at Path Slot 6 and continuing through Branch Slot 23 onward, one or more of a Slot's three choices may carry a Prerequisite: a specific earlier choice from two Levels back, never the Slot directly before it, from that SAME Path or Branch and that SAME line (an Ability Prerequisite must be an earlier Ability, a Spell Prerequisite must be an earlier Spell), that a character must already know before the later choice becomes available. For example, choosing the "y" option at Path Slot 15 might require the "x" option chosen at Path Slot 13; a later Slot's Prerequisite might chain off that same Attack, Protection, or Utility choice again, or off a different one entirely, so the type it draws from isn't fixed from one Prerequisite to the next. Prerequisites never cross from one Path or Branch into another, and never cross from the Ability line into the Spell line. Native Slots (1-3) never carry Prerequisites, since these are a character's opening choices before any Path exists yet.

These Abilities increase in potency as players advance within their chosen class paths, with the most powerful Abilities appearing toward the end of the tree. However, players are not restricted to only one class tree; instead, they can select Abilities from multiple trees to create versatile or specialized characters, provided they meet the proficiency requirements for the weapons and gear associated with each tree. To learn new Abilities, characters must visit trainers scattered across the world, which may involve undertaking quests or exploring dangerous areas.

If a character levels up deep within a dungeon or remote location, they can potentially use their Wayfarer skill, if available, or coordinate with other group members, such as mages or nearby trainers, to mark locations for later visits. This mechanic encourages strategic planning and resource management, as players balance their desire for powerful Abilities with the time and risk involved in learning new skills, ensuring that character growth remains a dynamic and engaging aspect of gameplay.

Avenger (Playable in Starter Set)

Alignment: Lawful

Fallen and Risen Classes

These rules are under heavy development. Details are subject to change.

Only classes bound to a Higher Power are capable of falling or rising. These are classes whose Abilities are granted by an external force, their alignment, oaths, and relationship with that power are the foundation of everything they can do.

Which Classes Can Fall or Rise

Lawful classes (Paladins, Clerics, Monks, Overseers, Avengers, etc.), bound to divine or lawful Higher Powers, may Fall.

Evil classes (Warlocks, Witches, Tyrants, Doom Knights, etc.), bound to evil or chaotic Higher Powers, may Rise.

Beginning the Path

A character may begin falling or rising at the very start of their main class creation, or drift into it through accumulated Title choices during play. Either way, once the Higher Power takes notice, penalties begin immediately and escalate systematically until Level 20. The GM tracks all fall/rise progression on their GM Sheet. Players track their side of the journey on the Character Sheet's Progression Tracker, which carries two bars for this purpose: the Alignment bar (a spectrum running Lawful, Neutral, Evil) records the character's current alignment position and drift direction, and the Falling/Rising bar records the character's current punishment Stage (1-9) and progress toward Level 20. Survival on this path is genuinely difficult, and that is by design.

Declared at Creation: A character announces they are beginning a Fall or Rise. Penalties begin at Level 1.

Undeclared (Drifting): A character may fall or rise without declaring it at creation. The GM tracks this through the character's accumulated Boasted and Exposed Titles. As one side outweighs the other, the character's Higher Power takes notice, and penalties begin to apply immediately, well before level 20. At level 20, the GM averages the character's Boasted and Exposed Titles to determine whether the fall or rise is confirmed. See the GM Codex for the full mechanic on weighing Titles toward this average.

The Punishment System, Two-Part Escalation

From the moment a Higher Power notices a character's Fall or Rise, they apply two escalating punishments: (1) Spell Reversals on abilities/spells cast from the original class tree, and (2) Class-Specific Tests sent to challenge the character's resolve. Punishments escalate in severity and frequency as the character approaches Level 20. The GM tracks this progression on their GM Sheet.

Part One: Spell Reversal

When a falling or rising character casts a spell or uses an Ability from their original class tree, the Higher Power may reverse it onto the caster. If the original spell harms others, the reversed spell harms the caster instead. If the original spell buffs the caster, the reversed spell debuffs the caster. The reversed spell carries the full power and effect of the original, but inverted.

Example, Falling Warlock: Original spell "Suck Vitality" drains 40 Life from a target and grants that Life to the Warlock. Reversed: The Warlock loses 40 Life instead and their target gains that Life (the Warlock is healed by their victim).

Example, Rising Tyrant: Original ability "Dominate Will" forces a target to obey one command. Reversed: The Tyrant becomes compelled to obey a command from any creature that speaks to them.

Frequency Progression: The chance of reversal escalates as the character approaches Level 20, determined by the stage of their Fall/Rise. The GM rolls once each time the character casts from their original tree:

  • Stages 1-2 (Levels 1-9): Roll 1d10. On a 1, spell reverses.
  • Stages 3-4 (Levels 10-15): Roll 1d6. On a 1, spell reverses.
  • Stages 5-6 (Levels 16-19): Roll 1d4. On a 1, spell reverses.
  • Stage 7 (Level 19, final month before transformation): Roll d100. On 1-50, spell reverses (50% chance).
  • Stage 8 (Level 20, the final week): Roll d100. On 1-75, spell reverses (75% chance).
  • Stage 9 (Level 20, the final days): Roll d100. On 1-90, spell reverses (90% chance).

Part Two: Class-Specific Tests and Adversaries

In addition to spell reversals, the Higher Power sends tests and adversaries to challenge the character's resolve. These encounters happen up to 3 times per day and can occur at any time, during day, night, during sleep, or while adventuring. Characters suffer resting penalties if a test occurs during their Long Rest sleep (they only receive Short Rest recovery that night).

Test Progression: Tests begin relatively benign and escalate in difficulty and danger. Early tests may be challenges or warnings. Mid-path tests become dangerous confrontations. Late-stage tests are life-or-death situations designed to stop the Fall/Rise entirely.

Class-Specific Test Charts: Each class has unique adversaries and test types determined by their Higher Power. The complete list of class-specific tests, including what adversaries may attempt (steal, trick, trap, harm, curse, hex, etc.) and what they seek, is detailed in the GM Codex. The GM consults the appropriate class chart and may choose any option listed, or improvise similar tests within reason. Examples:

  • Falling Paladin: Might face Divine Champions sent to test faith, temptations to break oaths, or trials of moral suffering. Adversaries may attempt to prove the Paladin's faith is false, steal symbols of their deity, or trap them in situations requiring a broken oath to escape.
  • Rising Warlock: Might face agents of their former patron seeking to reclaim them, manifestations of curses they've cast, or victims of their dark power. Adversaries may attempt to bind them in contracts, curse them with their own magic, or trap them with people they've harmed.
  • Falling Cleric: Might face manifestations of lost faith, clergy from their order demanding return, or divine punishment. Adversaries may steal relics, challenge their doctrine, or trick them into blasphemy.

Outcomes of Tests:

  • Character Passes/Overcomes the Test: The Higher Power grants a brief reprieve. No immediate punishment penalty applies. The character may feel a moment of clarity or strength. However, the test still counts toward the Fall/Rise progression, another test will follow.
  • Character Fails/Surrenders to the Test: The Higher Power tightens its grip. The character suffers an additional mechanical penalty determined by the GM (examples: -2 to all Ability checks for 24 hours, one random Ability weakened, loss of 1d4 Endurance chips, or accelerated progression toward the next test stage). The test counts fully toward Fall/Rise progression.

The Neutral Threshold, Spell Learning Cutoff

As a falling or rising character's alignment drifts across the spectrum toward its opposite pole, it passes through Neutral, the midpoint of the Alignment bar on the Character Sheet's Progression Tracker. The moment a falling or rising character's alignment reaches Neutral, their original Higher Power withdraws its investment entirely: the character can no longer learn any new Abilities or Spells from the class tree they are leaving. Trainers of that tree will not teach them, and no new Abilities from that tree may be selected at level-up from that point forward.

  • Abilities already learned remain usable, but they remain fully subject to Spell Reversal punishment (see Part One above) until the Level 20 transformation.
  • The cutoff is permanent for the duration of the Fall/Rise. The only way to resume learning from the original tree is to turn back before Level 20 and restore standing with the original Higher Power, see redemption in the GM Codex.
  • Level-up selections after the Neutral Threshold must come from other compatible class trees the character holds, or be banked until the Level 20 transformation opens the Fallen or Risen tree.
  • The GM confirms when the Neutral Threshold is crossed (tracked on the GM Sheet); the player updates their Alignment bar to match.

The Level 20 Gate

A character can only fall or rise at exactly level 20, no earlier, no later. Before that point they exist in between, penalized by their old power, not yet claimed by the new one. By chance, the higher power in question will most definitely attempt to stop the transition, but the character is also protected by their new higher power. Nevertheless, they may not survive.

What Happens at Level 20

When a character fully falls or rises:

  • They lose 10 Abilities permanently. Of the 20 Abilities held at this point - 3 native and 17 path, the player chooses which 10 to carry into the new tree; the other 10 are lost.
  • The 10 chosen Abilities weaken - shift, and transfer, changing in flavor and effect to reflect their new alignment. Each ability tree they drew from is locked.
  • The standard 59 Ability cap still applies from this point forward.

The Two Trees

There are only two paths available to fallen and risen characters:

  • The Fallen Tree, for lawful classes who have abandoned their oaths and Higher Power. Paladins, Clerics, Monks and others who fall enter here.
  • The Risen Tree, for evil classes who have turned toward the light. Warlocks, Witches, Tyrants, Doom Knights and others who rise enter here.

Each tree has three branches of 30 Abilities each, consistent with every other class tree. Since the transition occurs at level 20 and branches do not begin until Ability 21, no character will have chosen a branch at this point, that choice lies ahead in the new tree.

Fallen and Risen are themselves the new main class. As with all classes, characters may still draw from any other compatible class trees up to the 59 Ability cap, alignment restrictions continue to apply.

What Makes These Abilities Different

Fallen and Risen Abilities are slightly more powerful than standard class Abilities, but the real distinction is in their nature and duration. Fallen Abilities lean toward exclusive harm-causing effects that linger, debuffs, corruptions, and damage that are dramatically exaggerated in how they are applied and persists a bit longer over time than their regular counterparts. Risen Abilities lean toward healing and restorative effects that last longer than their standard counterparts, and tend to do so in a completely different nature than the ordinary. The biggest challenge and reward is surviving the path to get there.

Advantages

  • Access to a unique hybrid ability set blending remnants of their old class with the power of their new tree.
  • Fallen and Risen Abilities carry longer lasting effects than standard class Abilities of the same level.
  • Fallen and Risen characters carry unique titles and are recognized - or feared, across Essentia.
  • Because old ability trees are locked rather than erased - certain redemption paths may exist to reclaim dormant Abilities. See the GM Codex.

A Note on Prestige

Falling or rising dramatically affects a character's Prestige, alignment, reputation, and faction standing. Former allies may become enemies. New and unexpected alliances become possible. These are not just mechanical changes, they reshape the character's story entirely.
