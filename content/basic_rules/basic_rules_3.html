Combat

Combat is central to SorC, involving clear, consistent rules for resolving attacks, damage, and special techniques. Players describe actions, and the GM applies rules to determine outcomes, focusing on tactical and strategic gameplay.

  • Attacks: Compare the attack roll against the defender’s Defense Score (DEFS). Attack and damage are rolled simultaneously (e.g., 3d6). Success occurs if attack roll meets/exceeds DEFS; exact match deals half damage; failure means miss. Damage is reduced by the defender’s Armor Score (AS). Spells ignore armor unless armor can absorb magic. Attack modifiers include attributes, skills, bonuses, penalties, and limb-specific targeting (LST), which affects hit chance and damage.
  • Special Attacks & Techniques: Includes nature/spell abilities, miracles, combos, critical hits, brutal hits, gruesome kills, and various maneuvers. Class abilities, racial traits, and environmental factors influence combat options. LST allows precise hits on body parts, increasing damage but with higher difficulty and risks if missed. Critical hits occur on natural 20s, adding bonus damage and potential penalties to defenses.
  • Damage & Resistance: Damage types include slash, pierce, blunt, fire, ice, lightning, poison, acid, psychic, radiant, necrotic, force, sonic, blood, nature, and arcane. Creatures and objects have resistances or vulnerabilities, affecting damage calculation. Magic spells typically bypass armor unless armor has absorption properties against specific magic types. Resistance subtracts damage; weaknesses/vulnerabilities increase damage taken.
  • Defense & Difficulty Scores: DEFS: Represents a character’s ability to withstand attacks, influenced by armor, stats, and traits. DIFS: Used for non-combat challenges, like disarming traps. When attack roll equals DEFS, half damage is dealt; if below, attack misses.
  • Other Combat Elements: Surprise: Determined by Vigilance checks; surprise can give enemies the first move. Armor Score (AS): Reduces damage after a hit. Various attack results include normal hits, crits, dodges, blocks, resistances, counters, and special conditions like prone or stunned. LST involves penalties based on the targeted limb and can cause opponents to lose defenses temporarily if missed.
  • Actions & Tactics: Basic attacks include unarmed, melee, grappling, and special maneuvers like ambushes or combo hits. Out-of-combat and environmental damage are handled with difficulty scores. Attack opportunities (AoO) are triggered when enemies move through threatened squares. Multiple attack types (e.g., brutal hits, gruesome kills) add narrative flair and strategic depth.
  • Other Notes: Miracles and nature/spell abilities can be invoked with specific conditions. Initiative is based on attributes, speed, and situational factors, determining turn order. Time is measured in rounds and days, influencing combat pacing and logistics. Dying may result in penalties, resurrection, or becoming undead with specific consequences and processes for revival.

Panic Checks

When characters are in situational instances outside of combat that may trigger panic, such as scaling a high cliff or swimming across a roaring river while your stamina is being drained, or being chased by a monster, attempting to hide from danger, the GM may choose to roll a panic check against each character’s Courage score under certain circumstances.

Circumstances particularly threatening, terrifying, or the environment is intensely stressful, or if the character is inexperienced or vulnerable may require a panic check at the GM's discretion.

The GM rolls a d20 and adds the hiding characters’ Courage scores to the roll. If the total meets or exceeds the attacker’s Vigilance score (which is typically high for alert enemies like goblins with keen senses), the character remains calm and stays hidden. If the total is lower than the hunter’s Vigilance, the character becomes panicked and may accidentally reveal their position.

Once panicked, the character must make a noise check. Noises such as heavy breathing, eek, bumping or even odor can give the character away. The lower the outcome of their rolls, the louder the noise they make. If the noise roll indicates the noise is loud enough to be heard, the lurking threat is alerted. The threat then gains initiative and can make a free attack of opportunity if they choose to attack. Only characters whose noises are loud enough to be heard are at risk of being attacked.

Characters with certain talents or abilities, such as hide or invisibility respectively, add this to their rolls to boost their chances of avoiding detection.

Example in Play:
The party is hiding behind some ruined walls in a dark, tense village alley, trying to stay silent as a patrol of goblins with sharp senses searches nearby. The GM decides to roll a panic check for Rina, a nervous but brave fighter.

Rina has a Courage score of 8. The GM rolls a d20 and gets a 10; adding Rina’s Courage gives a total of 18. The goblins’ Vigilance score is 16, so Rina’s total exceeds that, and she remains calm and hidden.

Meanwhile, Mira, who is less confident, has a Courage score of 6. The GM rolls a d20 and gets a 12; adding Mira’s Courage totals 18 - still enough to beat the Vigilance, so Mira stays calm. However, Rolf, with a Courage score of 4, rolls a 5 on the d20, totaling only 9.

This is below the goblins’ Vigilance, so Rolf becomes panicked. Nervous and trembling, he accidentally knocks over a loose stone, creating a loud noise. The GM rules this noise is loud enough to be heard by the goblins, alerting them to the party’s position. The goblins gain initiative and move to attack, with the first goblin spotting Rolf and preparing to strike. The GM now rolls for the goblin count, which is 5 + 1d4. The GM determines there are 9 goblins against a party of 5, and each goblin gets to attack first. Characters with special abilities like invisibility can remain hidden but they need to roll separately to avoid detection. This allows them to avoid being attacked and gives them the possible element of surprise (see below) if positioned right.

This mechanic emphasizes how fear and nervousness can cause characters to inadvertently reveal their hiding spots, especially when facing enemies with heavy senses.
