Playable Classes

Reminder: As explained earlier in our rules, each Race features Favored and Averse main classes, dealing various bonuses and penalties respectively. See Favored and Averse Class Bonuses and Penalties under each Playable Race's entry, at Playable Races, pg. 2.

When dealing with Classes, it is also important to note a character's alignment determines which classes they can access tactics or spells from. Classes themed by alignment require the character to already hold that alignment before any of that class's tactics or spells can be chosen.

Alignment itself is determined by their background. When a background is selected (whether from a module's curated options or a custom background created with the GM), the GM confirms the character's alignment based on that background's moral and ethical elements immediately.

Once the character's background is confirmed by the player and GM, it locks in and cannot be changed. The player then chooses their race followed by their class, and while most classes remain open regardless of alignment, a few require a specific one.

For example, a character with an alignment of Lawful cannot choose tactics or spells from Doom Knight, an Evil-aligned class, unless their alignment later shifts to Evil through the bad deeds and choices they've made during adventuring, a shift that may happen rapidly or slowly depending on the nature of their practices.

Independent of alignment and a race's Favored or Averse standing, every main class tree also has its own required readied Armaments. This readied Armament Set consists of one donned armor piece and up to three carried weapon types, held for at least 24 hours before a new Armament Set can be readied. A weapon in hand may be swapped for a sheathed one to change which tactics or spells are accessible, at the cost of a turn to do so. Depending on what's donned or in hand, a character is either restricted from using certain tactics or spells outright, or sustains the Penalties described under Armaments, pg. 6.

After choosing their race, a player chooses their main class. At each Character Level, they gain one Ability (Tactics/Spells) pick to spend from their active main class tree(s), alignment permitting†, and a second pick at the halfway point toward the next Character Level, each pick filling one open Slot in a tree. Any pick can go toward the same tree as the one before it, advancing it a further Slot, or toward a different tree the character has access to. Within any single Slot, a player still chooses only one Ability out of the 3 available (see Slots and Prerequisites, below). At Slot 4 of a tree, its Path opens, letting a character continue investing in that tree's Path, or begin choosing from another main class tree's Tier instead, again alignment permitting. Staying on one main class's Path from Slot 4 through Slot 20, then continuing into that Path's Branch through Slot 30, maxes out that tree, which can only be done once since the Ability cap is 59 (see Class Tree Development, below, for the full pacing and capstone rules).

†Remember, some classes require a specific alignment, such as the Avenger requiring Lawful alignment.

Every Ability roll made in that class is then modified by the race's Favored or Averse standing toward it (see Favored and Averse Class Bonuses, pg. 2), along with Traits, buffs from magic or advantages, and other enhancements, such as those from Armaments.

Characters can carry any Armaments they want, so long as they can bear the weight. Characters can only ready a restricted loadout, three main and/or offhand weapons, ammunition and armor, at the start of each game day, and must keep that set for at least one day - 24 game hours (see Weapons Carried, pg. 4).

Abilities may be chosen from any tree the character wants, sequentially and progressively or sporadically, but they cannot be used unless the required Armaments are not only readied, but donned and drawn. Each readied loadout can be kept as long as the player chooses for their character, there's no expiration, but it must stay intact for at least 24 game hours, the next game day, before changing to a new Armament Set.

For example, a character can have a 1h holy hammer and shield readied and drawn while wearing chain armor, and use Abilities from the Cleric path (see Avenger) beginning at level 4.

At level 4, they could choose a Tactic from the Monk path, but would have to doff the chain and sheath the 1h holy hammer and shield in exchange for any wooden blunt weapons and light or delicate armor before using Monk path Tactics. The cloth or delicate armor and wood-only blunt weapon must be readied, even if it's respectively doffed and sheathed. Light and delicate armors are light in weight, so having either readied and carried doesn't require much in terms of strength. Three main weapons may be readied (drawn or sheathed) and switched at will, at the cost of their turn. Smaller weapons listed as ammunition (throwing daggers x3 per boot, one throwing axe per hip, etc., arrows in a quiver, etc.) are separate and don't need to be readied, but the character must be able to bear their weight and have the tools to carry them, for instance, quivers, sheaths, holsters, boot straps, belt straps, and so on.

Characters may switch in and out of Armaments at will, at the cost of the turn, as long as the Armaments are readied. They may carry as many as their strength/encumbrance will allow, including with the assistance of Companions, such as a Pack Mule, NPC Henchmen, and so forth, but this only makes them available, not readied, which can only be done every 24 hours.

Just as with main classes, each path and branch defines its own Armament Prerequisites that must be met to use its Abilities (see Basic Rules pg. 6). In terms of armor, paths are restricted by Grade, with Composite being the heaviest and Delicate the lightest. A path's armor threshold includes every Grade lighter than it: for instance, if Medium Grade is allowed, Light and Delicate are as well.

Weapons are required or restricted based not only on Style (Slashing, Blunt, Piercing, Chop, etc.), and there are other cases where specific weapons are allowed for certain tree branches, even though the weapon profile was earlier restricted for the main class and path tactics or spells. For instance, the Salii branch of the Priest path may use daggers (must be magic) and Medium Grade armor (again must be magic†), even though up to that point of the Avenger tree, neither is permitted.

†Any item (in this case daggers or Medium Grade armor) that has a magical effect.

To perform Abilities from the Avenger's - Cleric Path - characters are required to have readied a 1h blunt weapon and shield, or a 2h blunt weapon. Either satisfies the Path's Weapon Prerequisite Profile (see Weapon Prerequisite Profile, Basic Rules pg. 6), adding a choice of flavor and playstyle.

Maximizing their weight capacity (see Strength and Encumbrance), characters may carry various weapons and armor that allow them to utilize different main class, path and branch fighting styles, as long as these Armaments are readied and donned or drawn - in hand(s).

As Character's progress through a classes linear tier, path and branch, Armament options expand.

One Branch example is the Salii, which extends from the Priest Path, breaks away from Light Armor and 2h Blunt restrictions, allowing Medium Grade armor and Daggers (both must be Magic).

Within the Monk path, the Qirateka Branch eventually allows dual 1h Blunt wielding, or a 2h quarterstaff in the main hand with a small bladed weapon, such as a tonfa or sai, in the offhand.

Another example would be the Main Class Fighter that chooses to excel in the brawler path, where they will use their hands (wrapped) as well as environmental objects, such as; dust and dirt, broken glass, mugs, coin rolls, lamps, and so forth as their weapon choices. These characters will also develop tough skin, often wearing very little to protect themselves relying on their tough skin and urban acrobatics (see Olympiad Tree). Brawlers may wear Medium Grade bracers for their protection as well, giving them an element of parry (see Traits, Defense) for protection.

Certain Classes are also restricted to various modifications. For instance, because the Avenger Class alignment requires Lawful, Salii Priests cannot coat their daggers with poisons or carry other applied effects as a rogue or necromancer can. For a Salii the dagger must be magic and hold no properties such as applying poison. These daggers are only used to strike and cast, applying poisons to them would result in punishment by the Avenger's higher power.

Touching on some of what we've discussed above; Characters can have drawn any weapons, in hand(s), they choose, but they won't be able to perform certain Abilities if the weapons they're holding are not the requirements of that main class tree's Tier, Path and/or Branch.

This means a character that has chosen Tactics for a level 4 Monk can obtain a sword, and have it drawn, but they wouldn't be able to perform any of the Tactics since they require bare hands or various wood-only blunt weapons. They could use Actions during their turn to swap to a viable weapon, in this case either drop the sword (half an Action) or switch to a blunt/wood weapon (a full Action) if, in the latter case, they have one (of three possible - see readied weapons, pg. 4) readied and on their person.

Or if they have chosen Abilities from another class tree that allows the sword, e.g., Tactics from a Warrior Tree, they could just perform those Tactics. This system allows characters to hybrid through several classes at one time, sometimes without even changing 1 of 3 readied Armament sets.

However, there are other restrictions that don't allow the use of certain armaments, such as agility prerequisites for certain ranged Abilities, strength or charisma to wear certain armor, rank restrictions and path restrictions to weapon types; e.g., † Monks can't use edged weapons or blunt weapons made from anything other than wood, † Clerics can't use edged weapons, Warlocks cannot use holy weapons, while Paladins can't use unholy weapons; however Paladins are not restricted to any weapon type, unless they don't meet other requirements, such as the agility prerequisite to use an offhand dagger.

† Overseers and Avengers cannot use unholy weapons. They must begin to fall, taking penalties and challenges from their respective higher powers to Abilities of said tree(s).

† Fallen and Risen classes, although covered here are pg. 3 covered much more detailed in the GM Codex.

Each main class tree, and its Tier (levels 1-3), follows the same structure through its Path (4-20) into Branch picks (21-30), for a total of 30 (its cap) across the Tier and its respective Path and Branch, growing exponentially in power until the end. With that in mind, the tactic or spell power gap between pick 29 to 30 is the most significant in power of each tree. This gives players tough decisions between choosing raw power, moderate power and some diversity, or raw diversity with less power between their tactics and spells but gives them a wide array of choices per turn.

These Ability variations are derived from different main class tier, path and branch structures. In order for a main class to reach the 30 cap max Abilities, the character would have to choose linearly from Tier, through Path and Branch, staying on the same half of the Split, Tactic or Spell, the entire way. The Slot letter itself, A, B, or C, does not need to stay the same and can change freely level to level, only the Tactic/Spell half matters for the cap. For instance, an Avenger - Cleric - Warpriest reaches all 30 of the Cleric's Spells by staying on the Spell half at every level from Tier through Branch, picking Slot A at some levels, Slot B or C at others, whichever fits, while never once choosing a Tactic instead. This would be the ONLY route to a level 30 cap for that tree, since the Ability cap is 59. Breaking this linear pattern by crossing into the Tactic half at any point makes it impossible to reach 30. Not only because you won't be able to choose Abilities that have Prerequisites, but also because there are exactly 30 for each tier/path/branch.

It's important to note that Class tree structure varies. While some classes only give choices of one linear Tier, Path, and Branch, such as; Avenger Monk, Tactics only, and Strategist Rogue, also Tactics only, others run a Split Tier, Split Path, and Split Branch, such as; Avenger - Cleric and Strategist - Shadow Stalker, both offering a Split Tactic or Split Spell at every level of progression, from Path through Branch.

The 59 Ability cap (see pg. 3) ensures a character can only fully max one class tree. At most, one tree, from its Tier through Branch, can reach 30, and a second caps at 29 (total of 59). In the case of a Split class, its two halves share that same 30-cap rather than each reaching 30 separately, as long as they stay within the Split type, Tactic or Spell. For example, for Classes with Split Paths and Branches, that offer both Tactics and Spells (see Cleric), you can reach the 30th Ability choosing either Slot, A, B or C, but must stay within the Tactic or Spell split. You can choose between Tactics and Spells within the Split, but you'll never reach the 30th respective Ability. Not only because the math doesn't allow it, but you wouldn't have the Prerequisite Abilities for those that require them later.

Starting at Lvl 6 Path Slots A, B or C and continuing through Lvl 23 Branch Slots A, B or C onward, select Slot choices carry a Prerequisite. See Slots and Prerequisites, below, for the full rule, including how the Slot letter (A, B, or C) can change freely from one Prerequisite to the next, as long as it stays within the same Tactic or Spell half.

With a limit of 3 readied weapons carried Per Day (see above), a character cannot perform Abilities from every class tree in a single session. They could manage to go through a lot of them, within a day using three distinct weapon types on their person, swapping out at will, however. This makes wide-spread Ability investment genuinely viable for Characters allowing them to continue to choose Abilities throughout various class trees, maximizing only one. Characters without weapon types that adhere to their Abilities, can still use standard or makeshift Combat attacks (see Combat pg. 5). Characters can still hone their skills on crafts and hobbies, utilizing their endurance for the remaining time of the day, during their downtime waiting for the 24 hour readied weapon cool down.

Revisiting Page 2 Content Alignment and Class Compatibility

A character's alignment is determined by their backstory during creation and remains their foundational choice unless altered by decisions characters make during their adventures. Each module provides curated backgrounds with built-in moral and ethical elements related to Lawful, Evil, or Neutral.

Once a background is determined, the GM confirms the character's starting alignment based on that background's elements, alerting the player before confirming. Players can also create custom backgrounds built between themselves and the GM, who sets the alignment.

Alignment determines which classes a character can access. Two classes require Lawful alignment; Avenger and Overseer. Four classes require Evil alignment; Doom Knight, Warlock, Witch, and Tyrant. The remaining ten; Barbarian, Bard, Druid, Fighter, Magic User, Marksman, Shaman, Sorcerer, Strategist, and Warrior, accept all alignments. Once an alignment has been assigned, players select their class(es), tactics or spells from those available to that alignment.

A Lawful character can combine Lawful classes with any neutral class, but cannot hold Evil-tree Abilities. An Evil character can combine Evil classes with any neutral class, but cannot hold Lawful-tree Abilities. A Neutral character can combine Neutral classes together, but cannot select either Lawful-only or Evil-only classes.

Since Alignment shifts over time through a character's choices and actions during play, a character's current alignment position is tracked on the Alignment bar of their Character Sheet's Progression Tracker.

If a character's actions drift significantly from a character's alignment, it may trigger Fallen or Risen mechanics exclusive to that lawful or evil class tree. Falling or Rising characters whose alignment drifts all the way to Neutral can no longer perform or earn new Abilities from the class tree that is either lawful or evil AND they'll begin to take penalties and tests from their respective higher powers.

See Fallen and Risen below, and Titles under Prestige, for how a shifting alignment reshapes a character's class standing.

Class Tree Development

Abilities carry no point cost. A character gains one Ability pick at each Character Level, and a second at the halfway point toward the next Character Level, XP-wise, all the way from Character Level 1 through 30. The one restriction sits on the 30th Ability in any class tree, its capstone: it can only be claimed on a full Character Level, never at a halfway point, no matter how many picks a character has already banked toward that tree. Once chosen, an Ability is available whenever its required armament is equipped (for martial Tactics) or drawn (for casting Abilities).

The 59 Ability Cap, Four Build Examples

A tree's 30th Ability, its capstone, can only be claimed on a full Character Level, never at a halfway point (see Class Tree Development, above). A tree pushed hard from Character Level 1 reaches its 29th pick, one short of its capstone, by Character Level 15. From there, a player can choose to grab that capstone right away on any later Character Level, or hold off and build a second tree instead, spending both later Character Levels and halfway points on it, and only decide which tree gets its true 30th when a Character Level finally comes due. 30 + 29 = 59.

Example 1:

Theron, Warrior / Barbarian Tactic picks per level of progression.

30 Tactics Warrior Main Class, and 29 Tactics Barbarian Main Class for a total of maximum, 59 Tactics.

Both neutral trees, no alignment conflict. Theron puts every pick into Warrior from the start:

  • Character Level 1: Warrior's 1st Tactic (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 2 (150† XP, the midpoint between Lvl 1 and Lvl 2): Warrior's 2nd Tactic (Tier)
  • Character Level 2: Warrior's 3rd Tactic (Tier) - Warrior's Tier is complete
  • Halfway to Level 3: Warrior's 4th Tactic (Path begins)

†see pg. 2 for the XP table, covered in full detail in the GM Codex.

He continues this same pattern, one pick at every Character Level and one at every halfway point, sending every pick into Warrior. By Character Level 15, he chooses Warrior's 29th Tactic, from the Duelist branch, one short of the capstone, 30. The 30th Ability requires a full Character Level, never a halfway point, and only one can be claimed there.

Theron will need to wait 15 more levels before choosing that 30th Tactic.

Capped at 29 in Warrior, through the Blade Master path and into the Duelist branch, Theron reaches Character Level 16 and begins investing in the main class Barbarian, following the same pattern: Tier, Path, then Branch. By Character Level 29, he reaches Barbarian's 28th Tactic, and halfway to Level 30 he can choose Barbarian's 29th Tactic, from the Ghost branch.

At Level 30, Theron must now choose between the Duelist branch's (Warrior) 30th Tactic or the Ghost branch's (Barbarian) 30th Tactic. Theron chooses his 30th Tactic to cap the Warrior branch, Duelist.

Example 2:

Lyra, Sorcerer / Marksman / Bard Ability picks per level of progression.

20 Spells Sorcerer Main Class, 20 Tactics Marksman Main Class, and 19 Spells Bard Main Class for a total of maximum, 59 Abilities.

All three neutral trees, no alignment conflict. Lyra never sends two picks in a row to the same tree, she rotates:

  • Character Level 1: Sorcerer's 1st Spell (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 2: Marksman's 1st Tactic (Tier)
  • Character Level 2: Bard's 1st Spell (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 3: Sorcerer's 2nd Spell (Tier)

She continues rotating this way for the rest of her career, following her adventures rather than a fixed order. By Character Level 30, her picks land as 20 Spells in Sorcerer (through the Wizard path), 20 Tactics in Marksman (through the Bowman path), and 19 Spells in Bard (through the Strumalong path).

None of her three trees ever reach a capstone. A tree's 30th Ability requires being that tree's sole focus, and Lyra never sticks with one long enough to get there.

Example 3:

Varek, 14 trees Ability picks per level of progression.

3 to 5 Abilities in each of 14 Main Classes for a total of maximum, 59 Abilities.

Lawful and Evil trees can never be combined, so the widest possible spread for an Evil-Neutral character draws from 14 trees: Barbarian, Bard, Doom Knight, Druid, Fighter, Magic User, Marksman, Shaman, Sorcerer, Strategist, Tyrant, Warlock, Warrior, and Witch. Varek spreads wide from the start:

  • Character Level 1: Barbarian's 1st Tactic (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 2: Bard's 1st Spell (Tier)
  • Character Level 2: Doom Knight's 1st Ability (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 3: Druid's 1st Ability (Tier)

He continues cycling through all 14 trees this same way. Each tree picks up 3 Abilities (42 total), then each picks up its 4th, the first step into that tree's Path (14 more, 56 total). Three trees, Barbarian, Doom Knight, and Warlock, go one step further, reaching a 5th Ability each, the last 3 needed to hit 59, the cap.

No tree comes close to its capstone, and no branch is ever reached.

Example 4:

Kestrin, Strategist / Bard / Marksman / Sorcerer / Druid Ability picks per level of progression.

29 Abilities scattered across Bard, Marksman, Sorcerer, and Druid Main Classes, and 30 Abilities Strategist Main Class for a total of maximum, 59 Abilities.

All neutral trees, no alignment conflict. Through Character Level 15, Kestrin's first 29 picks go wherever the story calls for them:

  • Character Level 1: Bard's 1st Spell (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 2: Marksman's 1st Tactic (Tier)
  • Character Level 2: Sorcerer's 1st Spell (Tier)
  • Halfway to Level 3: Druid's 1st Ability (Tier)

She continues cycling this way through her first 29 picks: Bard 8, Marksman 8, Sorcerer 7, Druid 6, none of the four past their Tier and early Path.

Her 30th pick, arriving at the halfway point of Character Level 15, goes toward something new instead, the first Ability of Strategist. From there every remaining pick goes into Strategist alone, reaching 28 by Character Level 29, and 29 at the halfway point toward Level 30. Character Level 30 then delivers Strategist's 30th Ability, its capstone, her 59th pick overall, the cap.

Slots and Prerequisites, at each level of progression, Characters are granted a choice between ONE tactic or spell, or both (since there are two choices per level), of a path or branch between three slots; A, B or C.

Slot A is a Protector choice (Martial or Magic protection), Slot B is a Damage Dealer choice (Attack), and Slot C is a Heals/Utility choice (single-target heals, group heals, cleanse, survival, buffs and similar support effects), not limited to those examples.

A character gets one pick at each Character Level, and a second at the halfway point toward the next Character Level (see Class Tree Development, above, for the full pacing and capstone rules). The halfway point is reached when a character's cumulative XP hits the midpoint between their current Ch. Lvl's cumulative XP threshold and the next Ch. Lvl's threshold (see Character Level XP Progression, GM Codex Ch. II); for example, Ch. Lvl 15 sits at 161,300 cumulative XP and Ch. Lvl 16 at 194,100, so the halfway point toward Ch. Lvl 16 falls at 177,700 XP. Each pick fills a different Slot choice, whether the second choice is a linear advancement from the first or from a different main class's path or branch altogether. Once a pick is made, learned from a Class Trainer at a price, it locks in permanently, and there is no reverting.

Starting at Lvl 6 Path Slots A, B or C and continuing through Lvl 23 Branch Slots A, B or C onward, one or more of a Slot's three choices may carry a Prerequisite: a specific earlier choice from two Levels back, never the Slot directly before it, from that SAME Path or Branch and that SAME half of the Split (a Tactic Prerequisite must be an earlier Tactic, a Spell Prerequisite must be an earlier Spell), that a character must already know before the later choice becomes available. For example, choosing Slot C at Path Level 15 might require Slot A chosen at Path Level 13; a later Slot's Prerequisite might chain off that same Slot letter again, or off a different one entirely, so the letter it draws from isn't fixed from one Prerequisite to the next. Prerequisites never cross from one Path or Branch into another, and never cross from the Tactic half of a Split into the Spell half. Tier Slots (1-3) never carry Prerequisites, since these are a character's opening choices before any Path exists yet.

Note to writer: Avenger's Cleric Path (see below) has the Slot A/B/C (Protector/Damage Dealer/Heals-Utility, plus Martial/Magic type, damage type, and delivery/target detail) fully applied. Use it as the template for every other class's Tier and Path content that hasn't been converted yet.

Abilities increase in power in sequential order of advancement, with the 30th Ability bearing exponential power over the 29th. As we have learned, Characters are not restricted to only one main class tree's Tier, Path or Branch; which allows multiple trees to create versatile or specialized characters, provided they meet armament and alignment prerequisites associated with each tree. To learn new Abilities, characters must visit a Class Trainer, usually found in towns or metropolises across Essentia, and must pay a fee (prices vary per area); however, characters may run into NPC Class Trainers in isolated parts of the world, deep underground, deep in the woods, or in a vast desert as well, depending on the setting. This doesn't mean the Trainer will train the character, oftentimes Trainers will require Characters to establish stance with their factions before talking to them, let alone training them, but this usually isn't an issue in towns or large cities.

Class Trainers must be at least the Level of the Character to teach Abilities. Class Trainers only need to be of the Main Class to teach any Tier, Path or Branch within that Main Class, so as long as the Trainer is of the character's Level, they are qualified to teach the character Abilities.

Learning Abilities at the halfway point of the next Level still only requires the Trainer to be of that Level of the character. For instance, if a character is Level 11 and wants to learn his or her 12th Ability halfway to Level 12 (see rules), the Trainer still only needs to be Level 11 and must be a teacher of that main class's tree.

If a character levels up, or reaches a halfway point that grants Ability development, far from a town, for instance deep within a dungeon or remote location, they can potentially use their Wayfarer†, if available, or coordinate with other group members, such as mages (NPC or party), to port into the nearest town or city that would have a Trainer.

†See Animated Wayfarer, Basic Rules pg. 5, for the Faction Sense ability and what a Wayfarer can and can't tell its owner about a town's faction stance. Class faction alone is USUALLY enough stance for a character to learn Abilities, but if not, establishing enough stance within any faction within reason by completing a few quests usually earns the Trainer's trust.

Main Classes: Tiers, Paths and Branches

Before delving into each playable class's stat card, it's important to point out that playable classes are more than just a consolidation of stats. Playable Classes shape Characters at the table, not just in fighting style but culturally and retrospectively (see backstory) as well.

The Ogre warrior who trusts dual clubs and fur armor over a battle axe and plate, the human sorcerer who chooses a tribal war dress and staff over his well-off relative's robe and crystal-set staff, or the halfling strategist who wins a fight before it ever starts, while his elven comrade in arms chooses the Spellblade role within Strategist.

There are sixteen main classes available: Avenger, Barbarian, Bard, Doom Knight, Druid, Fighter, Magic User, Marksman, Overseer, Shaman, Sorcerer, Strategist, Tyrant, Warlock, Warrior, and Witch. Each runs its own unique Tier, Path, and Branch structure, with some leaning entirely on Tactics, some entirely on Spells, and some, such as the Avenger, offering Split choices between both.

Avenger (Playable in Starter Set)

Alignment Restrictions: Lawful

Faith-driven warriors bound to a higher power, at first restricted to blunt weapons and a light armor default threshold. As with all Paths and Branches, later Avengers unlock some 1h bladed weapons and Medium or Half Medium Armor.

Avengers practice righteous judgment showcasing an array of divine protection, healing, martial combative skills, hand to hand combat and mythical chants guided through their chosen deity or spiritual force. Where others seek to end a fight quickly, an Avenger is built to outlast it, wearing the wicked down through unshakable faith and sheer endurance until judgment finally comes due.

Fallen and Risen Classes

These rules are under heavy development. This is currently the full extent of the rule; more will be added over time.

Only classes bound to a Higher Power are capable of falling or rising. These are classes whose Abilities are granted by an external force, their alignment, oaths, and relationship with that power are the foundation of everything they can do.

Which Classes Can Fall or Rise

Lawful classes (Paladins, Clerics, Monks, Overseers, Avengers, etc.), bound to divine or lawful Higher Powers, may Fall.

Evil classes (Warlocks, Witches, Tyrants, Doom Knights, etc.), bound to evil or chaotic Higher Powers, may Rise.

Current Penalty

Falling or Rising characters whose alignment drifts all the way to Neutral can no longer perform or earn new Abilities from the class tree that is either lawful or evil, and they'll begin to take penalties and tests from their respective higher powers.
