Ranks

Character ranks reflect a hero's reputation and power. NPC and MAC ranks reflect the challenge and tier of any entity encountered. Each tier corresponds directly, a Pauper faces Common-rank threats, a Legend faces Divine. A character must be of a monster or challenge's Rank to engage it, reaching ahead into higher-ranked content isn't hidden, exactly, but blocks off as an obstacle (road guards, sealed gates, terrain too dangerous to cross) until Rank catches up, and none of its items or Companions can be found through exploration either. Characters cannot equip or use items ranked above their own, though they may still carry, store, or gift ones they've legitimately acquired some other way (gifted, traded, inherited). See pg. 4 for full rank rules. A character who retires at Legend rank keeps that status in the world's history, see Retirement, pg. 4.

The Seven Attributes

Attributes begin at 0 at creation, except where a race's Innate Trait Adjustments require a higher Starting Attribute Floor (see each race's entry) so no Trait exceeds its aligned Attribute. Hard cap: 50. Max 2 score per Attribute per level. See pg. 2.

Creativity, performance, dexterity-based crafts, and deception.

Fortitude (FRT)

Physical resilience, endurance, and resistance to ongoing effects.

Intellect (INT)

Perception (PER)

Physique (PHY)

Physical strength, speed, agility, and toughness.

Resilience (RES)

Magical resistance and protection from elemental damage.

Wisdom (WIS)

Attribute Development (Per Character Level)

Roll 1d6 nine times. Discard the two lowest results. Assign the remaining 7 results as points to Attribute Repository slots. Each Attribute may receive no more than 2 score per Character Level (Ch. Lvl). Cost: 3 REM Points per Attribute score, 2 REM Points per TST. Unused REM Points become Remnants saved for future Ch. Lvls. Attributes are first rolled at Level 0 (character creation). Once all results are assigned and locked in, the character advances to Ch. Lvl 1.

Vitality Summary

Vitality Progression: Life +18 per level + Constitution score. Mana +12 per level + Wit score. Stamina +12 per level + Willpower score. Endurance +11 per level. All sizes use the same rates, size identity is reflected in base values. Total Vitality = Base + (level x rate) + Trait modifier per level + Apex. Apex adds a flat bonus equal to the Apex score to all four Vitality resources. Developed through REM Point spending (2 per score), bounded by Wisdom score, capped at 25. Can be raised further through Relic quests. Spirit recovers 1 chip of Life, Mana, and Stamina per Spirit point between every turn, always active. Neither Apex nor Spirit are Traits. Vitality Cost Formula: an Ability's chip cost equals the Level it's learned at, divided by 2, rounded up, minimum 2 chips. Nature costs Stamina, Spell costs Mana, both use the same formula. Both also cost at least 1 action. Endurance base: 30 for all sizes, resets daily. See pg. 5.

Vitality Cost Formula: Chip Cost = ceil(Slot Level ÷ 2), minimum 2. A Level 3 Tier pick costs 2, a Level 5 Path pick costs 3, a Level 21 Branch pick costs 11, a Level 30 pick costs 15. This scales on Slot Level alone, the only axis the rules tie to power, rather than a separate multiplier per tier. Since a character gets one pick at each Character Level and a second at the midpoint toward the next Level (both landing together at Character Level 30, the exception, see Playable Classes, pg. 3), each pick is costed independently from its own Slot's Level, a Level that delivers two picks at once simply adds both costs together. It's deliberately kept low relative to Spirit's flat, Level-independent regeneration (see Spirit above), so Mana and Stamina stay a light per-turn throttle rather than a hard wall; Endurance's flat 30-actions-per-day cap remains the real limiter on how much a character can do across a full day. Full worked examples further covered step-by-step in the GM Codex.

Ability Uses Formula: N = floor(Character's Max Pool for that resource, Mana or Stamina, ÷ Ability's Cost), the number of times a full pool alone affords the Ability with no regeneration. Match N against a Tier: N≥12: Unlimited. N=6-11: 3/Round. N=4-5: 2/Round. N=3: 1/Round. N=2: 3/Day. N=1: 2/Day. Every Ability Card states both Cost and Uses, both must be satisfied to use the Ability again, Uses is the hard ceiling, Cost is whether the character can currently afford to reach it. Regardless of Tier, no Ability may be used more than once in the same Turn (Uses Cap, Per Turn, Basic Rules pg. 5), Round/Day Tiers govern spacing across Turns, never a same-Turn multiplier. Some Cards override the Tier outright, Resurrection, permanent transformations, and other narrative capstone effects are fixed at Uses: 1/Day by design, a Tier the formula alone can never reach since Breather/Short Rest regeneration always leaves room for a second use before a Long Rest. Worked example, Avenger's Shield-Wall Guard (Cleric Path, LVL 4, Slot A): Cost = ceil(4 ÷ 2) = 2 Stamina; at Level 4 a character is still at Growth 0, so Max Pool = base Standard Stamina, 32; N = floor(32 ÷ 2) = 16, N≥12, so Uses = Unlimited. Full worked examples further covered step-by-step in the GM Codex.

Base Vitality by Size: Goliath, Life 60, Mana 24, Stamina 40, Endurance 30. Standard, Life 50, Mana 32, Stamina 32, Endurance 30. Small, Life 40, Mana 40, Stamina 24, Endurance 30.

Ammo Damage Falloff

Every ranged weapon's listed damage is its Max Damage, dealt only within its Short band. Beyond Short, damage falls off incrementally by the same bands used for the to-hit penalty above, applying independently of it, a shot that still lands at Medium or Long range rolls against the harder to-hit penalty AND deals reduced damage on top of it. This applies uniformly across every ammo-based weapon type, Standard/Traditional Shoot, Thrown, TAW Ballistic, and TAW Energy alike, TAW Energy's charge weakening exactly the same way a Traditional arrow or TAW Ballistic round does the farther it travels.

Thrown weapons use the same three bands within their 15 ft. base range instead of a single flat distance, taking the same incremental falloff as every other ranged weapon type: Short 0-5 ft. Individual weapon, Thrown, TAW, and Energy cards may override these default percentages and band distances the same way they override standard range bands.

Armament Penalty (Armor) and Weapon Prerequisite (Weapons)

A character may use ANY class's Abilities, from any class tree they've spent Ability points into, with no penalty at all, so long as three things are true that day: the class's own default Armaments (its normal armor and weapon types) are part of that day's Armament Set, their alignment permits that class's Abilities, and they are physically able to carry what's equipped.

Armor and weapons are not treated the same way when mismatched, armor is a matter of weight and mobility, something raw physical capability can overcome, while a weapon requires meeting a class's own weapon Prerequisite, something a class's Abilities simply don't teach outside their own tree.

Armor Grade Threshold: Every class, Path, and Branch has a maximum Armor Grade its Abilities assume, stated in that tree's Armaments reminder (see Playable Classes, pg. 3, and Armor Grades & Materials, pg. 4, for the eleven Grades in ascending order: Delicate, Half Light, Light, Half Medium, Medium, Half Heavy, Heavy, Half Dense, Dense, Half Composite, Composite). Each Half Grade sits between a full Grade and the full Grade below it, a blend of the two, Half Light blends Light and Delicate, Half Medium blends Medium and Light, Half Heavy blends Heavy and Medium, Half Dense blends Dense and Heavy, Half Composite blends Composite and Dense. A character may wear their tree's maximum Grade, or any lighter Grade, with no Armament Penalty at all, weight and mobility below that ceiling are never a Prerequisite problem.

Armor Mismatch, Armament Penalty: When a character uses an Ability from a tree while wearing an Armor Grade heavier than that tree's maximum allows, a -5 Armament Penalty applies per Grade of difference, two Grades over the threshold applies -10, three Grades over applies -15, and so on. This penalty hits every roll made using that specific Ability, whether it's an attack roll, a Skill check, a crafting check, or any other action the Ability calls for, but only while that mismatched Ability is actually being used, never to unrelated actions. The penalty reduces by 1 per Grade of difference for every +5 a character has in whichever Trait most directly relates to overcoming that mismatch, typically Strength for encumbering armor. The GM determines the relevant Trait when it isn't obvious.

Example: A Monk's tree assumes Delicate Grade or lighter. A character with Heavy Grade (Plate) donned as their daily Armament is six Grades over that threshold (Delicate → Half Light → Light → Half Medium → Medium → Half Heavy → Heavy), taking a -30 Armament Penalty using any Monk Nature ability. With +5 Strength, that penalty reduces to -24.

Protector Trees: A handful of Paths and Branches built around frontline defense raise their tree's maximum Grade all the way to Dense or Composite, the two heaviest Grades that exist (see Armor Grades & Materials, pg. 4), meaning a character following one of these can don ANY armor in the game with zero Armament Penalty. This is never free: Dense and Composite both carry the steepest movement penalties of any Grade, and every Ability written for a Protector Path or Branch is built to trade damage output for raw survivability, expect a Protector to hit softer and move slower than a peer who stayed in lighter armor. See each class's Tiers, Paths and Branches, pg. 3, for which ones carry this trait.

Weapon Requirements: Every class, Path, and Branch has a required weapon profile stated in that tree's Armaments reminder (see Playable Classes, pg. 3), covering five parts: a Weapon Type (Martial, Magic, TAW, or Energy, see Weapon Type DMG Mods, pg. 4), a Weapon Style (Slashing, Blunt, Piercing, or Chop, for melee weapons; Shoot and Throw weapons carry no Style, see below), a Hand (1h (Main), 1h (Off), or 2h), a Range (Touch, Melee, Throw, or Shoot, each with its own distance band, see below), and a Grade & Material (the weapon's Grade, see Weapon Grade & Materials, pg. 4, and, for some Paths or Branches, a Material Restriction that narrows the Weapon Style further, such as wood-only). A weapon must satisfy every part of a tree's profile to activate its Abilities, matching the Weapon Type alone is not enough if the Style, Hand, Range, or Grade & Material don't also match.

Range: Every weapon and Spell resolves at one of five Ranges: Touch (self or an adjacent target, no distance), Melee (adjacent, weapon's reach), Throw (a thrown weapon's short distance, e.g. javelin, hand-axe, sling stone), Area (Spell-only, a distance shorter than Shoot that resolves against a zone), or Shoot (a mechanically launched weapon's long distance, e.g. bow, crossbow, black powder weapon, or a Spell's longest single-target cast distance). Throw and Shoot are not interchangeable, a thrown weapon and a drawn/mechanically loosed weapon are different Ranges even when their real-world distances overlap. Area still carries any Style (Cone, Line, Burst, Cloud, Single Target, or Chain), the Style just plays out at Area's shorter distance, a Line Style at Area Range becomes several short rays fanning out rather than one long beam, a Cone Style at Area Range becomes a tighter, closer wedge rather than a long one.

Weapon Type vs. Weapon Style: A weapon's Type is Martial, Magic, TAW, or Energy, the same Martial-or-Magic split used to flavor-tag Slots on pg. 3, plus the two culture-gated Types. A weapon's Style, where it has one, is Slashing, Blunt, Piercing, or Chop, describing a melee weapon's shape, not its Type. The two combine: Martial Blunt covers hammers, maces, clubs, and Staffs (Quarterstaff, Bō, Jō, Long Staff (2h), Short Staff (1h, dual-wieldable)); Magic Blunt covers Staves and wands, the dedicated caster implement, never a martial Staff, distinct in name specifically to avoid confusion between the two. The same split carries across the other Styles: a plain blade is Martial Slashing, an enchanted blade is Magic Slashing; a plain dagger or spear is Martial Piercing, an enchanted one is Magic Piercing; a plain axe or hatchet is Martial Chop, an enchanted axe is Magic Chop. Throw and Shoot weapons carry no Style at all, a javelin or hand-axe is Martial (Range: Throw); a bow or black powder weapon is Martial (Range: Shoot); a wand or orb channeled at range is Magic (Range: Shoot). Not every specific weapon crosses both Types, some are locked to one side by their nature, a two-handed hammer can only ever be Martial, an orb can only ever be Magic, while others, like blades, can go either way depending on how they're made. For damage-modifier purposes (see Weapon Type DMG Mods, pg. 4), a Magic-Type weapon uses the Magic row regardless of Style, a Martial-Type weapon uses its Style's own row instead. TAW and Energy stand apart from both Type values and from each other, both gated by culture and availability rather than training or magic (see Cultural Armaments & Rarity, below), but they are not the same thing: TAW is manufactured technology, a TAW blade or rifle built on an assembly line; Energy is elemental substance given a weapon's shape, a blade of living flame, a lightning whip, a coiled wind-blade, plasma, or raw force, never manufactured, mirroring the same TABA-versus-Energy split defined for armor above.

Spell Prerequisite Profile: Spells carry the same kind of profile weapons do, but built from five Spell-specific parts instead: a Cast (Verbal, a spoken incantation or chant; Somatic, a gesture or hand motion; Ritual, no Verbal or Somatic component required, but takes extended time to perform; Mind, no spoken, gestured, or ritual component at all, cast through will alone, covering ESP, psionics, and telekinesis; Reagents, cast by consuming a Reagent or Organic Element (Bone Dust, Bat Wings, Elderwood Ash, and the like, see Organic Elements, pg. 4) in place of Mana; Conjure, calls forth or animates a weapon, construct, or remains into being or motion, such as a conjured blade, spectral shield, or reanimated bones, that lingers and acts on its own each turn until dismissed or the encounter ends, not interruptible and not requiring further action from the caster, who is free to act or perform skill checks without losing it; Summon, calls a living or spirit creature to act with limited agency on the caster's behalf, such as a bound spirit-hawk or a hovering shrunken head, under the same non-interruptible terms as Conjure; or Orbital, an entity of various forms, shaped from raw magic rather than a weapon, creature, or remains, that hovers or floats around the caster to protect, attack, buff, or curse on its own, typically lasting for longer periods of time rather than resolving in a single instant, under the same non-interruptible terms as Conjure), a Mechanic (Instant, resolves immediately; DoT, damage ticking each round; HoT, healing ticking each round; Channel, held up turn after turn and interruptible if the caster is struck; or Drain, cast by spending Life, Stamina, or Endurance instead of Mana), a Style (Cone, a wedge extending from the caster; all other Styles, such as Line, Burst, Cloud, Single Target, and Chain, TBA), a School (the Spell's elemental or magical flavor, e.g. Fire, Ice, Arcane, Acid, Lightning, Necrotic, Holy, Psychic, Nature; a School can carry its own lingering sub-effect, such as Acid's Corrosion degrading equipment over time), and a Range (Touch, Shoot, or Area, see above; a Spell channeled through a melee or thrown weapon instead uses that weapon's Melee or Throw Range). A Spell's full profile is noted on its Spell Card.

Armor Type: Armor splits four ways, Martial (ordinary craft, steel, leather, hide, bone), Magic (its protective quality comes from an inherent magical nature rather than craft alone), TABA (Technologically Advanced Body Armor, gated by culture and availability the same as TAW, see pg. 4), and Energy (elemental in nature, plasma, living flame, raw force rather than manufactured plating, gated the same way as TABA but never built by Omnè's engineering). TABA and Energy are not the same thing, TABA is manufactured technology, Energy is elemental substance given form, a plasma cuirass is Energy even if no factory anywhere produced it. Any Armor Grade can in principle be reached by any of the four Types, a Heavy Grade breastplate might be plain Martial steel, TABA plating, elemental Energy given shape, or a rare Magic-forged piece, though some Structures lean toward one Type by nature, Force Fields are inherently TABA, Energy Shields are inherently Energy, and Mithril is inherently Magic, Type, Structure, and Grade (Half Light) are one and the same for it, it is never built as Martial, TABA, or Energy.

Cultural Armaments & Rarity: TAW and Energy are not special cases, they're an example of a wider rule: every playable race has its own culture-tied Armaments, a Weapon Type, armor style, or Martial or Magic technique unique to its people. Troll-make weaponry and armor and Ignis's Primal Armaments and Accessories (see pg. 4) are only two named examples among many, every race carries its own, whether written up yet or not. Each is rare outside that culture's reach. It becomes common only when at least one of two things is true: the campaign setting is within or near that culture's territory (an Omnè refugee camp counts, even off-world, same for a settlement on or near Ignis), or the character themselves belongs to that culture, whether by race or by a Background raised among its people (see Backgrounds, pg. 2), a human raised by Omnè qualifies the same as an Omnè character would, same for one raised on Ignis. Outside both conditions, that culture's Armaments are rare to find, buy, or craft, though a GM may still roll to let a character have managed to acquire a piece anyway, at their discretion. Many interactive MAC creatures carry their own MAC-exclusive Armaments the same way, entire classes built around them, Grave Wardens (Undead) wield Deathrattle bone weaponry and wrap themselves in Shroud Plate cured from grave-cloth and bone; Core-Wrought Sentinels (Construct) integrate a Forged-Core weapon limb and Plated Chassis armor directly into their bodies rather than wearing or wielding either; Alpha Predators (Beast) fight with natural Feral Claw and Hidebound weapon and armor types no character can equip or remove. These, and many more, are detailed per Module and/or in Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs rather than here.

Weapon Mismatch, Weapon Prerequisite: A class's Abilities can only be used with a weapon that satisfies its full Weapon Requirements. There is no penalty scale for this, no Trait reduces it, a weapon outside a class's required profile simply cannot be used to activate that class's Abilities at all. This is separate from the Armament Penalty above, and unlike armor, no amount of Strength, Agility, or any other Trait makes an unfamiliar weapon usable for a class's Abilities.

Example: An Avenger's Abilities assume a Blunt, 1h or 2h, Melee weapon. An Avenger wielding a dagger instead cannot use any Avenger Ability at all, regardless of Agility or any other Trait, until a blunt weapon is back in the day's Armament Set. A Sorcerer channels through a Magic, 1h or 2h, Melee-range Stave; wielding a different melee weapon instead, they cannot cast any Sorcerer Spell at all until the Stave is readied again.

Example, Material Restriction: A Monk's tree requires a Blunt weapon made of wood, 1h, 2h, or bare hands, Melee range. A hammer or mace is Blunt and Melee, but its metal head fails the wood-only Material Restriction, so it cannot activate any Monk Nature ability. A quarterstaff or nunchaku, being both Blunt and wood, satisfies the full profile and works normally.

Using a class's Abilities while equipped with that SAME class's own default Armaments for the day carries no Armament Penalty and no Weapon Prerequisite block at all, no matter how another class's Armaments compare.

Enchantment Creates a Magic Weapon: A weapon's Magic Type is not inherent in every case, most weapons start as Martial, and it's a successful Enchantment (see Enchanting, pg. 4) that converts a Martial weapon into a Magic one, this is what defines a magic weapon in these rules, not a glowing effect or a magical-sounding name, but having actually undergone Enchantment. A handful of dedicated caster implements, a Stave, wand, orb, or tome, are Magic by their own nature instead (see Weapon Type vs. Weapon Style, above) and never need Enchanting to qualify, the same way an orb can never be Martial no matter what's done to it.

Magic Weapons, Nature Drawn, and Spell Drawn: A Magic-Type weapon satisfies both a Nature Drawn ability's and a Spell Drawn ability's Weapon Requirements at once, so long as its Style, Hand, Range, and Grade & Material still match the tree's required profile, while a Martial-Type weapon only satisfies a Nature Drawn ability. This means a class like Barbarian, Ranger, Warrior, or Rogue, whose tree's Weapon Requirements stays weapon-based rather than Stave-or-orb-based across Tier, Path, and Branch, still performs its Nature Drawn abilities with the plain Martial version of that profile, the same as always, but casting one of its Spell Drawn abilities requires either an Enchanted (Magic-Type) version of that same weapon, or the tree's specific required default casting implement where one is stated instead (a Stave, orb, tome, wand, or similar), if that tree defines one as its true baseline focus. For example, a Barbarian's required 2h axe, once successfully Enchanted (see Enchantment Creates a Magic Weapon, above), becomes a Magic-Type 2h axe that satisfies the Barbarian's Spell Drawn abilities the same way a caster's Stave or orb would, no separate implement needed, so long as its Style, Hand, Range, and Grade & Material still match the Barbarian's own required profile.

Prestige measures how the world perceives a character, shaped by actions, achievements, and failures. It affects NPC interactions, faction standing, titles, and legacy. See pg. 2.

NPCs interact based on Stance, their standing relative to the character, their race, and their faction. Class Trainer Requirements & Fees

A Class Trainer needs to teach the character's Main Class, not the exact Tier, Path, or Branch, any Trainer who teaches that Main Class can teach any Tier, Path, or Branch within it (see Playable Classes, pg. 3). Paths and Branches are Sub-Classes of their Main Class, not separate trees of their own, freely chosen at Level 4 and Level 21 respectively. Separately, certain Slots carry a Prerequisite: every 4th Level starting at Level 3, one Slot, cycling A, B, C, is a Prerequisite for that same Slot and Split at the very next Level (see Slots and Prerequisites, pg. 3). A Trainer must be at least the character's own current Ch. Lvl, this holds whether the pick being learned came from reaching that Ch. Lvl outright or from the midpoint toward the next one, the requirement tracks the character's Level, not which Slot the pick filled. Reaching a Ch. Lvl, or the midpoint toward the next one, that grants a pick only makes it available, the pick still isn't learned until the character finds a qualifying Trainer, in sufficient faction standing, and pays the fee.

Slot RangeSuggested Fee (Gold)
Tier (1-3)5 × Slot Number
Path (4-20)10 × Slot Number
Branch (21-30)20 × Slot Number

Example: A character's 13th pick, a Path Slot, costs a suggested 130 Gold (10 × 13), learned from any Trainer of that Main Class who is at least the character's own current Ch. Lvl. A character's 27th pick, a Branch Slot, costs a suggested 540 Gold (20 × 27), under the same Trainer requirement.

These are suggested baseline fees, not fixed prices. Each Module sets its own actual Class Trainer fees and Trainer availability, the same way Planetary Markets pricing varies by world and region (see pg. 4). A GM may adjust a given Trainer's fee upward for a rare specialist, or downward for a common local one.

Fallen & Risen

These rules are under heavy development. This is currently the full extent of the rule; more will be added over time.

Only classes bound to a Higher Power can fall or rise, Lawful classes may Fall, Evil classes may Rise. Falling or Rising characters whose alignment drifts all the way to Neutral can no longer perform or earn new Abilities from the class tree that is either lawful or evil, and they'll begin to take penalties and tests from their respective higher powers. See pg. 3 for full rules.
