Brief Description of SorC Cards

SorC Cards
In SorC, players use a comprehensive card-based system to track their abilities, equipment, and companions, with cards representing everything from weapons and armor to pets and fellowship members. Each card has its original base stats, conditions and rarity, and is paired with a detailed index card for noting modifications, buffs, curses, and special traits, allowing characters to customize and adapt their gear and allies over time. SorC emphasizes active exploration and discovery, as players seek out new cards and forge relationships with guardians, mounts, and other creatures. The GM manages the distribution of card sets, item states, and modifications, creating a dynamic and evolving gameplay environment where equipment and alliances can change based on player choices and in-game events.

Card Acquisition Rules:
Characters cannot acquire a card higher than their own rank; commoner equals common rarity, and so forth. SorC’s starter set comes with a deck of base common cards, including: armor, weapons, fellowships, items, and their respective index cards to record edited data, such as enhancements and physical modifications.

Armor Cards

Each Armor card represents complete armor sets or individual armor pieces within SorC. Each card has a front side with the image of the armor and any enhancement effects (glowing, fire, etc) players upload to our servers. Base stats are shown on the back of the card and any enhancements are recorded on an armor index card.

The underside also provides detailed information about the specific armor piece, and includes the armor’s type (such as plate, Fur, leather), its source (like gnoll, orc, or mythical), and its base stats, including its defensive score and any effects or special properties. These effects can include resistances, i.e. to elements like fire, unique abilities like stealth or even hindrances like movement.

Players attach these armor cards to index cards with corresponding upgrades or modifications as time goes by, to track enhancements or additional pieces (e.g., boots, padrons or gauntlets). When characters acquire new pieces or upgrades, they can stack them to the default armor card and the piece of the armor being replaced stat is also replaced, so if the boots were listed as +1 to DEFS, and the character wanted to add +2 to DEFS with +3 to speed, the player would omit the boots +1 stat from the index card and add the +2 DEFS and +3 speed, so the new boots make the character faster and gives him 1 more point in DEFS.

This pattern continues throughout the course of the character's life. Some armor sets have set bonuses, so piecing them apart ruins the bonus.

Armor also has to be kept in good condition to function properly as well, and the condition of newly acquired armor is also random. Characters establish experience and wear per combat encounter.
