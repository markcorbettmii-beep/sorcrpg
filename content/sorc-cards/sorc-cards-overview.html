SORC Cards System Explained

SORC Cards help you keep track of everything your character can do, what they own, and who’s helping them in "Slayers of Rings & Crowns." Think of them like Pokémon cards, but instead of creatures, they show abilities, weapons, gear, and friends (companions) your character has.

What Are SORC Cards?

  • SORC Cards are special cards that help you keep track of your character’s abilities, equipment, companions, and more.
  • Each card has information like stats, abilities, upgrades, or changes.
  • If you change something about a card (like adding a buff or curse), you stack a Reference Card underneath it to show the new info, instead of scribbling on the original.
  • The only way to officially acquire SORC Cards is by purchasing a box set or expansion. Lost Common and Uncommon Cards can be downloaded for free using your box set code or ordered for a fee.
  • Some cards include a reference code or QR code for looking up more info/lore online or in books. QR codes must be refreshed if traded.

SORC Card Categories & Subcategories

1. Armament Cards

  • What they are: Cards for weapons and armor your character can use.
  • How they work: Each card shows the weapon/armor’s stats (like power, protection, quality, and rank).
  • Ranks: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Unique, Heroic, Elite, Legendary, Divine (the cooler the rank, the harder it is to get and use).
  • Upgrades: You can upgrade or modify your gear. If you do, use a Reference Card to show the new stats.
  • Restrictions: Some gear can only be used if your character is the right class or has the right proficiency.
  • Condition: Cards track their current state (ex: broken, worn, pristine). Broken gear goes to the boneyard until fixed.
  • Value: Cards may show trade/sale value.
  • Recipes: Craftable Armaments come with a recipe card (instructions and materials). Non-craftable Armaments are discovered in the world.
  • Punch out: Common Armament cards are included as punch out cards in every box set.

2. Item Cards

  • What they are: Cards for consumables (like potions), materials, quest items, and special stuff.
  • How they work: Each card says what it does (like healing, buffs, or quest details) and how many uses are left.
  • Tracker Book: You mark your Item Tracker Book when you use an item, so you don’t lose count.
  • Stacking: Some cards (like potions) represent multiple items—combine them if your character can carry them (use wagons/hirelings/storage if needed).
  • Recipes: Craftable items require a recipe card with instructions and materials. Non-craftable items are discovered.
  • Value/Condition: Cards may show trade value and condition.

3. Special Item Cards

  • What they are: Cards for really rare or unique things—event tickets, artifacts, scrolls, things that change your character’s appearance, etc.
  • How they work: They often have lore, special abilities, or impact the story.
  • Acquisition: Included according to the rank/level of the box set.
  • Reference Code: Card code for more info/lore access.
  • Recipes: Craftable special items come with recipe cards.

4. Companion Cards

  • What they are: Cards for pets, mounts, guardians, angelics, hirelings, and other allies that help you.
  • How they work: Each card shows their stats and abilities. You get three slots for companions (behemoth, large, small).
  • Variants:
    • Pets: Small animals (like mice, birds, bugs).
    • Angelics: Winged beings, sometimes magical or divine.
    • Drafts/Ruminants: Working animals (like cows, sheep, horses).
    • Guardians: Powerful allies you can ride or who fight with you.
    • Hirelings: People you hire to help (bodyguards, map-makers, etc).
    • Temps: Temporary allies (summons, magical creatures).
  • Restrictions: May require race, class, proficiency, or property (like barn for ruminants) to use certain types.
  • Condition: Fellowships have three performance tiers—basic, improved, elite—based on care and rest. If dead or lost, Rare+ can be revived, others are lost forever (boneyard).
  • Punch out: Common and Uncommon companion cards are included as punch outs in box sets.

5. Encounter Cards

These cards represent all the “hosts” you meet—NPCs, monsters, animals, traps, environments, and more. Subcategories include:

  • NPC Encounter Cards: Non-player characters like shopkeepers or quest givers.
  • Boss Encounter Cards: Big, powerful enemies with special abilities.
  • Mob Encounter Cards: Groups of enemies (like a pack of wolves or goblin army). Use Encounter Tokens to show how many are in the group and keep track as you fight them.
  • Companion Encounter Cards: Same as Companion Cards, but used in encounters.
  • Mechanical Encounter Cards: For machines, vehicles, traps, robots. Use Encounter Tokens for multiple parts, traps, or units.
  • Biological Encounter Cards: For living plants, critters, bugs, animals—sometimes dangerous, sometimes helpful.
  • Environmental Encounter Cards: For natural hazards like storms, earthquakes, or terrain features.
  • GM Kit: Each box set includes unique Encounter Cards for the GM.
  • Reference Code: For more details/lore lookup.

6. Lore Cards

  • What they are: Special cards tied to Lore Quests. They’re rare and can open up unique adventures.
  • How they work: Lore Cards can be traded online or with friends. Rank of Lore Cards is dictated by the module level. Each Lore Card may have a QR code for digital access, which must be refreshed and transfers on trade.
  • Function: Some Lore Cards unlock special quests (Lore Quests). PQs are special, elite quests that only drop if you have the card in your box set.
  • GM: The GM holds onto Lore Cards until the party earns them. If the Lore Card’s source is gone, the GM will provide an alternate method for the party to obtain it.
  • Module Rank Limits:
    • Levels 1 - 3: Maximum Lore Card rank is Commoner.
    • Levels 4 - 7: Maximum Lore Card rank is Adventurer.
    • Levels 8 - 14: Maximum Lore Card rank is Hero.
    • Levels 15 - 24: Maximum Lore Card rank is Elite.
    • Levels 25 - 30: Maximum Lore Card rank is Master.
  • Reference Code: For more details/lore lookup.

7. Prize Cards

  • What they are: Random reward cards included in every box set. You can trade them, but their rank matches the box set level.

8. Reference Cards

  • What they are: Small cards you stack under any SORC Card to show upgrades, buffs, curses, or changes.
  • How they work: Keep your main card clean, and you can quickly see all changes at a glance.
  • Acquisition: Downloadable from the website or orderable by mail, along with character sheets and other tracking material.

9. Game Engine Cards

  • Action Cards: Show what moves, skills, abilities, or talents your character can use in combat or other situations.
  • Narrative Event Cards: Used for story events, clever escapes, or interacting creatively with the world.
  • Dilemma Cards: Give you tough choices that affect your character’s reputation, titles, and relationships.
  • Trait Cards: Represent your character’s special traits or actions.
  • Three Action Card types:
    • Ability: Combat, martial, or spell.
    • Talent: Physical or intellectual characteristics characters learn.
    • Skill: Craftsmanship, must have recipe, skill, and at least a makeshift workshop.

Extra Details

  • Ranks: You can only use cards of the same rank as your character’s level/rank.
  • Card Upgrades: If you improve a weapon, armor, or companion, use Reference Cards to record what changed.
  • Trading: All cards can be traded online or with friends, as long as you follow the rules (can’t use cards above your level).
  • Encounter Tokens: Used with Mob, Boss, Mechanical, and Trap cards to keep track of the number of enemies or traps.
  • Boneyard: When companions or gear are lost, broken, or dead, their cards go to the boneyard until you fix or resurrect them.
  • Digital Access: Some cards (especially Lore Cards) allow digital access via QR code tied to the box set. If traded, digital access transfers to the new owner.
  • Authenticity: Only box sets provide official cards, discouraging cheating.

Quick Example

  • If you have a Sword Armament Card (Common rank), and you upgrade it by adding a magic gem, you slip a Reference Card underneath showing “+2 magic damage.” If your sword breaks, it goes to the boneyard and you need to get it fixed before you can use it again.
  • If you meet a group of goblins, the GM puts out a Mob Encounter Card for “Goblin Scouts” and uses Encounter Tokens to show there are six. As you defeat them, remove a token for each one.
  • If you get a Lore Card (like Igor’s Helm), you must complete the Lore Quest to obtain it. If the source is gone, the GM provides an alternate quest or method.

Why Use Cards?

  • Easy to organize: You can see what you have, what your character can do, and who’s with you.
  • Quick reference: Instead of flipping through books, just look at your cards.
  • Customizable: Stack Reference Cards to show upgrades, buffs, or changes without ruining the main card.
  • Trading: Cards can be traded physically or online, letting you swap gear or companions with friends.
  • Authenticity: Only box sets provide official cards, discouraging cheating.

Summary

  • SORC Cards track everything: abilities, gear, allies, upgrades, encounters, quests, and more.
  • Each card’s stats include quality, condition, value, rank, and a reference code.
  • Upgrades/changes use Reference Cards.
  • Recipe cards allow crafting; non-craftables are discovered.
  • Companions, creatures, and encounters all have their own cards with relevant stats and restrictions.
  • Prize and Lore Cards give you special rewards and quests.
  • All cards are physical (punch out) and may have digital access via QR code tied to the box set.
  • Gameplay is streamlined, organized, and always grounded in the physical tabletop experience.
