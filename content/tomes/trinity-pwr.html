The Trinity of Powers

The Higher Powers are the immortal beings upon which divine favor and pacts are built. They are organized into three distinct categories, each encompassing entities of power, will, and influence across the planes of Essentia.

The Three Categories of Higher Powers

1. Gods/Goddesses and Arch Devils/Arch Deviles

The supreme entities of creation and corruption. Gods and Goddesses channel the forces of good, order, and divine right. Arch Devils and Arch Deviles embody the antithesis, chaos, tyranny, and infernal dominion.

2. Deities/Dietess

Lesser entities of focused divine influence. Where Gods command entire domains, Deities and Dietesses hold dominion over singular forces, war, craft, knowledge, or shadow. Their favor is more accessible to mortals willing to pledge themselves.

3. Pantheons/Celestials

Organized societies of powerful beings unified by purpose, philosophy, or kinship. Pantheons represent established hierarchies of divine servants and celestial powers, from the most exalted seraph to the humble angel.

Named Powers and Beings of Zailister

The Zailister map records divine powers and supernatural beings alongside its oceans, islands, and regions. The Trinity book is the home of the complete Higher Power system: Gods, Goddesses, Deities, Dietesses, Demigods, Devils, Arch Devils, Divine Beings, and other Higher Power forms. These classifications are separate from MAC. A divine title describes a Higher Power; a supernatural classification describes the nature of a living being and does not automatically make it divine.

Gods and Goddesses

  • Terrora is the Goddess of Terrora, the land that shares her name and is home to the mythical forest Everblue Bristles. She is a nature power allied with Aurora and protects the Giant Angel Fish.
  • Quintus is the God of the Seas, commanding the waters, currents, and depths toward the Titan Ocean.

Deities and Dietesses

  • Aurora is the Dietess of the Aurora Keys, a nature power allied with Terrora and protector of the Giant Angel Fish.

Every planet of Essentia contains a labyrinth of intricate mazes leading toward a hidden throne room and a secret gate to the infernal planes. These labyrinths are not ordinary dungeons: each is shaped by its planet’s hazards, wildlife, elemental forces, cultures, and buried history.

Planetary Labyrinths and Arch Devils

Each of the thirteen planets guards its own route. At the deepest throne room waits an Arch Devil tied to that planet or realm, protected by a retinue of demons, guards, officers, pets, and minions. An Arch Devil cannot be slain by ordinary means, but can be defeated or compelled to retreat long enough for a party to begin the gateward rites.

The gates are conduits rather than separate hells. There is one infernal destination, reached through many planetary entrances, and every entrance demands its own path through traps, environmental hazards, minion forces, and grand puzzles.

The Gateward Expedition

Reaching a gate is a campaign-scale undertaking. A successful expedition may require a Paladin, Priest, Monk, Cleric, Sorcerer trained across the elemental spectrum, Illusionist, Bard, Primordial, Ranger, and Strategist, with each role solving a different part of the labyrinth’s defense. The old expedition tradition also names Greenlings, Halflings, Humans, Bridge Trolls, Dwarves, Arch Elves, Darklings, Gnomes, and Centaurs among the peoples best suited to the attempt. These are adventure requirements and traditions, not changes to playable-race rules.

Other Holy Planes and Protected Chambers

Beyond the direct gate routes lie other protected planar chambers: places of power, sealed sanctums, and puzzle-locked thresholds watched by infernal lords and their consorts. The source tradition calls these the Other, Holy, Planes; in Trinity they are recorded as contested sacred spaces, not as proof that every chamber is benevolent.

At the heart of the current campaign account, a party reaches the throne-room labyrinth at the core of Zailister. Its route descends through natural bridges, fiery caverns, molten depths, hostile wildlife, exposure, and demonic defenses. Without magic, no living party can survive the deepest descent; even with magic, bringing everyone back is unlikely.

Where a Higher Power Stands

Category says what a Higher Power is. It says nothing about where it stands. Every Higher Power holds a place on the five-point spectrum, and that place is the base Alignment of the Path sworn to it (see Alignment, pg. 2). A Pantheon may be Lawful or Amoral. A single Deity may be Moral or Evil. The three categories cut across the spectrum rather than along it.

Only Lawful and Moral Powers carry the risk of Falling, and only Amoral and Evil Powers carry the risk of Rising. An Ambivalent Path can do neither, since there is no bond to break. That freedom is the trade: no devotion owed, no tests to survive, and no blessing either.

Reaching a Power is not the same as serving one. A Path may be learned from anywhere inside its Spectrum, its base Alignment plus one step in either direction. Its Higher Power pays out only while the Character stands at that base Alignment exactly. Step off it and the blessings, buffs and gifts stop until repentance brings the Character back.

The Avenger tree carries one Power at each of three different places on the spectrum: Aelthora the Righteous, a Goddess, at Lawful; Kendur the Stillborn, a Deity, at Moral; and The Luminae Circle, a Pantheon of Celestials, at Amoral. No single Character reaches all three.

Binding

A Character binds to one Higher Power, and one only, chosen at LVL 4 alongside their first Path and drawn from that Path's own patron. It is a lifetime commitment across every Class Tree the Character ever invests in, not a choice made once per tree. A second Path taken later in another Class grants no second patron, and that Path's own Higher Power bonuses go unclaimed.

Binding grants a Benefit on Abilities drawn from any Path sworn to that Power, and on checks made in its service or in its places. It costs a Drawback on every roll made against that Power's declared foe, against that foe's faithful, its consecrated ground, and its Armaments. A Power will also close individual rival Paths to a Character while the bond holds, whatever their alignment.

Choosing no Higher Power at all is a real option, and the only one that keeps every Path open. An unbound Character forfeits every Higher Power bonus and can never Fall or Rise, but carries no Drawback and no Path is ever closed to them. The full rules are under Higher Power, pg. 2.
