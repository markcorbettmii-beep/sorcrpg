Work While Away

At the conclusion of each session, characters can opt to "work while away," allowing them to potentially gain experience during the interim between sessions.

As long as the Character has the proficiency of the prerequisite, the recipe, and has the material to craft the items, they can do this. While absent, the extent of work a character can perform daily is the remaining actions left at the end of the session, and after the full 8 hours of rest half the number of actions they can usually execute in a day, each day until the next session begins. The number of actions allowed is determined by their endurance attribute (refer to section 0.00x, Vitality for more details on Vitality).

Skill and Talent Progression

  • In Active Play, each successful attempt increases a skill’s progression by 2%. Additionally, a natural 20 roll grants an extra 3% bonus for that attempt (resulting in a total of 5% progression), while a natural 1 causes a 2% setback.
  • In ‘Work While Away’ mode, progression is scaled to 50% efficiency - that is, a successful attempt yields 1% progression (instead of 2%), a natural 20 still gives a total of 3% progression, and a natural 1 results in a 2% setback.

The distinction between talents and skills ensures a diverse range of gameplay actions, encouraging players to employ their characters’ various strengths uniquely.

However, players must consider the endurance component associated with their Vitality attribute, as this governs the number of actions they can perform in a single day. As stated in our Vitality section, each character is allowed a set number of actions per day based on their Endurance score. This limitation requires players to carefully allocate their actions between abilities (combat), talents, and skills, urging them to choose strategically how their characters spend their action through various interactive scenarios.

Skill Mechanics and Action Allocation

In SorC, characters employ dice rolls, typically ranging from d4 to d20, to attempt tasks involved in both talents and skills. These rolls are adjusted by relevant modifiers based on race, attributes, or applicable conditions. The objective is to meet or exceed a Difficulty Score (DIFS) set by the Game Master, determining the success of the action.

Characters begin discovering these recipes at the Apprentice level, can purchase them throughout their career, or create new ones starting at the Artisan level. Crafted recipes are aligned with the character's current tier, offering benefits relevant to their expertise, whether at the Artisan, Master, or Grandmaster level.

The number of actions a character can perform each day is governed by their Endurance score, linked to their Vitality attribute. This limitation directs players to allocate their actions strategically among abilities (combat), talents, and skills, encouraging thoughtful engagement with the game's challenges.
