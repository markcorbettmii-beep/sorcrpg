GM Codex

Chapter IV: Combat & Death

This chapter is still mostly unwritten - Death Penalty, Resurrection, Revitalize, and Rejuvenation are covered in Basic Rules Pg. 5 for now, with their full GM-facing procedures still to be ported here. MAC Creation is where this chapter starts.

MAC Creation

MAC (Monsters, Animals, Creatures/Critters - see MAC, Basic Rules Pg. 6) across Essentia are built into each module dependent on the module's locations. Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs (see Tomes of Essentia, Basic Rules Appendix) collects summarized versions of creatures across Essentia's 13 worlds, but those are previews, not final stat blocks - the complete, playable version of any creature featured there appears within whichever module actually uses it. A GM may use a summarized MAC from Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs directly, but must change its name first, since the eventual official version in a module may differ. MAC Creation below is for when a GM wants to build a custom MAC beyond what a module or the bestiary already provides.

A MAC is not a player character, and none of the restrictions Character Simulation (GM Codex Ch. II) places on PCs apply to one. A GM creating their own MAC simulates its Growth, Character Level, and Rank all directly and freely - there is no RP to earn, no years to live through, and no gate to satisfy. A custom MAC simply is whatever the encounter needs it to be.

Reskinning a Summarized MAC into Variants

A single summarized MAC class from Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs isn't locked to one role - it's a starting point a GM can branch into as many variants as an encounter needs, each renamed and adjusted in Rank, Character Level equivalent, and Growth (Age) equivalent to fit. A Skeleton Archer, for example, could branch into a Skeleton Guardian (higher Toughness, melee-focused), a Skeleton Soldier (a standard Mob Encounter Card entry, ranked and leveled to match the party, with a squad of them represented by Encounter Tokens - "mob tokens" - on that single card rather than a separate card per skeleton, see Encounter Tokens, Basic Rules Pg. 4), a Skeleton Shaman (Rank and Abilities shifted toward spellcasting), or a Skeleton King (a Boss Encounter Card - see Mob, Boss and Mechanical Cards, Basic Rules Pg. 4 - built at a much higher Rank as the encounter's centerpiece, with no Tokens since a Boss is singular). There's no fixed list of what a class can branch into - use imagination, and reference Player Class Abilities or other MAC Abilities and Spells for inspiration on what a variant's kit should look like.

What Gets Assigned

  • Rank: The primary lever. A MAC's Rank (Common through Divine - see Character Rank vs. Encounter Rank, GM Codex Ch. II) sets its place in every Rank-scaled system already published: Item Drop Rates by Rank and Rank Differential - Loot Scaling for what it carries and how its rewards scale against the party's own Rank, and Base Hit Rate (Common Rank, No Modifiers) and Armor Common Base AS and Rank Bonuses for how it fights and defends (all Basic Rules Appendix).
  • Character Level equivalent: Freely assigned to whatever Attribute Score and TST spread the encounter calls for. Unlike Character Simulation for PCs, there is no Attribute Repository to roll through level by level - a GM or module author simply writes the final Attribute Scores and TST a MAC needs.
  • Growth (Age) equivalent: Freely assigned as flavor and Vitality scaling - an ancient dragon and a young one can share a Rank while differing in raw Vitality, entirely at the GM's discretion. Unlike a PC, a MAC has no 40-year Retirement window and no Starting Age formula to anchor to - its age can be as young as a freshly hatched wyrmling or effectively infinite (ageless elementals, undying spirits, creatures with no natural death at all). Age and Rank are independent for MAC: a high Rank doesn't imply old age, and a low Rank doesn't imply young age.

Naming and Abilities

NPCs and MAC ranked Heroic and above carry fictional names and possess special Abilities and gear (see Rank, GM Codex Ch. II) - Common through Unique MAC are typically unnamed and use their base stat block as-is.

MAC Simulation Examples

Example A: A Young MAC - Wyrmling

A freshly hatched dragon for a starter module. Rank: Uncommon. Character Level equivalent: 3 - a handful of Attribute Scores and TST, nothing developed. Growth (Age) equivalent: 2 years old. Low Rank, low Level equivalent, and young age all point the same direction here, but that's a choice, not a rule - see Example C below.

Example B: An Ancient MAC - Elder Wyrm

The same dragon, centuries later, for an endgame module. Rank: Legendary. Character Level equivalent: 28 - a fully developed Attribute/TST spread and a deep Ability list, matching Legendary's naming and special-Ability rule above. Growth (Age) equivalent: effectively infinite - centuries or millennia old, with no upper bound the way a PC's 40-year Retirement window caps things. A MAC's age never forces anything like Retirement; it's as old as the story needs it to be.

Example C: A Timeless MAC - Elemental Spirit

Rank: Rare. Character Level equivalent: 12. Growth (Age) equivalent: ageless - this MAC was never born and cannot die of old age, so "Growth (Age)" isn't a number at all here, just narrative flavor (timeless, eternal). This is the case that breaks any assumption that Rank and age track together: a Rare-rank spirit isn't "young" just because its Rank sits mid-ladder, and it isn't "ancient" just because it's ageless.

To Do - MAC Vitality/Stat Budget by Rank: a published baseline for how much Life/Mana/Stamina/Endurance and total Attribute Score a MAC should carry at each Rank tier, so module authors have a consistent starting point instead of inventing numbers per encounter. This is the biggest remaining gap in MAC Creation - every other piece above already has a home in an existing table.
