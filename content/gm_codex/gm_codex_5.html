GM Codex

Chapter V: Armaments & Economy

Armaments, maintenance, and the broader economy are described from the player side across Basic Rules pg. 4, gear proficiency, condition and maintenance fees, field repair, Enhancements, Sundering, and Set Bonuses. That section points here, to the GM Codex, several separate times for the pricing tables and success-rate procedures a GM actually needs at the table. Those gaps are this chapter.

Armor Grades, including Half Grades: Basic Rules pg. 4 and Appendix cover the full ten-Grade ladder (Delicate, Light, Half Medium, Medium, Half Heavy, Heavy, Half Dense, Dense, Half Composite, Composite) with Base AS, Value, Vulnerability/Resistance, Movement, and Weight for every Grade including the four Half Grades. A Half Grade sits between a full Grade and the full Grade below it, blending both, use the Basic Rules tables directly, nothing GM-exclusive is layered on top of them yet.

Still To Do, Chapter V

To Do, Class Trainer Availability by Module: Basic Rules Appendix (Class Trainer Requirements & Fees) sets a Trainer's Level requirement to the character's own current Ch. Lvl (any Trainer of the right Main Class qualifies, regardless of Tier/Path/Branch) and suggested Gold fees by Slot range, but leaves actual Trainer fees and availability to be set per Module, the same way Planetary Markets pricing varies by world and region. This still needs GM-facing guidance for where qualifying Trainers are placed on the map by Module, how faction stance requirements are adjudicated at the table, and how a GM should adjust the baseline fee for a rare specialist versus a common local Trainer.
To Do, Armorer Field Repair: Basic Rules pg. 4 establishes that an Armorer can field-repair Armaments before a long rest at a discount, one daily action per repair, but the actual pricing and daily action limits aren't written yet. This needs a table scaling repair cost by Armament rank/condition, and how many field repairs an Armorer can realistically perform per day.
To Do, Guardsmith Scrolls: Basic Rules pg. 4 establishes Guardsmith Scrolls guarantee an Excellent-condition repair and boost Enhancement odds, gated behind Merchant Stance with the blacksmith. Pricing by stance level and the Enhancement odds boost itself still need numbers.
To Do, Sundering: Basic Rules pg. 4 establishes Sundering as an Armorer skill performed only on Excellent-condition gear, extracting materials at or above the Armament's own rank with a chance at exclusive materials. Success rates and the material drop tables still need to be written.
To Do, Companion Mount Checks: Basic Rules pg. 4 establishes that mounting a significantly larger Companion requires a Strength check scaled to the size gap between rider and mount, but the actual success/failure thresholds by size-gap tier aren't published yet.
To Do, Cuirass Set Enhancement: Basic Rules Appendix establishes that only a set's Cuirass may receive a set-level Enhancement once Heroic rank and above, but the enhancement rules themselves, what it costs, what it does, and how it interacts with a broken set, aren't written yet.
To Do, Running a Shop/Market: Not yet promised explicitly player-side, but a genuine gap, GM-facing guidance for stocking a vendor by planet/region and Rank band, restock timing, and how Planetary Markets pricing (Basic Rules pg. 4) actually gets set at the table.
To Do, Regional Price Variance and Trade: Projected from standard tabletop economy design (trade goods and regional scarcity are common concerns across the genre), a good on one world isn't priced the same everywhere. Basic Rules pg. 4 already establishes that item availability varies by planet and geography, this chapter needs the actual price-modifier guidance for importing goods across worlds, hemispheres, and Zailister's own regions.
To Do, Managing Player Wealth Over a Campaign: A standard GM-book topic (economy pacing, avoiding runaway Gold accumulation or a starved party), this chapter needs guidance for a GM tuning loot payouts, maintenance fees, and Kit/Guardsmith Scroll costs against a party's Rank and Ch. Lvl so the economy stays meaningful at every stage of a campaign, not just the start.
