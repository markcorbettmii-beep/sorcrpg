Chapter IV: Combat & Death

This chapter is still mostly unwritten, Death Penalty, Resurrection, Revitalize, and Rejuvenation are covered in Basic Rules pg. 5 for now, with their full GM-facing procedures still to be ported here. MAC Creation is where this chapter starts.

MAC Creation

MAC (Monsters, Animals, Creatures/Critters, see MAC, Basic Rules pg. 7) across Essentia are built into each module dependent on the module's settings. Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs (see Tomes of Essentia, library.html) holds summarized versions of thousands of MAC across Essentia's 13 worlds, but those are previews, not final stat blocks, the complete, playable version of any creature featured there appears within whichever module actually uses it.

GMs may use a summarized MAC from Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs directly, but must change their names since the eventual official version in a module may differ. MAC Creation below is for when a GM wants to build a custom MAC beyond what a module or the bestiary already provides. GMs can also create and simulate their own MAC (see below) as well.

A MAC is not a player character, and none of the restrictions Character Simulation (GM Codex Ch. II) places on PCs apply to one, a PC's Rank can never be simulated, but a MAC's Rank may be. A GM creating their own MAC simulates its Rank directly and freely, there is no RP to earn, no years to live through, and no gate to satisfy, but Character Level equivalent and Growth (Age) still have to line up with that Rank rather than being picked independently of it. Character Level equivalent is derived straight from Rank; Growth (Age) is chosen freely but must be old enough to plausibly justify the Rank; and Life comes from that Growth (Age), not from Rank at all, see Calculating MAC Stats, below, for the full formula.

Reskinning a Summarized MAC into Variants

A single summarized MAC class from Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs isn't locked to one role, it's a starting point a GM can branch into as many variants as an encounter needs, each renamed and adjusted in Rank, Character Level equivalent, and Growth (Age) equivalent to fit. A Skeleton Archer, for example, could branch into a Skeleton Guardian (higher Toughness, melee-focused), a Skeleton Soldier (a standard Mob Encounter Card entry, ranked and leveled to match the party, with a squad of them represented by Encounter Tokens, "mob tokens", on that single card rather than a separate card per skeleton, see Encounter Tokens, Basic Rules pg. 4), a Skeleton Shaman (Rank and Abilities shifted toward spellcasting), or a Skeleton King (a Boss Encounter Card, see Mob, Boss and Mechanical Cards, Basic Rules pg. 4, built at a much higher Rank as the encounter's centerpiece, with no Tokens since a Boss is singular). There's no fixed list of what a class can branch into, use imagination, and reference Player Class Abilities or other MAC Abilities for inspiration on what a variant's kit should look like.

What Gets Assigned

  • Rank: The primary lever. A MAC's Rank (Common through Divine, see Character Rank vs. Encounter Rank, GM Codex Ch. II) sets its place in every Rank-scaled system already published: Item Drop Rates by Rank and Rank Differential, Loot Scaling for what it carries and how its rewards scale against the party's own Rank, and Base Hit Rate (Common Rank, No Modifiers) and Armor Common Base AS and Rank Bonuses for how it fights and defends (all Basic Rules pg. 6). Rank also drives Hit and PROTS directly, see Calculating MAC Stats, below.
  • Character Level equivalent: Not picked independently, read straight off the Character Rank vs. Encounter Rank table (GM Codex Ch. II) using whatever Level Prerequisite belongs to the paired PC Rank. A Rare MAC's Level equivalent is always 9, the same as a Commoner; a Divine MAC's is always 30, the same as a Legend. Unlike Character Simulation for PCs, there is no Attribute Repository to roll through level by level, a GM or module author simply writes the final Attribute Scores and TST that Level equivalent implies.
  • Growth (Age): A real stat field, written on the card as "Growth (Age): X" where X is the MAC's actual age in years. Chosen freely by the GM, but it must be old enough to plausibly justify the MAC's Rank, a freshly hatched Divine MAC isn't valid. Unlike a PC, a MAC has no 40-year Retirement window and no Starting Age formula to anchor to, its age can be as young as a freshly hatched wyrmling or effectively infinite (ageless elementals, undying spirits, creatures with no natural death at all), whichever the Rank and the story call for. Growth (Age) is also what determines Life, see Calculating MAC Stats, below.

Calculating MAC Stats

Hit and PROTS scale directly off Rank; Life scales off Growth (Age) instead. This was derived directly from the two real MAC on file, the Common Skeleton Soldier (Mob Encounter Card) and the Divine Chimassu (Boss Encounter Card, both Basic Rules pg. 4), and the formula below holds exactly against both.

Rank Tier numbers each Rank, Common through Divine, 0 through 7:

  • Hit = 8 + (6 × Rank Tier). Common lands at +8, the Skeleton Soldier's Main Hit, exactly. Divine lands at +50, Chimassu's base Hit on every attack before reach/dive modifiers, exactly.
  • PROTS = 26 + (9 × Rank Tier). Common lands at 26, the Skeleton Soldier's PROTS, exactly. Divine lands at 89, Chimassu's PROTS, exactly.
  • Character Level equivalent, see What Gets Assigned, above: 1, 5, 9, 14, 18, 23, 26, and 30 for Common through Divine.
  • Damage dice scale the same direction as Hit and PROTS, the Skeleton Soldier's Main attack swings 1d6, Chimassu's core attacks swing 6d6. There's no exact formula for dice yet since only these two end anchors are on file; add roughly a die per Rank tier and match the Hit/PROTS curve until more MAC are catalogued.

Naming and Abilities

NPCs and MAC ranked Heroic and above carry fictional names and possess special Abilities and gear (see Rank, GM Codex Ch. II), Common through Unique MAC are typically unnamed and use their base stat block as-is.

MAC Simulation Examples

Example A: A Mob, Rank Rare, Growth (Age) 27

Rank Tier 2. Character Level equivalent: 9 (Rare pairs to Commoner, whose Level Prerequisite is 9). Growth (Age) 27 falls in the Adult stage (16-100), so Life: 35. PROTS: 26 + (9 × 2) = 44. Main Hit: +20 (8 + 6×2), DMG 2d6. Off Hit: +10, DMG 1d6. Rare sits below the Heroic naming threshold (see Naming and Abilities, above), so this MAC stays unnamed with a base stat block, a Mob Encounter Card with Encounter Tokens ("mob tokens"), the same shape as the Skeleton Soldier.

Example B: A Boss, Rank Divine, Growth (Age) 12,574

Rank Tier 7. Character Level equivalent: 30 (Divine pairs to Legend, whose Level Prerequisite is 30). Growth (Age) 12,574 falls in the Ancient stage (5,001-20,000), so Life: 220. PROTS: 26 + (9 × 7) = 89. Main Hit: +50 (8 + 6×7), DMG 6d6. Off Hit: +25, DMG 3d6. Divine clears the Heroic naming threshold by a wide margin, so this Boss gets a fictional name and a full kit of special Abilities befitting its Rank, built fresh for this MAC rather than reused from Chimassu, even though both are Divine.
