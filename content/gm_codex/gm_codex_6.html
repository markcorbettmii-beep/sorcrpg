GM Codex

Chapter VI: Kits

Talent Tree Kits, Profession tool kits, and the proficiency tiers they run on (Apprentice through Grandmaster) are described from the player side in Basic Rules pg. 2, alongside a starting price table covering Standard and Advanced (Omnè import) kits. Basic Rules Appendix flags that Grandmaster sits near end-game for proficiency and points here for what comes after it. This chapter is where that, and the rest of the GM-facing Kit procedures, belong.

Still To Do, Chapter VI

To Do, Beyond Grandmaster: Basic Rules Appendix explicitly defers "expanded proficiency rules and Character Level (Ch. Lvl) thresholds beyond Grandmaster" to the GM Codex. This chapter needs to define what, if anything, exists past Grandmaster for a Talent Tree or Profession, is it a hard ceiling, or does something like a Legendary tier exist, and if so, what it unlocks.
To Do, Custom/Homebrew Kit Design: Basic Rules pg. 2's Kit price table only lists Standard and Advanced (Omnè import) tiers for the kits already named. This chapter needs guidance for a GM or module author pricing and statting a brand-new Kit that isn't on that list, what a Kit's bonus should scale to at each proficiency tier, and how Kit recipes get introduced into a module.
To Do, Kit Crafting Resolution at the Table: Basic Rules pg. 2 covers how a Crafting Resolution check determines a crafted item's bonus in general terms, this chapter needs the GM-facing walkthrough for running that check specifically for Kit crafting, DIFS-setting guidance by recipe tier, and how a failed craft is handled (lost materials, wasted action, or something gentler).
To Do, Makeshift vs. Genuine Kit Adjudication: Basic Rules pg. 2 establishes that a makeshift substitute lets a character attempt a task without the real Kit but grants no bonus. This chapter needs GM guidance on what counts as a valid makeshift substitute case by case, and when a GM should rule an attempt isn't possible at all without the genuine Kit.
To Do, Downtime Crafting Time Investment: Projected from downtime crafting systems common across the tabletop genre, where crafting ties to time spent rather than just a single roll, this chapter needs guidance for how long a Kit-based craft actually takes by proficiency tier and recipe complexity, and how that time is tracked against in-game downtime versus a single Crafting Resolution check.
To Do, Kit Loss, Damage, and Theft: A standard consideration in crafting-focused GM guides, this chapter needs rules for what happens when a character's Kit is lost, destroyed, or stolen mid-campaign, whether a makeshift penalty applies immediately or there's a grace period, and how quickly a replacement can be bought or crafted.
