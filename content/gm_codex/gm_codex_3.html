Chapter II: Character Progression

Basic Rules Pg. 4 introduces Growth, Character Level, and Rank as a summary. This chapter is the full, unabridged version of all three - the tables, schedules, and procedures a GM needs to run progression at the table.

Growth Development

Growth is a character's maturity and aging - measured and recorded in Eons of Adoria (EA), or "years."

A character ages every year they're played, but Growth itself doesn't arrive every year - the wait grows each time. The first Growth takes 1 year. The second takes 2 more years after that. The third takes 3 more after that, and so on - each Growth requiring one additional year over the last (see How a Year Passes, below, for what counts toward that wait).

Every Growth grants Vitality (Life, Mana, Stamina, Endurance) increases, and the player chooses one Nature Drawn or Spell Drawn Ability (see Nature Drawn and Spell Drawn Abilities, Basic Rules Pg. 6) of various trees.

Vitality gains and Drawn Ability picks land on the same schedule - every Growth, on the same escalating years above - but are tracked as two separate charts below.

A character's age always starts at their race's Starting Age and climbs every year they're played, whether or not a Growth lands that year - they progress in Vitality and Drawn Ability picks only when a Growth actually arrives, as shown in the tables above.

Various races have different starting ages within their 40 year retirement limit (see each race's card, Basic Rules Pg. 2) and the Lifespan, Starting Age, and Retirement Age table in the Basic Rules Appendix for every race's numbers.

Retirement arrives automatically at year 40, the same window for every race no matter how differently their story started (see Retirement, Basic Rules Pg. 4) or their current rank. Because Growth's wait grows every time, Growth 8 (36 cumulative years) is about as far as any character will get before that window closes - Growth 9 and beyond exist for reference and extended-play campaigns, but sit past what a standard 40-year career reaches.

How a Year Passes

A single year is 1 Eon of Adoria - 360 days (see Time, Basic Rules Pg. 4) - built from the same units as everything else on the clock: the 6-second Turn and the Round (every character's Turn taken once, see Turns, Rounds and Time, Basic Rules Pg. 5) for combat, Short Rests (6 hours) and Long Rests (8 hours) for recovery (see Rest, Basic Rules Pg. 5), and full days for travel and downtime (see More on Downtime, Basic Rules Pg. 5). Offtime counts exactly as much as active adventuring does. The GM adds all of it together as it happens, in days, and once that running total reaches 360, the character ages 1 year. Whether that year also brings a Growth depends on how many years the current wait requires - see the escalating schedule above.

Character Level

Character Level (Ch. Lvl) is the faster track, and it's where a build actually takes shape. Every Ch. Lvl, the player rolls a fresh Attribute Repository (see Attribute Development, Basic Rules Pg. 5) to spend REM Points on Attribute Scores and Talents, Skills, and Traits (TST), and is granted a new Ability or Spell pick from their class tree. A new character reaches Ch. Lvl 1 almost immediately - often before the first conversation with an NPC wraps up - so the point-spending system is in the player's hands from the very start. Table talk will often shorten this to just "Level," but it always means Ch. Lvl unless Growth is specified.

At the table's preference, campaigns may use milestone leveling, where GMs announce XP based on story events that guide advancement.

Character Level XP Progression

Ch. Lvl 1 through 10 use the same early-game pace as a familiar D&D-style reference table, unscaled - Ch. Lvl 1 costs nothing (a new character starts there immediately), and the next several levels stay cheap to keep the early game fast and empowering. From Ch. Lvl 11 onward, the cost of each level compounds roughly 20% over the last, so the climb steepens noticeably through the back half of the tree. Ch. Lvl 30 - the point at which a class tree's Ability/Spell picks run out entirely (see When the Class Tree Runs Out, Basic Rules Pg. 4) - sits at roughly 2.58 million cumulative XP.

This table only sets the total XP needed to reach each Ch. Lvl - it says nothing about how quickly a character actually gets there. That depends entirely on how much XP a module or GM awards per achievement, encounter, or discovery, the same way Rank Point Progression (above) only sets RP totals while actual pacing depends on RP Earning from Modules. XP Progression Examples

The specific award values below are illustrative, not a published baseline - see Module XP Award Guidelines below. They exist to show how award sizes need to scale alongside the XP Progression table above, not to set the numbers in stone.

Example A: Party Beginning a Ch. Lvl 1-5 Module Campaign

A party of Ch. Lvl 1 characters begins a Ch. Lvl 1-5 module. Over several sessions, they:

  • Defeat the module's main mini-boss encounter: 800 XP
  • Discover a hidden dungeon area: 400 XP
  • Complete three major quests: 1,200 XP each (3,600 XP total)
  • Survive a dangerous ambush encounter: 500 XP

Total: 5,300 XP accumulated. The party continues and completes a secondary objective (1,200 XP), reaching 6,500 XP total - exactly the threshold for Ch. Lvl 5.

Example B: Party Continuing into a Ch. Lvl 15-20 Module Campaign

A party that has reached Ch. Lvl 15 (161,300 cumulative XP) continues into a module built for that band. Higher-level modules need to award proportionally larger XP per achievement to keep pace with the steeper curve:

  • Defeat a major regional threat: 60,000 XP
  • Complete the module's central quest chain: 90,000 XP total across multiple quests
  • Discover a hidden lore vault: 30,000 XP
  • Survive a climactic siege encounter: 65,000 XP

Total: 245,000 XP gained, carrying the party from 161,300 to 406,300 - just short of the 406,700 needed for Ch. Lvl 20, closed out by any smaller side objective. Compare the award sizes here to Example A: they're roughly 50-75 times larger, tracking the same growth the XP Progression table itself shows between these two bands.

Rank

Rank is measured by Rank Points (RP) and reflects a hero's reputation, achievements and more. As a character gains RP through exceptional actions and exploration, Rank advances in this ascending order: Pauper, Peasant, Commoner, Adventurer, Hero, Uber, Elite, and Legend. Hostile Creatures and NPCs have Ranks too, and group challenges are balanced by averaging Ranks across the group, for instance a pauper matches to a Common NPC.

NPCs and MAC each carry a Rank that reflects their power and determines the challenge and rewards of an Encounter. Their ranks in ascending order are: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Unique, Heroic, Elite, Legendary, and Divine. Defeating foes of similar Rank awards standard Experience Points (XP), overcoming stronger opponents yields greater rewards, and trivial Encounters grant little or no XP. NPCs ranked Heroic and above carry fictional names and possess special Abilities and gear.

Character Rank advances as a character accumulates Rank Points through exceptional actions, exploration, and achievements, rising through: Pauper, Peasant, Commoner, Adventurer, Hero, Uber, Elite, and Legend.

Characters cannot use items that are of a rank above their own.

Rank is a measure of a character's overall reputation and power, determined by accumulating Rank Points (RP) from achievements, exceptional actions, and exploration. RP is awarded for significant accomplishments, and can be lost if reputation is diminished. Rank governs access to certain Abilities, quests, and items, and is used by the GM to determine the difficulty and rewards of Encounters, as well as to balance challenges between individual characters or groups.

Rank Point Progression

Characters advance through ranks by accumulating Rank Points from module-specific achievements, exceptional actions, and exploration. The amount of RP granted for each accomplishment is determined by the module being played. The following table shows the total RP needed to reach each rank:

Rank Level Prerequisites

Advancing through Rank tiers requires two prerequisites, met independently of each other and of RP: a minimum Ch. Lvl, and a minimum Growth (Beginning Age + Years) - a character's own chosen Beginning Age (see Age, Basic Rules Pg. 2) plus a fixed number of years for that tier. The years-added side of that formula reuses the same numbers Growth's own escalating schedule is built on (see Growth Development, above), but this gate grants none of Growth's Vitality increases or Drawn Ability picks - those stay on Growth's own separate track regardless of Rank. Both gates work purely as thresholds: reaching them unlocks a rank, they do not grant it. Because a character's Beginning Age is itself chosen from a 5-year range at creation, the actual age required at every rank is a range too, not a single number - see the worked examples below the table.

Pauper's Growth (Beginning Age + Years) requirement is met the instant a character begins - a freshly created character's current age already equals their own Beginning Age. A character cannot advance to a higher rank until they have reached the corresponding minimum Ch. A character who reaches it is, in the fiction of the world, already spoken of as one. See Retirement, Basic Rules Pg. 4, for what becomes of a character's legend once the player is done telling their story.

Rank Requirement

Rank gates access on two fronts - challenges and usage.

Challenges: A character must be of the same Rank as a monster or challenge to view it, find it, or accept it. Monsters, Encounters, Lore Quests, and Bosses ranked above a character's Rank stay hidden and inaccessible until Rank catches up. This does not lock a party out of the module they're actively playing - a module's own standard content remains accessible to characters working toward that module's Rank through it (see RP Earning from Modules below), since RP is earned by engaging that very content. The restriction bites hardest reaching ahead - into future modules, expansions, or Rank-locked Lore Quests and Bosses beyond what the current module offers.

To Do - Rank Requirement (Challenges) Adjudication: the rule above establishes that content above a character's Rank stays hidden and inaccessible - still needed: how a GM actually adjudicates "hidden" at the table - does the party get a vague rumor of it, nothing at all, a locked map marker, or something else? Needs a concrete GM-facing procedure before this rule is fully usable in play.

Usage: Characters must reach the rank of any item, armament, or equipment to equip or use it. Rank requirements apply to all items without exception. Acquiring or possessing an item does not grant Rank Points - items are restricted to characters who have already earned the corresponding rank through module-specific achievements, exploration, and exceptional actions. A character may still carry, store, or gift an item above their own Rank, provided they have sufficient Strength or storage capacity for it (see Encumbrance, Basic Rules Pg. 5) - they simply cannot equip or activate it until their Rank matches.

RP Earning from Modules

Each module records specific RP values for achievements, tasks, and discoveries based on their difficulty. Characters must meet both the Ch. Lvl and Growth (Beginning Age + Years) requirements for each rank before they can advance to that rank (see Rank Level Prerequisites above). Characters may enter and explore a module above their own Rank, but any monster, challenge, quest, or Encounter within it that exceeds their Rank stays hidden and inaccessible until they catch up - see Rank Requirement above. Items and Companions found within the module will be of that Rank; characters cannot use items or Companions above their current Rank, regardless of their Ch. Lvl or age, though they may carry, store, or gift what they can't yet use. For information on how characters earn Growth and Character Levels, refer to Growth Development and Character Level above.

GMs may simulate a character's Ch. Lvl but cannot simulate Ranks, Growth, or age. Simulating Ranks is not recommended and breaks the intended progression system.

Rank and Death

Rank is only capped when Ch. Lvl is lost through the Death Penalty (see Basic Rules Pg. 5) - age is never lost to death, so the Growth (Beginning Age + Years) gate is unaffected. A character's Rank can never exceed what their current Ch. Lvl permits, per Rank Level Prerequisites above - so a Ch. Lvl lost to death may cap Rank at a lower tier until the Level is regained. Rank Points already earned are never lost or spent down; they stay banked in full, and Rank re-establishes automatically the moment the character's Ch. Lvl reaches that Rank's prerequisite again.

Companions and Rank

Gaining a Companion does not grant RP. However, characters can earn RP by discovering Companions during exploration. Characters must be at the Rank of any Companion to have it follow them. Companions found in a module will not exceed that module's Rank. Lower-ranked Companions are not available in higher-ranked modules, as they are presumed to have been acquired in earlier, lower-ranked modules.

Rank and Armaments in Higher-Ranked Modules

Characters entering a higher-ranked module with a lower personal Rank may find and carry armaments and items of higher Ranks (within their encumbrance limits), but cannot equip or use them until they reach the matching Rank. This allows characters to discover valuable gear early and prepare for future advancement.

Rank Progression Examples

Example A: Party Beginning a Character Level 1-5 Module Campaign

A party of Ch. Lvl 1 characters (Pauper rank) begins a Ch. Lvl 1-5 module with Common-rank items. Over several sessions, they:

  • Defeat the module's main mini-boss encounter: 150 RP
  • Discover a hidden dungeon area: 75 RP
  • Complete three major quests: 100 RP each (300 RP total)
  • Discover a Companion during exploration: 100 RP

Total: 625 RP accumulated. The party continues and defeats a secondary objective (75 RP), reaching 700 RP total. Once they also reach Ch. Lvl 5, they advance to Peasant rank and can now equip Peasant-rank items found in later modules.

Example B: Simulated Character Level 6 Character Beginning a Character Level 5-8 Module Campaign

A GM simulates a Ch. Lvl 6 character to begin a Ch. Lvl 5-8 module campaign. The character is Ch. Lvl 6 but still Pauper rank because they haven't accumulated the 700 RP needed to reach Peasant rank yet.

The module contains Peasant-rank (Uncommon) items. The Pauper character discovers:

  • A Peasant-rank longsword (Uncommon rank)
  • A Peasant-rank leather armor set (Uncommon rank)
  • A Peasant-rank Dog Companion (Uncommon rank)

The character can carry both armaments within encumbrance limits, but cannot equip or use either until reaching Peasant rank (which requires Ch. Lvl 5-already met-and 700 RP). They can discover the Dog Companion and earn RP toward Peasant rank, but the Companion will not follow them until they reach Peasant rank. They must rely on gear they brought or use Pauper-rank items if available in the module.

Example C: Simulated Ch. Lvl 15 Character Skipping Ahead into a Higher-Ranked Module

A GM simulates a Ch. Lvl 15 character so a new player can jump straight into a Ch. Lvl 15-20 module without playing the earlier ones. The character is still Peasant rank - simulating Ch. Lvl never simulates RP, and this player hasn't earned any yet.

The module contains Rare-rank items and rewards, matching its higher Character Level band. The Peasant character discovers:

  • A Rare-rank greatsword
  • A Rare-rank suit of plate armor
  • A Rare-rank Griffon Companion

The character can carry the greatsword and armor within encumbrance limits, but cannot equip or use either until reaching Commoner rank. Their simulated Ch. Lvl 15 already clears Commoner's Ch. Lvl 9 requirement, so the 1,900 RP is the only gate left standing between them and using what they've found. They can discover the Griffon and earn RP toward it, but it will not follow them until they reach Commoner rank.

Because this character skipped every earlier module, they never picked up the Common- and Uncommon-rank starting gear a Pauper or Peasant character normally accumulates along the way - they own nothing they can actually equip yet. The GM directs the player to the downloadable PDF version's back-catalog to acquire Peasant-rank-appropriate starting gear matching what those earlier modules would have provided, so the character isn't left empty-handed at their actual earned Rank while surrounded by gear several tiers above what they can use.

Character Rank vs. Encounter Rank

Character ranks are named differently from armament and encounter ranks for roleplay purposes - a character's rank as a hero reflects their reputation and story, while the world uses consistent terms for threats and gear. They match directly: each character rank corresponds to the encounter and armament rank of the same tier and color.

Characters must match the rank of any armament or item to use it, plus any additional prerequisites listed on the item itself (such as minimum Trait scores or class requirements).
