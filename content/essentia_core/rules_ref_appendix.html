Ranks

Character ranks reflect a hero's reputation and power. NPC and MAC ranks reflect the challenge and tier of any entity encountered. Each tier corresponds directly, a Pauper faces Common-rank threats, a Legend faces Divine. A character must be of a monster or challenge's Rank to engage it, reaching ahead into higher-ranked content isn't hidden, exactly, but blocks off as an obstacle (road guards, sealed gates, terrain too dangerous to cross) until Rank catches up, and none of its items or Companions can be found through exploration either. Characters cannot equip or use items ranked above their own, though they may still carry, store, or gift ones they've legitimately acquired some other way (gifted, traded, inherited). See pg. 4 for full rank rules. A character who retires at Legend rank keeps that status in the world's history, see Retirement, pg. 4.

Characters

The Seven Attributes

Attributes begin at 0 at creation, except where a race's Innate Trait Adjustments require a higher Starting Attribute Floor (see each race's entry) so no Trait exceeds its aligned Attribute. Hard cap: 50. Max 2 score per Attribute per LVL. See pg. 2.

Artistry (ART)

Creativity, performance, and articulation expressed through precise dexterity, hand-eye coordination, body fluidity, and craftsmanship; encompassing charisma, tactical deception, and the mastery of fine motor skills. Aligns with Dexterity, Deceit, Charisma, Craftsmanship, and Calibration traits.

Fortitude (FRT)

Physical and mental resilience, resistance to ongoing effects such as poison, disease, bleeding, and damage over time spells, as well as resistance to fear, intimidation, mental control, and elemental damage. Governs Wellness recovery and decline. Aligns with Vigor, Health, Toughness, Constitution, and Willpower traits.

Intellect (INT)

Mental acuity, spellcasting power, reasoning, and resistance to mental effects. Aligns with Wit, Focus, Acuity, Courage, and Warded traits.

Physique (PHY)

Physical strength, speed, agility, and toughness. Aligns with Agility, Strength, Grit, Defense, and Atavism traits.

Vigilance (VIG)

Alertness, watchfulness, and awareness. Aligns with Intuition, Perception, and Focus traits.

Wisdom (WIS)

Knowledge, intuition, and insight. Aligns with Piety and Knowledge traits.

Attribute Development (Per Character LVL)

Roll 1d10 nine times. Discard the two lowest results. Assign the remaining 7 results as points to Attribute Repository slots. Each Attribute may receive no more than 2 score per Character LVL (LVL). Cost: 2 REM Points per Attribute score, 1 REM Point per TST (Novice LVL). Unused REM Points become Remnants saved for future Levels. Attributes are first rolled at LVL 0 (character creation). Once all results are assigned and locked in, the Character advances to LVL 1.

Vitality Summary

Vitality Progression: Life, Mana, Stamina, and Endurance all grow every Character LVL and Growth, plus an aligned Trait bonus for Life/Mana/Stamina. All sizes use the same rates, size identity is reflected in base values. Apex is a race-specific Vitality score starting at a value of 1-11 determined by the character's race, increasing whichever Vitality resource(s) a character allocates it to (Player's choice, splittable across resources). Apex grows every 5 years of age after the first year at a rate specific to the character's race (every 5 Apex = 1 Chip of capacity). Spirit is a race-specific Vitality score starting at a value of 1-11 determined by the character's race, providing regeneration from whichever of Life, Mana, and Stamina a character allocates it to (Player's choice, splittable, every turn, always active). Spirit grows every 5 years of age after the first year at a rate specific to the character's race (every 5 Spirit = 1 Chip of regeneration per turn). Neither Apex nor Spirit are purchased with any currency; both are their own Vitality scores, not Traits, and grow automatically through age and Growth progression. Nature Abilities cost Stamina, Spell Abilities cost Mana, both scale with the LVL learned. Endurance base: 35 for all sizes, resets daily.

Base Vitality by Size: Goliath, Life 60, Mana 24, Stamina 40, Endurance 35. Standard, Life 50, Mana 32, Stamina 32, Endurance 35. Small, Life 40, Mana 40, Stamina 24, Endurance 35.

Thrown Damage Falloff

A throw does not fall off from the moment it leaves the hand, it needs room to travel. A thrown weapon reaches its hardest at 11-15 ft. and drops away past that, unlike a shot weapon, which is at its hardest the instant it is fired. Thrown attacks use Agility alone to hit, trading a melee weapon's Strength for it. Slope Mod† is what the ground itself is worth to the throw, High Ground where the thrower stands above their target, Low Ground where they stand below it. High Ground is the one exception to the last row, a thrower above their target still deals damage at 26 ft. and past, where level ground deals none; Low Ground carries a penalty in its place.

Sentimental Value

Applies to armaments (weapons and armor), accessories, charms, rings, amulets, cloaks, symbols (holy, unholy, or other class-specific implements), and any item readied or worn. Armament Penalty (Armor) and Weapon/Hand Proficiency (Weapons)

A character may use ANY class's Abilities, from any Class Tree they've spent Ability points into, with no Armament Penalty and the full Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus, so long as two things are true that day: the class's own default Armaments (its normal armor and weapon types) are part of that day's Armament Set, and they are physically able to carry what's equipped. Abilities already learned remain fully usable even if the character can no longer access that class's Path due to alignment or Fallen/Risen mechanics.

Armor and weapons are not treated the same way when mismatched. Armor is a matter of weight and mobility, something raw physical capability can overcome, wearing heavier than a tree allows costs a scaling Armament Penalty. A weapon is a matter of Culture instead, and no Trait overcomes it: a tree's Abilities require one of that tree's listed weapons to be readied, and a weapon it does not list, or one it names Forbidden, performs none of them. Wielding the tree's own stated weapon both satisfies that requirement and grants its Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus.

Armor Grade Threshold: Every class, Path, and Branch has a maximum Armor Grade its Abilities assume, stated in that tree's Armaments reminder (see Playable Classes, pg. 3, and Armor Grades & Materials, pg. 4, for the eleven Grades in ascending order: Delicate, Half Light, Light, Half Medium, Medium, Half Heavy, Heavy, Half Dense, Dense, Half Composite, Composite). Each Half Grade sits between a full Grade and the full Grade below it, a blend of the two, Half Light blends Light and Delicate, Half Medium blends Medium and Light, Half Heavy blends Heavy and Medium, Half Dense blends Dense and Heavy, Half Composite blends Composite and Dense. A character may wear their tree's maximum Grade, or any lighter Grade, with no Armament Penalty at all, weight and mobility below that ceiling are never a Prerequisite problem.

Armor Mismatch, Armament Penalty: When a character uses an Ability from a tree while wearing an Armor Grade heavier than that tree's maximum allows, a -5 Armament Penalty applies per Grade of difference, two Grades over the threshold applies -10, three Grades over applies -15, and so on. This penalty hits every roll made using that specific Ability, whether it's an attack roll, a Skill check, a crafting check, or any other action the Ability calls for, but only while that mismatched Ability is actually being used, never to unrelated actions. The penalty reduces by 1 per Grade of difference for every +5 a character has in whichever Trait most directly relates to overcoming that mismatch, typically Strength for encumbering armor. The GM determines the relevant Trait when it isn't obvious.

Example: A Monk's tree assumes Delicate Grade or lighter. A character with Heavy Grade (Plate) donned as their daily Armament is six Grades over that threshold (Delicate → Half Light → Light → Half Medium → Medium → Half Heavy → Heavy), taking a -30 Armament Penalty using any Monk Nature Ability. With +5 Strength, that penalty reduces to -24.

Protector Trees: A handful of Paths and Branches built around frontline defense raise their tree's maximum Grade all the way to Dense or Composite, the two heaviest Grades that exist (see Armor Grades & Materials, pg. 4), meaning a character following one of these can don ANY armor in the game with zero Armament Penalty. This is never free: Dense and Composite both carry the steepest movement penalties of any Grade, and every Ability written for a Protector Path or Branch is built to trade damage output for raw survivability, expect a Protector to hit softer and move slower than a peer who stayed in lighter armor. See each class's Tiers, Paths and Branches, pg. 3, for which ones carry this trait.

Weapon/Hand Proficiency: Every class, Path, and Branch has an ideal weapon profile stated in that tree's Armaments reminder (see Playable Classes, pg. 3), covering five parts: a Weapon Type (Tactical, Magic, TAW, or Energy, see Weapon Type DMG Mods, pg. 4), a Weapon Style (Slashing, Blunt, Piercing, or Chop, for melee weapons; Shoot and Throw weapons carry no Style, see below), a Hand (1h (Main), 1h (Off), or 2h), a Range (Touch, Melee, Throw, or Shoot, each with its own distance band, see below), and a Grade & Material (the weapon's Grade, see Weapon Grade & Materials, pg. 4, and, for some Paths or Branches, a Material Restriction that narrows the Weapon Style further, such as wood-only). A weapon must satisfy every part of a tree's profile to earn that tree's full Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus, matching the Weapon Type alone is not enough if the Style, Hand, Range, or Grade & Material don't also match. A weapon that satisfies the tree's Weapon Type and Style, without being Forbidden by its Culture, is enough to perform the tree's Abilities at all; the remaining parts govern only the bonus.

Range: Every weapon and Spell resolves at one of five Ranges: Touch (self or an adjacent target, no distance), Melee (adjacent, weapon's reach), Throw (a thrown weapon's short distance, e.g. javelin, hand-axe, sling stone), Area (Spell-only, a distance shorter than Shoot that resolves against a zone), or Shoot (a mechanically launched weapon's long distance, e.g. bow, crossbow, black powder weapon, or a Spell's longest single-target cast distance). Throw and Shoot are not interchangeable, a thrown weapon and a drawn/mechanically loosed weapon are different Ranges even when their real-world distances overlap. Area still carries any Style (Cone, Line, Burst, Cloud, Single Target, or Chain), the Style just plays out at Area's shorter distance, a Line Style at Area Range becomes several short rays fanning out rather than one long beam, a Cone Style at Area Range becomes a tighter, closer wedge rather than a long one.

Weapon Type vs. Weapon Style: A weapon's Type is Tactical (ordinary craft), Magic (Enchanted, or a caster's implement by nature), TAW (manufactured technology, splitting into TAW Ballistic and TAW Charged), or Energy (elemental substance held in the shape of a weapon, melee or ranged, neither cast nor built, and equally strong on every world, see Energy Weapons, pg. 4), the same Martial-or-Magic split used to flavor-tag Slots on pg. 3, plus the two culture-gated Types. A weapon's Style, where it has one, is Slashing, Blunt, Piercing, Chop, Flexible, Grab, or Chain, describing a melee weapon's shape, not its Type. The two combine: Tactical Blunt covers hammers, maces, clubs, and Staffs (Quarterstaff, Bō, Jō, Long Staff (2h), Short Staff (1h, dual-wieldable)); Magic Blunt covers Staves and wands, the dedicated caster implement, never a martial Staff, distinct in name specifically to avoid confusion between the two. The same split carries across the other Styles: a plain blade is Tactical Slashing, an enchanted blade is Magic Slashing; a plain dagger or spear is Tactical Piercing, an enchanted one is Magic Piercing; a plain axe or hatchet is Tactical Chop, an enchanted axe is Magic Chop; a plain rope or whip is Tactical Flexible, an enchanted one is Magic Flexible; a plain pair of tongs, pincers, claws, or hooks is Tactical Grab, an enchanted pair is Magic Grab. Unlike every other Style, Grab never rolls standard attack damage, its modifier applies to a Grapple or Grab check instead (see Grapple and Grab, Basic Rules pg. 5). Chain stands apart from the rest, it isn't forged into one fixed shape, a chain-whip is Flexible, a chain-sword is Slashing, and a chain-and-weight mounted on a pole is Blunt, each specific Chain weapon states which of the three it counts as on its own Card, using that row's DMG mod rather than a row of its own. Throw and Shoot weapons carry no Style at all, a javelin or hand-axe is Tactical (Range: Throw); a bow or black powder weapon is Tactical (Range: Shoot); a wand or orb channeled at range is Magic (Range: Shoot). Not every specific weapon crosses both Types, some are locked to one side by their nature, a two-handed hammer can only ever be Tactical, an orb can only ever be Magic, while others, like blades, can go either way depending on how they're made. For damage-modifier purposes (see Weapon Type DMG Mods, pg. 4), a Magic-Type weapon uses the Magic row regardless of Style, a Tactical-Type weapon uses its Style's own row instead. TAW and Energy stand apart from both Type values and from each other, both gated by culture and availability rather than training or magic (see Cultural Armaments & Rarity, below), but they are not the same thing: TAW is manufactured technology, a TAW blade or rifle built on an assembly line; Energy is elemental substance given a weapon's shape, a blade of living flame, a lightning whip, a coiled wind-blade, plasma, or raw force, never manufactured, mirroring the same TABA-versus-Energy split defined for armor above.

Spell Prerequisite Profile: Spells carry the same kind of profile weapons do, but built from five Spell-specific parts instead: a Cast (Verbal, a spoken incantation or chant; Somatic, a gesture or hand motion; Ritual, no Verbal or Somatic component required, but takes extended time to perform; Mind, no spoken, gestured, or ritual component at all, cast through will alone, covering ESP, psionics, and telekinesis; Reagents, cast by consuming a Reagent or Organic Element (Bone Dust, Bat Wings, Elderwood Ash, and the like, see Organic Elements, pg. 4) in place of Mana; Conjure, calls forth or animates a weapon, construct, or remains into being or motion, such as a conjured blade, spectral shield, or reanimated bones, that lingers and acts on its own each turn until dismissed or the encounter ends, not interruptible and not requiring further action from the caster, who is free to act or perform skill checks without losing it; Summon, calls a living or spirit creature to act with limited agency on the caster's behalf, such as a bound spirit-hawk or a hovering shrunken head, under the same non-interruptible terms as Conjure; or Orbital, an entity of various forms, shaped from raw magic rather than a weapon, creature, or remains, that hovers or floats around the caster to protect, attack, buff, or curse on its own, typically lasting for longer periods of time rather than resolving in a single instant, under the same non-interruptible terms as Conjure), a Mechanic (Instant, resolves immediately; DoT, damage ticking each round; HoT, healing ticking each round; Channel, held up turn after turn and interruptible if the caster is struck; or Drain, cast by spending Life, Stamina, or Endurance instead of Mana), a Style (Cone, a wedge extending from the caster; all other Styles, such as Line, Burst, Cloud, Single Target, and Chain, TBA), a School (the Spell's elemental or magical flavor, e.g. Fire, Ice, Arcane, Acid, Lightning, Necrotic, Holy, Psychic, Nature; a School can carry its own lingering sub-effect, such as Acid's Corrosion degrading equipment over time), and a Range (Touch, Shoot, or Area, see above; a Spell channeled through a melee or thrown weapon instead uses that weapon's Melee or Throw Range). A Spell's full profile is noted on its Spell Card.

Armor Type: Armor splits four ways, Tactical (ordinary craft, steel, leather, hide, bone), Magic (its protective quality comes from an inherent magical nature rather than craft alone), TABA (Technologically Advanced Body Armor, gated by culture and availability the same as TAW, see pg. 4), and Energy (elemental in nature, plasma, living flame, raw force rather than manufactured plating, gated the same way as TABA but never built by Omnè's engineering). TABA and Energy are not the same thing, TABA is manufactured technology, Energy is elemental substance given form, a plasma cuirass is Energy even if no factory anywhere produced it. Any Armor Grade can in principle be reached by any of the four Types, a Heavy Grade breastplate might be plain Tactical steel, TABA plating, elemental Energy given shape, or a rare Magic-forged piece, though some Structures lean toward one Type by nature, Force Fields are inherently TABA, Energy Shields are inherently Energy, and Mithril is inherently Magic, Type, Structure, and Grade (Half Light) are one and the same for it, it is never built as Tactical, TABA, or Energy.

Cultural Armaments & Rarity: TAW and Energy are not special cases, they're an example of a wider rule: every playable race has its own culture-tied Armaments, a Weapon Type, armor style, or Tactical or Magic technique unique to its people. Troll-make weaponry and armor and Ignis's Primal Armaments and Accessories (see pg. 4) are only two named examples among many, every race carries its own, whether written up yet or not. Each is rare outside that culture's reach. It becomes common only when at least one of two things is true: the campaign setting is within or near that culture's territory (an Omnè refugee camp counts, even off-world, same for a settlement on or near Ignis), or the Character themselves belongs to that culture, whether by race or by a Background raised among its people (see Backgrounds, pg. 2), a human raised by Omnè qualifies the same as an Omnè character would, same for one raised on Ignis. Outside both conditions, that culture's Armaments are rare to find, buy, or craft, though a GM may still roll to let a character have managed to acquire a piece anyway, at their discretion. Many interactive MAC creatures carry their own MAC-exclusive Armaments the same way, entire classes built around them, Grave Wardens (Undead) wield Deathrattle bone weaponry and wrap themselves in Shroud Plate cured from grave-cloth and bone; Core-Wrought Sentinels (Construct) integrate a Forged-Core weapon limb and Plated Chassis armor directly into their bodies rather than wearing or wielding either; Alpha Predators (Beast) fight with natural Feral Claw and Hidebound weapon and armor types no character can equip or remove. These, and many more, are detailed per Module and/or in Behemoths, Beasts & Bugs rather than here.

Required Weapons: Every Tier, Path, and Branch lists the weapons its Abilities are performed with. One of them must be readied to use any Ability from that tree. This is not a penalty scale and no Trait softens it: a weapon the tree does not list, or one its Culture names Forbidden, simply performs none of that tree's Abilities. A Character may still carry and fight with whatever they like, Basic Attacks are never restricted by class, Path, or Branch, and any weapon a Character can wield performs one normally (see Basic Attacks, Basic Rules pg. 5). The restriction exists for Culture rather than capability: an Avenger is perfectly able to swing a TAW mace, their faith simply does not channel through manufactured tech, so no Avenger Ability comes out of it.

Weapon Match, Weapon/Hand Proficiency: Satisfying a tree's requirement and earning its bonus are two different bars. Wielding the weapon that satisfies a tree's full profile grants that tree's stated Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus, typically a flat bonus to Hit. Where a Path or Branch calls out a specific alternate weapon of its own, wielding that one instead grants a smaller version of the same bonus. This bonus is separate from the Armament Penalty above, and unlike armor, no amount of Strength, Agility, or any other Trait changes either number, they're fixed by what's in hand.

Example: An Avenger's Abilities assume a Blunt, 1h or 2h, Melee weapon, wielding one grants the Avenger's Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus. An Avenger wielding a dagger instead still uses every Avenger Ability normally, they simply don't earn that bonus until a Blunt weapon is back in the day's Armament Set. A Sorcerer channels through a Magic, 1h or 2h, Melee-range Stave for the same bonus; wielding a different melee weapon instead still casts every Sorcerer Spell normally, just without it.

Example, Material Restriction: A Monk's tree calls for a Blunt weapon made of wood, 1h, 2h, or bare hands, Melee range, to earn its Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus. A hammer or mace is Blunt and Melee, but its metal head fails the wood-only Material Restriction, so it doesn't earn the bonus, though it still activates every Monk Nature Ability normally. A quarterstaff or nunchaku, being both Blunt and wood, satisfies the full profile and earns the bonus.

Using a class's Abilities while equipped with that SAME class's own default Armaments for the day carries no Armament Penalty and earns that tree's full Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus, no matter how another class's Armaments compare.

Enchantment Creates a Magic Weapon: A weapon's Magic Type is not inherent in every case, most weapons start as Tactical, and it's a successful Enchantment (see Enchanting, pg. 4) that converts a Tactical weapon into a Magic one, this is what defines a magic weapon in these rules, not a glowing effect or a magical-sounding name, but having actually undergone Enchantment. A handful of dedicated caster implements, a Stave, wand, orb, or tome, are Magic by their own nature instead (see Weapon Type vs. Weapon Style, above) and never need Enchanting to qualify, the same way an orb can never be Tactical no matter what's done to it.

Magic Weapons, Martial, and Spell: A Magic-Type weapon earns both a Martial Ability's and a Spell Ability's Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus at once, so long as its Style, Hand, Range, and Grade & Material still match the tree's ideal profile, while a Tactical-Type weapon only earns it for a Martial Ability. This means a class like Barbarian, Ranger, Warrior, or Rogue, whose tree's Weapon/Hand Proficiency profile stays weapon-based rather than Stave-or-orb-based across Tier, Path, and Branch, still performs its Martial Abilities with the plain Tactical version of that profile, the same as always, but casting one of its Spell Abilities at full bonus calls for either an Enchanted (Magic-Type) version of that same weapon, or the tree's specific default casting implement where one is stated instead (a Stave, orb, tome, wand, or similar), if that tree defines one as its true baseline focus, either way the Spell itself still casts without one. For example, a Barbarian's 2h axe, once successfully Enchanted (see Enchantment Creates a Magic Weapon, above), becomes a Magic-Type 2h axe that earns the Barbarian's Spell Weapon/Hand Proficiency bonus the same way a caster's Stave or orb would, no separate implement needed, so long as its Style, Hand, Range, and Grade & Material still match the Barbarian's own ideal profile.

Example, Martial In Hand, Casting Without One: A Salii Priest can strike with a plain dagger as a Martial Ability normally, no Mana spent and no Enchantment needed, that half of the tree never touches either. To cast one of Salii's Spell Abilities through that same dagger instead, the dagger must first be Enchanted into a Magic-Type dagger, plain Tactical steel doesn't channel a Spell no matter how it's swung. An Avenger Cleric works the same way with a hammer: swinging it as a Martial Ability costs no Mana and needs no Enchantment, wielded empty-handed of magic entirely if that's all the Cleric has on them, but casting a Spell Ability through that hammer calls for both at once, enough Mana in the tank to pay the Spell's cost, and a Magic-Type hammer to actually channel it, a Tactical hammer and an empty Mana pool each block it on their own.

Standing Categories

Standing is held across seven categories, each with its own Standing and its own Contributions, tracked apart: Family (the Character's kin), Affiliations (formal faction membership), Companions (Pets, Guardians, mounts, Fellowships, recorded as Mood), Campaign (people partied with), Fellows (the friends list), Neighbours (those sharing or bordering the Character's coordinates), and Wellness (the Character's Standing with themselves, their self-directed Contributions). See Standing under Prestige for full descriptions.

Contribution, Neglect & the Mark

Each Standing category carries two lines: Standing (Exalted through Hated, how the party regards the Character) and Contribution (what the Character puts in, with Neglect as its downward direction, ticking on its own like Wellness). Recorded apart. Only the Standing line carries a Mark, so each category holds three numbers: Standing now, the Mark, and Contribution now.

Neglect floor: Neglect can only carry a Character toward Neutral, never past it. Falling below Neutral takes a deliberate act.

The Mark: the highest Standing ever held with a party, recorded alongside current Standing. Never decays. It distinguishes drifting apart from betrayal: two parties at Neutral read differently if one's Mark is Exalted and the other's is Neutral.

Betrayal scales with the Mark: a deliberate act drops a party as far below Neutral as their Mark stood above it. Mark Adored (+2) lands at Disliked (−2); Mark Exalted (+4) lands at Hated. Minimum one tier below Neutral regardless of Mark.

Sealing: a Mark is never erased, but a Bond Quest may seal it. The record stands; the GM stops applying it. See Contribution, Neglect & the Mark under Prestige, pg. 2.

Praise & Blowback

Praise is what a Character receives from the friends and allies of those they hold good Standing with. Blowback is what they receive from the enemies of those same parties. Both arrive unasked. Rising with one faction is by itself a way of falling with its rivals, so there is no neutral gain in Standing. A mission statement worked towards draws Praise from those who hope to see it done and Blowback from those who do not; a mission statement neglected reverses both. The ripple: when Standing with a target moves, allies of that target move the same direction by half the tiers moved, rounded down, minimum 1; enemies move the opposite direction by the same amount; parties marked neutral do not move. See Praise & Blowback under Prestige, pg. 2.

NPC Disposition

NPCs interact based on their Standing relative to the Character, their race, and their faction. Some Standings are set by default (racial defaults), and shift through Player actions and story events. Standing runs: Exalted, Cherished, Adored, Respected, Neutral, Tolerable, Disliked, Hated. Exalted is reached only through a Bond Quest, which becomes available at Cherished. NPC Disposition is tracked on NPC Encounter Cards. See Standing Tiers under Prestige for full tier descriptions.

Class Trainer Requirements & Fees

A Class Trainer needs to teach the Character's Main Class, not the exact Tier, Path, or Branch, any Trainer who teaches that Main Class can teach any Tier, Path, or Branch within it (see Playable Classes, pg. 3). Paths and Branches are Sub-Classes of their Main Class, not separate trees of their own, freely chosen at LVL 4 and LVL 21 respectively. Separately, certain Slots carry a Prerequisite: every 4th LVL starting at LVL 3, one Slot, cycling A, B, C, is a Prerequisite for that same Slot and Split at the very next LVL (see Slots and Prerequisites, pg. 3). A Trainer must be at least the Character's own current LVL, this holds whether the pick being learned came from reaching that LVL outright or from the midpoint toward the next one, the requirement tracks the Character's LVL, not which Slot the pick filled. Reaching a LVL, or the midpoint toward the next one, that grants a pick only makes it available, the pick still isn't learned until the Character finds a qualifying Trainer, in sufficient faction standing, and pays the fee.

Slot RangeSuggested Fee (Gold)
Tier (1-3)5 × Slot Number
Path (4-20)10 × Slot Number
Branch (21-30)20 × Slot Number

Example: A character's 13th pick, a Path Slot, costs a suggested 130 Gold (10 × 13), learned from any Trainer of that Main Class who is at least the Character's own current LVL. A character's 27th pick, a Branch Slot, costs a suggested 540 Gold (20 × 27), under the same Trainer requirement.

These are suggested baseline fees, not fixed prices. Each Module sets its own actual Class Trainer fees and Trainer availability, the same way Planetary Markets pricing varies by world and region (see pg. 4). A GM may adjust a given Trainer's fee upward for a rare specialist, or downward for a common local one.

Fallen & Risen

Scope: Only Paths bound to a Higher Power can Fall or Rise; Ambivalent Paths cannot (they have no patron to betray or appease).

Which Paths Can Fall or Rise: Lawful and Moral Paths may Fall. Amoral and Evil Paths may Rise. (Alignment is set at character creation and Path choice; only Higher Power devotion can change, not alignment itself.)

Fallen Mechanic: When a Lawful or Moral Path's character neglects devotion (missing daily Endurance actions or weekly ceremonies for extended periods), the Higher Power tests for Fallen: the character loses all Higher Power bonuses tied to that Path and its Abilities, and begins taking penalties and random tests from the wronged patron. The character keeps every Ability already learned from that Path but can no longer learn new Abilities from it until the bond is restored. Neglect continues until the debt is repaid through renewed devotion and ceremony (see Recovery and Redemption, below).

Risen Mechanic: When an Amoral or Evil Path's character betrays their patron's core values (refusing services, breaking oaths, or working against the patron's interests for extended periods), the Higher Power tests for Risen: the character loses all Higher Power bonuses tied to that Path and its Abilities, and begins taking penalties and wrath from the betrayed patron. The character keeps every Ability already learned from that Path but can no longer learn new Abilities from it until the bond is restored. Betrayal continues until the debt is repaid through service to the patron or finding a new Higher Power (see Recovery and Redemption, below).

Recovery and Redemption: A broken bond may be restored by returning to full devotion (Lawful/Moral) or renewed service (Amoral/Evil) until the patron deems the debt repaid, typically after 4-6 weeks of resumed ceremony and service. The Higher Power may test the character's sincerity. Once restored, the character regains all bonuses and may once more learn new Abilities from that Path. Alternatively, a character may petition a different Higher Power at LVL 5 or beyond (abandoning the broken Path's patron entirely), but this choice is permanent and irrevocable—the old patron's wrath remains unless the new patron offers reparations or the debt is paid in full.
