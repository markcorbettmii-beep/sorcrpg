Avenger (Playable in Starter Set)

Faith-driven warriors bound to a higher power, at first restricted to blunt weapons and a light armor default threshold. As with all Paths and Branches, later Avengers unlock some 1h bladed weapons and Medium or Half Medium Armor.

Avengers practice unwavering judgment showcasing an array of protection, healing, martial combative skills, hand to hand combat and mythical chants guided through their chosen deity or spiritual force, whether that devotion is righteous and public or quieter and more self-interested. Where others seek to end a fight quickly, an Avenger is built to outlast it, wearing down whoever stands against them through unshakable faith and sheer endurance until judgment finally comes due.

Passive Abilities:
These passive Abilities belong to the Main Class alone, granted once and active regardless of Tier, Path, or Branch; they are not re-granted, replaced, or duplicated at any Path or Branch.

Avenger Blessing, lasts 6 hours (must be recast every 6 hours to stay active). No mana cost for Main Class Abilities.

Zealous Reckoning, Once Per Day the Avenger calls down judgment, dealing holy damage equal to twice their Piety Score to one target and healing themselves for half that amount.

Extra 1d6 + lvl radiant damage on Critical Hits.
Once per encounter, Avengers can Flourish their blunt weapon to grant an ally a free Defense roll.

Avengers get +5 feet their base movement speed, once per encounter.

Avengers get +4 plus their PIETY Score on Knowledge checks involving religious lore.

Avenger Paths and Branches
Each Path and Branch has full descriptions in their Ability section.
i. Cleric - The frontline face of the faith, standing in the shield-line and striking with holy conviction alongside their allies.
  a. Holy - The Cleric's default and most balanced expression, a steady shield-and-mace defender.
  b. Oracle - A farther-reaching caster who trades armor weight for spell range, wielding only wood or glass blunt weapons to keep their channeling pure.
  c. Warpriest - A dual-wielding Cleric who pairs a blunt main-hand weapon with any offhand blunt weapon, eventually earning a magic slashing weapon in the main hand.
ii. Monk - A disciplined, often unarmed practitioner of the faith who channels devotion through the body itself rather than steel.
  a. Chanter - A Monk who channels devotion through music, using a light instrument as a magical focus for their chants.
  b. Qirateka - A tankier Monk branch that uses medium armor and bracers to parry blows, drawing enemy attention away from allies with taunts.
  c. Shapeflow - The most mobile Monk branch, forgoing all but the lightest armor to strike with precise, piercing unarmed blows.
iii. Priest - A quieter servant of the faith who deals in ritual, shadow, and blood-price rather than open combat.
  a. Discipline - A defensively-minded Priest who fights with a blunt weapon and shield, or dual blunt weapons, in a disciplined guarding stance.
  b. Salii - The Priest branch that finally unlocks true enchanted daggers and heavier armor.
  c. Shadow Weaver - A stealth-focused Priest who favors concealed blades and shadow over open ritual combat.

Materials Forbidden (Culture, Cleric):
◆None. The Cleric Culture judges a weapon by its shape and its making, not its substance.
Armor Forbidden (Culture, Cleric):
◆None by Culture; the Armor Grade threshold below still applies. Cleric orders have always sent their own into the shield-line, and armor of any make is thought no barrier between a Cleric and their Higher Power.
Cleric doctrine forbids the shedding of blood by an edge. Its Abilities are performed with weapons that break rather than cut, which is why the mace, hammer and shield-boss carry the tradition and the sword never has. Bladed and Chopping weapons perform no Cleric Ability, though a Cleric may of course still swing one as a Basic Attack.

Branch Exceptions (Culture, Cleric):
This Culture carries through to every Cleric Branch. Each Branch below states only where its own practice departs from it; anything unlisted stays as set above.
◆Holy - Permits: a Piercing weapon, the blessed spear, and Heavy Grade (Sanctified Plate). A spear consecrated at the altar is held to strike as a rod rather than a blade, the one edge-adjacent arm the doctrine has ever admitted.
◆Oracle - Refuses: all metal, in weapon and armor alike. Oracles read what is to come through untouched substance, and hold worked metal to cloud it. Wood and glass only, Light Grade (Ivory-threaded Cloth) or lighter.
◆Warpriest - Permits: a 1h (Main) Slashing weapon where it is Magic, and Medium Grade (Battle Chain). An enchanted edge is reckoned an instrument of the power that blessed it rather than a blade of war, and so falls outside the prohibition.

The Monk Culture holds that what is carried shapes what is practiced. Metal is worked for war and carries that purpose into the hand; the parts of a beast are taken by harm and carry that. A Monk trains with what grew and was cut without violence, or with nothing at all, which is why the staff and the open hand carry this tradition. The restriction is on Monk Abilities only, never on what a Monk may pick up and swing.

Branch Exceptions (Culture, Monk):
This Culture carries through to every Monk Branch. Each Branch below states only where its own practice departs from it; anything unlisted stays as set above.
◆Chanter - Permits: a chant instrument of any grown material, gourd, cane, reed or string, and Light Grade (Songweave Cloth). The voice is the instrument proper; what carries it is held to be furniture rather than arm.
◆Qirateka - Permits: worked metal in light bladed hand weapons such as tonfa or sai, Medium Grade (Banded Chain), and Medium Grade bracers for Parry. Qirateka answered the question of who stands in front by deciding a body between the enemy and the weak is worth the compromise the rest of the tradition will not make.
◆Shapeflow - Refuses: all bracers and every rigid piece, beyond even the Path's own bar. Delicate Grade (Featherwrap Silk) and hand wraps only, since anything stiffer is held to interrupt the motion the whole practice is built on.
