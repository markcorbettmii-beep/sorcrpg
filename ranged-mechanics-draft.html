How to read this page.

The table. Find your weapon, then your distance. The Velocity Curve column names the state your shot is in. The three columns after it are the consequences: Hit MOD. is added to your d100 roll, DMG. MOD. is the Trait Score added to damage, and Base DMG. is how many of the weapon's dice survive the flight.

Dice fractions. Base DMG. is written as a share of the weapon's listed dice. On a Rare 4d6 weapon: Max DMG. (+¼ dice) is 5d6, Base DMG. (full dice) is 4d6, Decay (¾ dice) is 3d6 and Decay (½ dice) is 2d6. Agility is added after the dice are set, in full, at every band.

Diamonds. ✦ and ✧ mark the two footnotes directly under the table. ✦ is Point Blank, ✧ is Velocity Drop.

Charts. Every combat chart this page draws on is reproduced at the bottom, under Charts. Each carries its own anchor so it can be linked to by name.

Anchors. Every heading, footnote and chart on this page has an id. Link to one by adding #id to the page address, for example #earthborn-ranged or #crit-chart.

SORC Ranged Mechanics

Ranged Attacks

Ranged combat covers any attack delivered from a distance using projectiles or spells. All ranged attacks use the d100 roll-over system where your relevant Trait SCR (Agility for ranged weapons, Acuity for spells, Strength for Melee, etc) is added directly to your hit roll against a target's Protection Score (PROTS).

Ranged attacks fall into four primary categories based on their origin: Earthborn (bows, crossbows, thrown weapons, and black powder firearms), Technologically Advanced Weapons (TAW) (manufactured technology, splitting into TAW Ballistic and TAW Charged), Energy (elemental substance carried around a real primal weapon, neither cast nor built), and Spell (magical effects cast, in this case from a distance). Each type uses different range bands, damage calculations, and armor interactions.

Earthborn - Ranged

Earthborn projectiles operate under physics-based range mechanics where distance, projectile velocity drops, and stability directly affect accuracy and damage. Distance is measured in feet. (Given: 1 sq. = 5 ft). Every ranged shot, whether arrow, bolt, hand thrown or slung, falls into one of four velocity states based on how far it travels: Point Blank (0–5 ft.), Oscillation, Stabilized, and Velocity Drop (gravity and air resistance, out to the weapon's maximum range). Band distances past Point Blank vary by weapon class and are read off the table below.

At Point Blank range (0–5 ft.), if the attacker's d100 roll is a Critical Hit (96-100), it lands automatically; and a defensive roll from the target, called a Botch (grapple), is waived off. The Crit deals 2x damage dice + Agility Score of the attacker (see Critical Hit Formula, Combat, Hit Results, pg. 5). If the attacker's hit roll is a non-critical hit that meets or exceeds the defender's Protection Score (PROTS), the defender may attempt a Point Blank Botch (grapple) to avoid or reduce damage. The defender rolls d100 and adds the higher of their Agility Score or Strength Score, plus their Defense Trait Score, stacked with their PROTS. If this roll meets or exceeds the attacker's total roll (including modifiers), the defender successfully botches the attack. In this case, the target only sustains partial damage (how much is pending).

Note: Innate Aptitude and Inaptitude apply to this roll; for example, a Gnome with an innate -5 Strength (STR) Inaptitude would add their Agility Score instead of Strength when calculating their defense roll. If the defender's roll fails to meet the attacker's roll, the shot lands uncontested and deals full damage.

Compact Earthborn weapons (shortbows, light crossbows, slings, hand cannons, shotguns, blunderbusses, dart guns) reach a maximum of 75 ft. Extended weapons (longbows, heavy crossbows, war cannons, siege mortars, ballistae, harpoon guns) reach a maximum of 150 ft. Individual weapon cards may override these defaults. Rifles and longer ranged Earthborn weapons distances are pending.

Black Powder: Earthborn covers black powder firearms as well as drawn and thrown weapons. Hand cannons, blunderbusses, muskets and pistols burn black powder to drive a lead ball, slug or shot, and that charge is what separates them from Technologically Advanced Weapons, which never use black powder. A black powder firearm reads off the same table as every other Earthborn projectile, taking the same velocity states, band distances, and hit and damage columns as a bow or crossbow of its reach.

✦Point Blank. A d100 roll of 96-100 is a Critical Hit and lands automatically; the defender gets no roll against it. Any other hit that meets or exceeds the defender's PROTS is a normal hit, and a normal hit at this range opens the defender to a Point Blank Botch (grapple).

✧Velocity Drop. The band runs to the weapon's maximum range, 75 ft. for Compact weapons and 150 ft. for Extended weapons. Damage decays across the band at the rate shown in the DMG. MOD. column, and nothing may be fired past the maximum.

The Velocity Curve column tracks projectile physics and identifies the physical sweet spot where ammunition reaches peak velocity and damage potential.

Abbreviations: "Melee" is a Close Quarters strike, not a shot, and uses Strength. "Pt. Blank" is the shaft at full launch energy but still unsettled, devastating at contact and the only band where a Botch (grapple) may be attempted. "Oscillation" is the shaft still flexing out of the archer's paradox, flying at Base DMG. rather than a peak. "Stabilized" is the second peak, where the shaft has finished settling and flies true. "Velocity Drop" shows the projectile losing energy and accuracy as gravity and air resistance accumulate, reading "Less DMG." on a shortbow and "Decay" on a longbow. This column directly informs tactical positioning: engage at the Stabilized band, 26–45 ft. with a shortbow and 46–100 ft. with a longbow, for the highest damage output at range.

Thrown weapons use the Throw band, a 0–25 ft. range, and Agility alone applies to hit and defense. A throw is at its hardest inside Point Blank and flies at Base DMG. through the rest of its range; it reaches no Stabilized band and takes no Velocity Drop within 25 ft.

Improvised Projectile: A projectile driven by hand rather than launched, an arrow punched into a target or a bolt used as a spike, is a Close Quarters attack and not a ranged attack. It uses Strength for both hit and damage, Base + STR, and follows standard melee rules. Velocity state, the Velocity Curve column and Agility Score play no part in it. The projectile's Grade sets its base damage the same as it would when launched, see Projectiles below.

Projectile Recovery: Earthborn projectiles may be recovered after combat. Typically, 50% of arrows or bolts fired land recoverable. Recovered projectiles can be salvaged for crafting materials or sold. Ballistic projectiles (slugs, pellets, stones) may also be recovered and used for materials. Technologically Advanced Weapons (TAW) and Energy projectiles cannot be recovered; they are expended on use.

Weapon Families. Earthborn, Technologically Advanced Weapons (TAW) and Energy are the three power sources a ranged weapon can draw on, and the distinction governs range bands, armor interaction and planetary penalties alike. Earthborn is forged or knapped and driven by muscle or mechanism. TAW is technologically manufactured and tuned to the sun of its native planet, losing range off-world. Energy draws on the elemental energy of the universe rather than either sun, which is why it alone takes no planetary Bonus or Penalty. All three are defined under Tech and Magic Across Essentia (Planetary Markets, Basic Rules pg. 4).

Reach weapons do not use range bands; they simply extend melee reach to 10 ft. and follow standard melee rules.

Earthborn Projectiles

Earthborn is by far the most diverse projectile family, spanning four material tiers. A projectile's Grade is the material of the part that strikes, the head, tip or point, not the shaft, so an ordinary wooden shaft under an Adamantium head is an Adamantium projectile.

  • Knapped and carved: Wooden, Bone, Stone, Obsidian, the oldest projectiles on any world and the only ones every people can make.
  • The forged standard: Steel/Iron, Bronze, Hardened Steel, Chromium, what most bundles are built from.
  • Light metals: Silver, Mithril, Refined Steel, worked for the cut rather than the weight.
  • Dense and legendary: Tungsten, Osmium, Iridium, Platinum, Ruthenium, Titanium, Vibranium, Adamantium, Dragon Scale, heads that punish the drawing arm as much as the target.

Slope Mod belongs to whatever launches the projectile, the arm or the bow, and is read off the launcher's own rules (see Slope Conditions below). Full Grades, damage, bundle sizes and costs are on each Projectile's Card (see Projectile Grade, pg. 4).

TAW Ranged

Technologically Advanced Weapons (TAW) are rail guns, mini guns, high velocity sniper rifles, laser pistols, plasma pistols and rifles, plasma grenades that stick, and other technologically advanced weaponry originally manufactured on the planets further out from Adoria. TAW splits into two: TAW Ballistic, which fires a physical round, and TAW Charged, which fires light or plasma. A laser pistol or a plasma cannon is TAW Charged, not Energy, because it was built (see Energy Weapons, pg. 4).

TAW also fields shotguns, and they fire more than plasma. A TAW shotgun will throw minerals much the way lead is thrown from an Earthborn gun, but it never burns black powder to do it. The propellant for any TAW round that is not light or plasma is one of two systems: nuclear pulse propulsion or an electrothermal-chemical (ETC) charge.
